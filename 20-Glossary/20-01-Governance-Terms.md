# Chapter 20 – Glossary

## 20.1 Governance Terms

This section defines the core **governance terminology** used throughout the GRC Knowledge Base. These terms establish a common vocabulary for understanding how organizations direct, oversee, control, and improve their activities in alignment with business objectives, regulatory obligations, risk appetite, and stakeholder expectations.

| Term                                  | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Accountability**                    | The obligation of an individual or organizational unit to take responsibility for decisions, actions, outcomes, and compliance with established requirements.                                               |
| **Authority**                         | The formally granted power to make decisions, approve actions, allocate resources, or direct organizational activities.                                                                                     |
| **Board Governance**                  | The system through which the board of directors provides oversight, strategic direction, accountability, and supervision of organizational performance and risk.                                            |
| **Business Governance**               | The structures, policies, decision rights, and oversight mechanisms used to direct and control business activities toward organizational objectives.                                                        |
| **Committee**                         | A formally established group responsible for reviewing, overseeing, advising on, or making decisions within a defined area of responsibility.                                                               |
| **Compliance Governance**             | The governance mechanisms used to ensure that the organization identifies, implements, monitors, and reports compliance with applicable laws, regulations, standards, contracts, and internal requirements. |
| **Corporate Governance**              | The system of principles, structures, processes, and relationships through which an organization is directed and controlled.                                                                                |
| **Decision Rights**                   | Clearly defined authority specifying who has the power to make particular decisions within an organization.                                                                                                 |
| **Delegation of Authority**           | The formal assignment of decision-making or operational authority from one organizational level or role to another while retaining appropriate accountability and oversight.                                |
| **Directive**                         | A formal instruction issued by an authorized person or body requiring specific actions or behaviors.                                                                                                        |
| **Governance Framework**              | A structured set of principles, policies, roles, responsibilities, processes, and mechanisms used to direct and oversee an organization or specific domain.                                                 |
| **Governance Model**                  | The defined structure describing how authority, accountability, decision-making, oversight, and reporting are organized within an organization or function.                                                 |
| **Governance Structure**              | The organizational arrangement of boards, committees, management, functions, and reporting relationships responsible for governance activities.                                                             |
| **Governance Body**                   | A board, committee, executive group, or other formally authorized body responsible for governance, oversight, or decision-making.                                                                           |
| **Governance Charter**                | A formal document defining the purpose, authority, responsibilities, membership, decision rights, and operating procedures of a governance body.                                                            |
| **Governance Objective**              | A desired outcome established to ensure that governance activities support organizational strategy, risk management, compliance, and stakeholder expectations.                                              |
| **Governance Policy**                 | A formally approved statement establishing mandatory principles, expectations, or requirements for organizational governance.                                                                               |
| **Governance Process**                | A defined sequence of activities used to perform governance functions such as decision-making, oversight, monitoring, escalation, and reporting.                                                            |
| **Governance Oversight**              | The supervision and review performed by authorized governance bodies to ensure that management and organizational activities remain aligned with objectives and requirements.                               |
| **Management**                        | The individuals or functions responsible for planning, directing, executing, and controlling organizational activities.                                                                                     |
| **Management Accountability**         | The responsibility of management to ensure that organizational activities, resources, risks, controls, and performance are appropriately managed.                                                           |
| **Mandate**                           | A formally established requirement, authority, or responsibility assigned to an individual, function, committee, or organization.                                                                           |
| **Oversight**                         | The process of supervising, reviewing, challenging, and monitoring activities to ensure that objectives and requirements are being achieved.                                                                |
| **Policy**                            | A formally approved statement of management intent, principles, or mandatory requirements that guides organizational decisions and behavior.                                                                |
| **Principle**                         | A fundamental rule, value, or proposition that provides guidance for organizational decision-making and behavior.                                                                                           |
| **Reporting Line**                    | The formal relationship defining to whom an individual, function, or organizational unit reports.                                                                                                           |
| **Responsibility**                    | The obligation to perform assigned tasks, activities, or functions. Responsibility may be delegated, while accountability generally remains with the accountable owner.                                     |
| **Role**                              | A defined position or set of functions assigned to an individual or organizational unit.                                                                                                                    |
| **Steering Committee**                | A governance body established to provide strategic direction, oversight, prioritization, and decision-making for a program, project, or organizational initiative.                                          |
| **Strategic Alignment**               | The degree to which organizational activities, investments, risks, controls, and decisions support established business strategy and objectives.                                                            |
| **Three Lines Model**                 | A governance model that distinguishes responsibilities among operational management, risk and compliance functions, and internal audit to support effective risk management and assurance.                  |
| **Transparency**                      | The degree to which relevant decisions, information, responsibilities, processes, and performance are made visible and understandable to appropriate stakeholders.                                          |
| **Trustee**                           | An individual or body entrusted with responsibility for protecting and managing assets, interests, information, or responsibilities on behalf of another party.                                             |
| **Executive Management**              | Senior organizational leaders responsible for executing strategy, managing operations, allocating resources, and overseeing organizational performance and risk.                                            |
| **Stakeholder**                       | An individual, group, organization, regulator, customer, employee, partner, or other party that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by organizational decisions or activities.    |
| **Stakeholder Governance**            | Governance practices that consider and appropriately address the interests, expectations, rights, and obligations of relevant stakeholders.                                                                 |
| **Governance Effectiveness**          | The degree to which governance structures, processes, decisions, and oversight mechanisms achieve their intended objectives.                                                                                |
| **Governance Maturity**               | The level of development, consistency, effectiveness, integration, and continuous improvement of an organization's governance capabilities.                                                                 |
| **Escalation**                        | The formal process of raising an issue, risk, decision, exception, or noncompliance matter to a higher level of authority when it exceeds established thresholds or decision rights.                        |
| **Exception**                         | A formally approved deviation from an established policy, standard, control, requirement, or process.                                                                                                       |
| **Exception Management**              | The process of identifying, evaluating, approving, documenting, monitoring, and closing authorized deviations from established requirements.                                                                |
| **Quorum**                            | The minimum number of authorized members required for a governance body to conduct valid business or make formal decisions.                                                                                 |
| **Resolution**                        | A formally documented decision or determination made by an authorized governance body.                                                                                                                      |
| **Minutes**                           | The official record of discussions, decisions, actions, attendees, and significant matters addressed during a governance meeting.                                                                           |
| **Decision Log**                      | A controlled record documenting significant decisions, their rationale, decision-makers, dates, and related actions.                                                                                        |
| **Governance Calendar**               | A structured schedule identifying recurring governance activities, meetings, reviews, reporting requirements, assessments, and approval cycles.                                                             |
| **Management Review**                 | A formal evaluation by management of the continuing suitability, adequacy, effectiveness, and performance of a management system or governance process.                                                     |
| **Assurance**                         | An objective or independent evaluation that provides confidence to stakeholders that governance, risk management, and control processes are appropriately designed and operating effectively.               |
| **Independent Assurance**             | Assurance performed by a function sufficiently independent from the activities being assessed to provide objective conclusions.                                                                             |
| **Internal Audit**                    | An independent and objective assurance and advisory function designed to evaluate and improve governance, risk management, and control processes.                                                           |
| **Governance Accountability Matrix**  | A structured representation of governance roles and decision responsibilities, often using models such as RACI to clarify accountability and participation.                                                 |
| **RACI**                              | A responsibility assignment model identifying who is **Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed** for a particular activity or decision.                                                           |
| **Control Ownership**                 | The formal assignment of responsibility and accountability for ensuring that a control is appropriately designed, implemented, maintained, and monitored.                                                   |
| **Risk Ownership**                    | The assignment of accountability to an individual or organizational unit for managing a specific risk within defined risk appetite and tolerance boundaries.                                                |
| **Policy Owner**                      | The person or function accountable for developing, maintaining, reviewing, and ensuring the continued relevance of a policy.                                                                                |
| **Process Owner**                     | The individual accountable for the performance, effectiveness, documentation, and continual improvement of a defined business or governance process.                                                        |
| **Governance Owner**                  | The person or organizational function accountable for the effectiveness of a governance domain, framework, or governance process.                                                                           |
| **Approval Authority**                | The individual or governance body authorized to formally approve a policy, risk acceptance, exception, investment, decision, or other controlled activity.                                                  |
| **Risk Appetite Authority**           | The governance authority empowered to establish, approve, or modify the organization's risk appetite.                                                                                                       |
| **Risk Acceptance Authority**         | The person or governance body authorized to formally accept a defined level of risk when the risk cannot or will not be fully mitigated.                                                                    |
| **Challenge Function**                | An independent or appropriately separated capability responsible for questioning assumptions, decisions, risk assessments, controls, and management actions.                                                |
| **Governance Reporting**              | The structured communication of governance information, including risks, compliance status, performance, decisions, exceptions, issues, and required actions to authorized stakeholders.                    |
| **Governance Dashboard**              | A consolidated visual representation of key governance indicators, risks, compliance status, controls, issues, actions, and performance measures.                                                           |
| **Key Governance Indicator (KGI)**    | A metric used to evaluate the effectiveness, performance, or health of governance activities.                                                                                                               |
| **Governance Breach**                 | A failure to comply with an established governance requirement, decision, authority boundary, policy, or governance process.                                                                                |
| **Governance Failure**                | A significant breakdown in governance structures, responsibilities, decision-making, oversight, or accountability that prevents effective organizational direction or control.                              |
| **Governance Risk**                   | The risk that ineffective governance structures, decision-making, accountability, oversight, or leadership may prevent an organization from achieving its objectives or meeting its obligations.            |
| **Good Governance**                   | Governance characterized by appropriate accountability, transparency, effectiveness, ethical behavior, responsible decision-making, stakeholder consideration, and appropriate oversight.                   |
| **Ethical Governance**                | Governance that incorporates ethical principles, integrity, fairness, responsible conduct, and consideration of stakeholder impacts into organizational decision-making.                                    |
| **Governance Framework Integration**  | The alignment and coordinated operation of multiple governance frameworks, standards, policies, and processes to reduce duplication and improve organizational effectiveness.                               |
| **Governance Lifecycle**              | The recurring sequence of governance activities including establishment, planning, approval, implementation, monitoring, review, reporting, improvement, and retirement.                                    |
| **Governance Assurance**              | The systematic provision of confidence that governance arrangements are appropriately designed, implemented, monitored, and operating effectively.                                                          |
| **Governance Review**                 | A formal evaluation of governance structures, processes, responsibilities, performance, and effectiveness against defined expectations or requirements.                                                     |
| **Governance Improvement**            | The systematic enhancement of governance structures, processes, capabilities, controls, and decision-making practices.                                                                                      |
| **Continuous Governance Improvement** | The ongoing identification and implementation of improvements to governance practices based on performance, risks, incidents, audits, regulatory changes, lessons learned, and stakeholder feedback.        |

### Key Governance Distinctions

Several terms in governance are closely related but should not be treated as interchangeable:

* **Accountability vs. Responsibility:** Responsibility concerns performing an activity; accountability concerns being answerable for its outcome.
* **Governance vs. Management:** Governance provides direction, oversight, accountability, and strategic control; management executes strategy and manages operations.
* **Authority vs. Accountability:** Authority provides the power to make or approve decisions; accountability establishes who is answerable for the outcome.
* **Policy vs. Procedure:** A policy establishes required principles or expectations; a procedure describes how those requirements are performed.
* **Oversight vs. Management:** Oversight evaluates and challenges activities; management performs and controls the activities.
* **Risk Owner vs. Control Owner:** The risk owner is accountable for managing a risk; the control owner is accountable for the effectiveness of a particular control.
* **Assurance vs. Audit:** Audit is one form of assurance. Assurance may also include assessments, reviews, certifications, testing, monitoring, and other independent or objective evaluation activities.
* **Governance Model vs. Governance Framework:** A governance model describes how governance is structured and operates, while a governance framework provides the broader principles, requirements, processes, and mechanisms supporting governance.

### Governance in the GRC Context

Within a **GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance)** environment, governance establishes the direction and accountability necessary for risk and compliance activities to operate effectively. Governance determines **who makes decisions, who is accountable, what requirements apply, how risks are escalated, how compliance is monitored, and how performance is reported**.

The governance terminology in this section therefore provides the foundation for the subsequent glossary sections covering **risk, compliance, controls, cybersecurity, privacy, audit, resilience, AI governance, regulatory requirements, and GRC technology**.
