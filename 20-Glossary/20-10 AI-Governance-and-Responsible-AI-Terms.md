# 20.10 AI Governance and Responsible AI Terms

This section defines the core terminology associated with **AI governance, responsible AI, trustworthy AI, AI risk management, AI safety, AI security, AI ethics, AI lifecycle management, and AI assurance**.

> **Terminology note:** This glossary is designed for the GRC knowledge base and aligns conceptually with major AI governance references including **ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894, NIST AI RMF, NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile, OECD AI Principles, and the EU AI Act**. Terminology can vary slightly between frameworks and jurisdictions.

---

# AI Governance and Responsible AI Glossary

| Term                                            | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**                | A machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers from inputs how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments.              |
| **AI Governance**                               | The system of policies, processes, roles, responsibilities, controls, oversight mechanisms, and decision rights used to direct and manage AI-related activities and risks.                                                                 |
| **Responsible AI**                              | An approach to designing, developing, deploying, and operating AI in a manner that addresses ethical, legal, societal, security, privacy, safety, and organizational considerations.                                                       |
| **Trustworthy AI**                              | AI that demonstrates characteristics such as validity and reliability, safety, security and resilience, accountability and transparency, explainability and interpretability, privacy enhancement, and fairness with harmful bias managed. |
| **Secure AI**                                   | AI designed, developed, deployed, and operated to resist, detect, respond to, and recover from security threats and attacks.                                                                                                               |
| **AI Safety**                                   | The discipline of preventing AI systems from causing unacceptable harm through unsafe behavior, failures, misuse, accidents, or unintended consequences.                                                                                   |
| **AI Ethics**                                   | Principles and practices addressing moral, societal, human-rights, and value-based considerations associated with AI.                                                                                                                      |
| **AI Risk**                                     | The potential for an AI-related event, condition, or decision to adversely affect individuals, organizations, society, or the environment.                                                                                                 |
| **AI Risk Management**                          | The systematic process of identifying, analyzing, evaluating, treating, monitoring, and communicating risks associated with AI systems throughout their lifecycle.                                                                         |
| **AI Risk Assessment**                          | The structured evaluation of AI-related risks, including their likelihood, severity, affected stakeholders, and potential consequences.                                                                                                    |
| **AI Risk Treatment**                           | Actions taken to modify, mitigate, transfer, avoid, or otherwise manage AI-related risk.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **AI Risk Appetite**                            | The amount and type of AI-related risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.                                                                                                                                  |
| **AI Risk Tolerance**                           | The acceptable level of variation or exposure to a particular AI-related risk.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **AI Governance Framework**                     | The structured set of governance principles, policies, processes, controls, roles, and oversight mechanisms used to govern AI.                                                                                                             |
| **AI Governance Policy**                        | A formally approved organizational statement establishing requirements and expectations for responsible and controlled use of AI.                                                                                                          |
| **AI Governance Operating Model**               | The organizational structure defining how AI governance responsibilities, decision rights, processes, committees, controls, and reporting operate.                                                                                         |
| **AI Governance Committee**                     | A governance body responsible for reviewing, directing, overseeing, or approving AI-related initiatives, risks, policies, and decisions.                                                                                                   |
| **AI Governance Board**                         | A senior governance body providing strategic oversight and accountability for AI-related organizational activities and risks.                                                                                                              |
| **AI Governance Roles and Responsibilities**    | Defined accountability and decision rights for AI development, deployment, operation, monitoring, risk management, compliance, and oversight.                                                                                              |
| **AI System Owner**                             | The individual or organizational function accountable for an AI system's governance, performance, risk, and lifecycle management.                                                                                                          |
| **AI Product Owner**                            | The person responsible for business requirements, priorities, outcomes, and lifecycle decisions for an AI-enabled product or capability.                                                                                                   |
| **AI Developer**                                | An individual or organization involved in designing, developing, training, configuring, testing, or modifying an AI system.                                                                                                                |
| **AI Deployer**                                 | An organization or person that deploys or operates an AI system for a specific use case or environment.                                                                                                                                    |
| **AI Provider**                                 | An entity that develops an AI system or general-purpose AI model and places it on the market or puts it into service under applicable regulatory definitions.                                                                              |
| **AI User**                                     | A person or organization that interacts with or uses an AI system for an intended purpose.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **AI Subject**                                  | An individual, group, organization, or other entity affected by an AI system or its outputs.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Affected Stakeholder**                        | A person, group, organization, or community that may be affected by an AI system or its outcomes.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Human Oversight**                             | Measures designed to enable humans to effectively oversee, interpret, intervene in, or override AI system behavior where appropriate.                                                                                                      |
| **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)**                    | An AI operating model in which humans actively participate in decision-making or system actions.                                                                                                                                           |
| **Human-on-the-Loop (HOTL)**                    | An operating model in which humans supervise AI system operation and can intervene when required.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Human-in-Command**                            | A governance concept in which humans retain ultimate authority over significant AI-related decisions.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Human Oversight Mechanism**                   | A technical, organizational, or procedural mechanism enabling human monitoring, intervention, review, or override of AI system outputs or actions.                                                                                         |
| **AI Lifecycle**                                | The sequence of stages through which an AI system progresses, including planning, design, development, training, validation, deployment, operation, monitoring, maintenance, and retirement.                                               |
| **AI Lifecycle Management**                     | Governance and management activities performed throughout the AI system lifecycle.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **AI Use Case**                                 | A defined business, operational, analytical, or other purpose for which an AI system is used.                                                                                                                                              |
| **AI Use Case Inventory**                       | A maintained record of approved, proposed, deployed, or retired AI use cases.                                                                                                                                                              |
| **AI System Inventory**                         | A centralized record of AI systems, models, applications, owners, purposes, risk classifications, and lifecycle status.                                                                                                                    |
| **AI Asset Inventory**                          | A record of AI-related assets, including models, datasets, applications, infrastructure, APIs, and supporting services.                                                                                                                    |
| **AI Model Inventory**                          | A maintained register of AI models used, developed, purchased, or integrated by an organization.                                                                                                                                           |
| **AI Model**                                    | A computational representation learned from data that can generate predictions, classifications, decisions, content, or other outputs.                                                                                                     |
| **Machine Learning (ML)**                       | A form of AI in which systems learn patterns or relationships from data to perform tasks without being explicitly programmed for every outcome.                                                                                            |
| **Deep Learning**                               | A machine-learning approach based primarily on multilayer neural networks.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Generative AI (GenAI)**                       | AI capable of generating new content such as text, images, audio, video, software code, or other outputs.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Large Language Model (LLM)**                  | A large-scale AI model trained on substantial amounts of data to process and generate natural-language content.                                                                                                                            |
| **Foundation Model**                            | A model trained on broad data at scale that can be adapted or used for multiple downstream tasks.                                                                                                                                          |
| **General-Purpose AI (GPAI)**                   | AI models or systems capable of performing a wide range of tasks and potentially being integrated into numerous downstream applications.                                                                                                   |
| **AI Application**                              | A software application that incorporates one or more AI models or capabilities.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **AI Service**                                  | An AI capability provided as a service, commonly through an API, cloud platform, or managed service.                                                                                                                                       |
| **AI API**                                      | An application programming interface that allows software applications to interact with an AI model or service.                                                                                                                            |
| **Model-as-a-Service (MaaS)**                   | A service model in which AI models are provided through managed platforms or APIs rather than operated entirely by the customer.                                                                                                           |
| **AI Supply Chain**                             | The ecosystem of developers, model providers, data providers, cloud providers, software vendors, hardware suppliers, integrators, and other parties contributing to an AI system.                                                          |
| **AI Third-Party Risk**                         | Risk arising from external organizations, models, datasets, platforms, applications, APIs, or services used within an AI capability.                                                                                                       |
| **AI Vendor Risk Management**                   | Processes for identifying, assessing, treating, monitoring, and governing risks associated with third-party AI providers and suppliers.                                                                                                    |
| **AI Model Risk Management**                    | Processes for identifying, assessing, controlling, validating, monitoring, and governing risks associated with AI models.                                                                                                                  |
| **Model Governance**                            | Governance processes used to control model development, approval, validation, deployment, monitoring, change management, and retirement.                                                                                                   |
| **Model Lifecycle**                             | The stages through which a model progresses from development and training through deployment, monitoring, modification, and retirement.                                                                                                    |
| **Model Validation**                            | Independent or structured evaluation to determine whether a model performs as intended and meets defined requirements.                                                                                                                     |
| **Model Monitoring**                            | Continuous or periodic observation of model performance, behavior, drift, risk, and other relevant characteristics.                                                                                                                        |
| **Model Drift**                                 | A change in the relationship between model inputs and outputs or in the underlying data patterns that may reduce model performance.                                                                                                        |
| **Data Drift**                                  | Change in the statistical characteristics or distribution of input data over time.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Concept Drift**                               | Change in the underlying relationship between input variables and the target outcome over time.                                                                                                                                            |
| **Model Degradation**                           | Decline in model performance, reliability, accuracy, or other defined quality characteristics.                                                                                                                                             |
| **Model Card**                                  | Documentation describing a model's intended uses, limitations, evaluation results, risks, and other relevant characteristics.                                                                                                              |
| **System Card**                                 | Documentation describing an AI system, including its capabilities, limitations, evaluations, safety considerations, and risks.                                                                                                             |
| **Data Card**                                   | Documentation describing a dataset's characteristics, provenance, intended use, limitations, and relevant risks.                                                                                                                           |
| **AI Impact Assessment**                        | A structured assessment of the potential effects of an AI system on individuals, groups, organizations, society, or the environment.                                                                                                       |
| **Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA)**         | A structured process for evaluating potential impacts, risks, and consequences of an algorithmic or AI system.                                                                                                                             |
| **Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA)** | An assessment of potential impacts of certain AI systems on fundamental rights, particularly relevant under applicable regulatory regimes.                                                                                                 |
| **Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)**             | An assessment of privacy risks associated with processing personal information.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)**    | A formal assessment used to identify and mitigate privacy and data-protection risks associated with processing personal data.                                                                                                              |
| **AI Security Risk Assessment**                 | Assessment of cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities affecting AI models, systems, infrastructure, data, and interfaces.                                                                                                                |
| **AI Safety Risk Assessment**                   | Assessment of risks that an AI system may cause physical, psychological, societal, environmental, or other unacceptable harm.                                                                                                              |
| **Ethical Impact Assessment**                   | Evaluation of ethical consequences associated with an AI system and its intended or unintended uses.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Bias**                                        | Systematic differences in AI outputs or decisions that may result in unfair or undesirable outcomes.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Algorithmic Bias**                            | Bias arising from the design, implementation, training, data, or operation of an algorithmic system.                                                                                                                                       |
| **AI Bias**                                     | Systematic error or preference in AI system outputs that may produce inequitable or undesirable outcomes.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Fairness**                                    | The extent to which an AI system avoids unjustified or harmful differences in treatment or outcomes among relevant individuals or groups.                                                                                                  |
| **Fairness Assessment**                         | Evaluation of whether an AI system produces materially different or unjustified outcomes across relevant groups or populations.                                                                                                            |
| **Discrimination**                              | Unjustified differential treatment or adverse impact on individuals or groups based on protected or otherwise relevant characteristics.                                                                                                    |
| **Protected Characteristic**                    | A characteristic protected by applicable law or regulation, such as race, sex, age, disability, or other legally protected attributes.                                                                                                     |
| **Representativeness**                          | The degree to which training, validation, or evaluation data appropriately reflects the populations or conditions relevant to intended use.                                                                                                |
| **Inclusiveness**                               | The extent to which an AI system considers and appropriately serves diverse users and affected populations.                                                                                                                                |
| **Accessibility**                               | The ability of people with diverse abilities and circumstances to effectively access and use an AI system.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Transparency**                                | The degree to which information about an AI system, its purpose, operation, limitations, risks, and decision processes is made available to relevant stakeholders.                                                                         |
| **Explainability**                              | The ability to provide meaningful explanations of how or why an AI system produced a particular output or decision.                                                                                                                        |
| **Interpretability**                            | The degree to which humans can understand the internal logic, behavior, or relationships represented by an AI model.                                                                                                                       |
| **Explainable AI (XAI)**                        | Methods and techniques designed to make AI system behavior or outputs more understandable to humans.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Interpretability Technique**                  | A technical method used to understand or explain model behavior, such as feature importance or local explanations.                                                                                                                         |
| **Accountability**                              | The obligation of individuals or organizations to accept responsibility for AI-related decisions, actions, outcomes, and impacts.                                                                                                          |
| **Traceability**                                | The ability to track and reconstruct relevant information about an AI system's data, models, processes, decisions, changes, and lifecycle activities.                                                                                      |
| **Auditability**                                | The ability to provide sufficient evidence and records to enable an AI system or governance process to be independently evaluated.                                                                                                         |
| **AI Assurance**                                | Activities designed to provide confidence that an AI system is appropriately governed, controlled, secure, reliable, compliant, and fit for purpose.                                                                                       |
| **AI Audit**                                    | A systematic and independent examination of AI systems, governance processes, controls, risks, or compliance against defined criteria.                                                                                                     |
| **AI Assessment**                               | A structured evaluation of an AI system, process, model, control, risk, or governance capability.                                                                                                                                          |
| **AI Certification**                            | Formal third-party confirmation that an AI-related system, process, or management system meets defined certification criteria.                                                                                                             |
| **AI Conformity Assessment**                    | Evaluation of whether an AI system, process, or organization meets specified requirements or standards.                                                                                                                                    |
| **AI Control**                                  | A technical, organizational, administrative, or procedural safeguard designed to manage AI-related risks.                                                                                                                                  |
| **AI Control Framework**                        | A structured collection of AI-related controls mapped to governance, risk, security, privacy, safety, ethical, and compliance objectives.                                                                                                  |
| **AI Policy**                                   | A formal organizational requirement governing the development, procurement, deployment, use, monitoring, or retirement of AI.                                                                                                              |
| **AI Standard**                                 | A documented technical or organizational requirement used to establish consistent AI practices.                                                                                                                                            |
| **AI Procedure**                                | A documented sequence of actions for performing a specific AI governance, risk, security, compliance, or operational activity.                                                                                                             |
| **AI Control Objective**                        | The desired condition that an AI control is intended to achieve.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **AI Risk Register**                            | A structured record of identified AI risks, risk owners, ratings, treatments, status, and monitoring information.                                                                                                                          |
| **AI Incident**                                 | An event involving an AI system that results in or could result in harm, security compromise, policy violation, regulatory noncompliance, or unacceptable behavior.                                                                        |
| **AI Incident Management**                      | Processes for detecting, reporting, analyzing, responding to, containing, recovering from, and learning from AI-related incidents.                                                                                                         |
| **AI Incident Response Plan**                   | A documented plan defining responsibilities and procedures for responding to AI-related incidents.                                                                                                                                         |
| **AI Failure**                                  | A condition in which an AI system fails to perform as intended or violates defined requirements.                                                                                                                                           |
| **AI Misuse**                                   | Use of an AI system in an unauthorized, inappropriate, harmful, or unintended manner.                                                                                                                                                      |
| **AI Abuse**                                    | Deliberate exploitation or use of an AI system to cause harm, bypass controls, or perform prohibited activities.                                                                                                                           |
| **Misalignment**                                | A condition where an AI system's behavior or outputs diverge from intended objectives, values, policies, or constraints.                                                                                                                   |
| **AI Hallucination**                            | An AI-generated output that contains false, fabricated, unsupported, or misleading information presented as if it were valid.                                                                                                              |
| **Confabulation**                               | Generation of plausible but inaccurate or unsupported information by an AI system.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Prompt Injection**                            | An attack in which malicious or manipulative instructions are introduced into an AI system's inputs to influence behavior or bypass intended controls.                                                                                     |
| **Indirect Prompt Injection**                   | Prompt injection in which malicious instructions are embedded in external content that an AI system later processes.                                                                                                                       |
| **Jailbreak**                                   | A technique intended to bypass an AI system's safety, policy, or behavioral restrictions.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Adversarial Example**                         | An input intentionally modified to cause an AI model to produce an incorrect, unsafe, or unexpected output.                                                                                                                                |
| **Adversarial Attack**                          | An attack designed to manipulate an AI system by exploiting weaknesses in its data, model, inputs, architecture, or implementation.                                                                                                        |
| **Data Poisoning**                              | Deliberate manipulation of training, validation, or other data to influence model behavior or performance.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Model Poisoning**                             | Deliberate manipulation of a model or model components to introduce malicious or undesirable behavior.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Backdoor**                                    | A hidden mechanism that allows an attacker to trigger unauthorized or malicious model behavior under specific conditions.                                                                                                                  |
| **Model Evasion**                               | An attack in which inputs are crafted to evade AI-based detection or classification.                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Model Extraction**                            | An attack in which an adversary attempts to reproduce or approximate the behavior of a proprietary AI model.                                                                                                                               |
| **Model Inversion**                             | An attack attempting to infer sensitive information about model training data or inputs from model behavior.                                                                                                                               |
| **Membership Inference**                        | An attack attempting to determine whether a specific data record was included in a model's training dataset.                                                                                                                               |
| **Data Leakage**                                | Unauthorized exposure or disclosure of information through an AI system, model, application, output, or supporting infrastructure.                                                                                                         |
| **Prompt Leakage**                              | Unauthorized disclosure of system prompts, instructions, policies, or hidden configuration information by an AI system.                                                                                                                    |
| **Sensitive Data Exposure**                     | Unauthorized disclosure of personal, confidential, proprietary, regulated, or otherwise sensitive information.                                                                                                                             |
| **Training Data**                               | Data used to train or fit an AI model.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Validation Data**                             | Data used to tune model parameters, evaluate model choices, or support model development.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Test Data**                                   | Data used to evaluate model performance after development or training.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Evaluation Dataset**                          | A dataset used to assess model or system performance against defined criteria.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Data Provenance**                             | Information describing the origin, history, transformations, ownership, and lineage of data.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Data Lineage**                                | The traceable history of data as it moves through collection, transformation, storage, processing, and use.                                                                                                                                |
| **Data Quality**                                | The degree to which data is accurate, complete, consistent, timely, relevant, valid, and fit for intended use.                                                                                                                             |
| **Synthetic Data**                              | Artificially generated data designed to reproduce relevant characteristics or patterns of real-world data.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Personal Data**                               | Information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)**   | Information that can be used to identify or be linked to an individual, depending on the applicable legal definition.                                                                                                                      |
| **Privacy-Preserving AI**                       | AI approaches designed to reduce privacy risks while enabling useful analysis or model functionality.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Differential Privacy**                        | A mathematical privacy technique designed to limit the ability to determine whether a specific individual contributed data to a dataset or computation.                                                                                    |
| **Federated Learning**                          | A machine-learning approach in which models are trained across distributed data sources without necessarily centralizing the underlying data.                                                                                              |
| **Data Minimization**                           | Principle of collecting and processing only the data necessary for the intended purpose.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Purpose Limitation**                          | Principle that personal or sensitive data should be collected and used for defined and legitimate purposes.                                                                                                                                |
| **Consent**                                     | A legally or ethically recognized indication of an individual's agreement to a particular processing activity, where applicable.                                                                                                           |
| **Automated Decision-Making**                   | Decision-making performed partly or entirely by automated systems with limited or no human intervention.                                                                                                                                   |
| **High-Impact AI**                              | AI whose use may have significant consequences for individuals, organizations, society, safety, rights, or critical services.                                                                                                              |
| **High-Risk AI System**                         | An AI system classified as presenting elevated risk under a particular legal or regulatory framework, such as the EU AI Act.                                                                                                               |
| **Prohibited AI Practice**                      | An AI practice prohibited by applicable law or regulation.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **AI Regulatory Compliance**                    | The process of ensuring that AI systems and their governance practices meet applicable legal and regulatory requirements.                                                                                                                  |
| **AI Act**                                      | The European Union's legal framework establishing harmonized rules for artificial intelligence and a risk-based regulatory approach.                                                                                                       |
| **AI Literacy**                                 | The skills, knowledge, and understanding necessary for individuals and organizations to use, manage, develop, or oversee AI appropriately.                                                                                                 |
| **General-Purpose AI Model**                    | An AI model capable of performing a wide range of distinct tasks and potentially serving as a component of multiple downstream AI systems.                                                                                                 |
| **Foundation Model**                            | A broadly trained model that can be adapted or reused for multiple applications and tasks.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**        | An AI architecture that retrieves relevant information from external sources and provides it to a generative model to improve the relevance or factual grounding of outputs.                                                               |
| **Grounding**                                   | Connecting AI-generated outputs to trusted, relevant, and verifiable information sources.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Guardrail**                                   | A technical, procedural, or policy control designed to constrain AI behavior within acceptable boundaries.                                                                                                                                 |
| **AI Content Moderation**                       | Processes used to identify, classify, filter, or manage potentially harmful or prohibited AI-generated or user-provided content.                                                                                                           |
| **Content Provenance**                          | Information about the origin, creation process, modifications, and history of digital content.                                                                                                                                             |
| **AI Watermarking**                             | Techniques used to embed detectable signals or patterns into AI-generated content to support identification or provenance.                                                                                                                 |
| **Deepfake**                                    | Synthetic or manipulated media generated or altered using AI to represent a person, event, or statement in a misleading manner.                                                                                                            |
| **Synthetic Media**                             | AI-generated or AI-manipulated audio, video, images, text, or other media.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Model Transparency**                          | Availability of meaningful information about a model's purpose, development, limitations, risks, performance, and operation.                                                                                                               |
| **Model Robustness**                            | The ability of a model to maintain acceptable performance when exposed to expected variations, disturbances, or adversarial conditions.                                                                                                    |
| **Model Reliability**                           | The degree to which an AI system consistently performs as intended under defined conditions.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Model Validity**                              | The degree to which a model accurately represents or solves the intended problem.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Model Accuracy**                              | The degree to which model outputs correspond to expected or correct results under defined evaluation conditions.                                                                                                                           |
| **Precision**                                   | The proportion of predicted positive results that are actually positive.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Recall**                                      | The proportion of actual positive cases correctly identified by the model.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **F1 Score**                                    | A performance metric representing the harmonic mean of precision and recall.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **False Positive**                              | A case in which a system incorrectly identifies something as positive or present.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **False Negative**                              | A case in which a system fails to identify something that is actually positive or present.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Performance Threshold**                       | A predefined level of model performance required for acceptable use.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Safety Threshold**                            | A predefined boundary beyond which AI behavior or risk is considered unacceptable.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Red Teaming**                                 | Adversarial testing designed to identify weaknesses, harmful behaviors, vulnerabilities, misuse opportunities, or failure modes in an AI system.                                                                                           |
| **AI Red Team**                                 | A group responsible for conducting adversarial assessments of AI systems.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **AI Evaluation**                               | Systematic testing of an AI model or system against defined performance, safety, security, fairness, reliability, or other criteria.                                                                                                       |
| **Benchmark**                                   | A standardized dataset, task, or measurement method used to compare AI system performance.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Benchmark Testing**                           | Evaluation of an AI system against standardized performance or risk criteria.                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Safety Evaluation**                           | Testing designed to identify harmful, unsafe, prohibited, or unexpected AI behaviors.                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Security Evaluation**                         | Testing designed to identify cybersecurity weaknesses and attack opportunities affecting AI systems.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Bias Testing**                                | Testing designed to identify systematic differences or unfair outcomes across relevant groups.                                                                                                                                             |
| **Stress Testing**                              | Testing AI systems under extreme, unusual, high-load, adversarial, or otherwise challenging conditions.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Scenario Testing**                            | Evaluation of an AI system against predefined operational, risk, security, safety, or ethical scenarios.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Human Review**                                | Examination of AI outputs, decisions, or actions by an appropriately qualified human.                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Human Override**                              | The ability of an authorized person to stop, modify, reject, or override an AI-generated action or decision.                                                                                                                               |
| **Kill Switch**                                 | A mechanism designed to rapidly disable an AI system or capability when predefined risk conditions occur.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Fail-Safe**                                   | A design principle in which a system transitions to a safer state when a failure or abnormal condition occurs.                                                                                                                             |
| **Fail-Secure**                                 | A design principle in which a system defaults to a secure state when a failure occurs.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Safe Deployment**                             | Deployment of an AI system only after appropriate risk assessment, testing, validation, controls, and approval have been completed.                                                                                                        |
| **AI Change Management**                        | Governance and control of changes to AI models, datasets, prompts, applications, configurations, infrastructure, and use cases.                                                                                                            |
| **Model Versioning**                            | Maintaining identifiable versions of AI models and documenting changes between versions.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Model Retirement**                            | Controlled removal of an AI model from operational use.                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **AI Decommissioning**                          | Controlled retirement and removal of an AI system, including associated models, data, integrations, credentials, and infrastructure.                                                                                                       |
| **AI Continuous Monitoring**                    | Ongoing observation of AI performance, security, safety, fairness, compliance, and operational behavior.                                                                                                                                   |
| **AI Post-Market Monitoring**                   | Monitoring an AI system after deployment or market release to identify emerging risks, incidents, failures, or regulatory concerns.                                                                                                        |
| **AI Transparency Report**                      | A report communicating relevant information about an organization's AI systems, governance, risks, performance, incidents, or responsible-AI practices.                                                                                    |
| **AI Register**                                 | A centralized record of AI systems, use cases, models, owners, risks, suppliers, approvals, and lifecycle status.                                                                                                                          |
| **AI Acceptable Use Policy**                    | A policy defining permitted, restricted, and prohibited uses of AI within an organization.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Shadow AI**                                   | Unauthorized or unmanaged use of AI tools, services, models, or applications by personnel outside approved governance processes.                                                                                                           |
| **AI Governance Maturity**                      | The degree to which AI governance processes, controls, capabilities, accountability, and oversight are established, implemented, measured, and improved.                                                                                   |
| **AI Governance Maturity Assessment**           | An evaluation of the organization's AI governance capabilities against defined maturity criteria.                                                                                                                                          |
| **AI Governance Gap Assessment**                | A structured comparison of current AI governance capabilities against desired requirements, standards, regulations, or target-state capabilities.                                                                                          |
| **AI Governance Roadmap**                       | A prioritized plan for developing, implementing, and improving AI governance capabilities.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Responsible AI Framework**                    | A structured set of principles, policies, processes, controls, and practices for developing and using AI responsibly.                                                                                                                      |
| **AI Ethics Committee**                         | A governance body responsible for reviewing ethical implications, societal impacts, and responsible-AI concerns.                                                                                                                           |
| **Ethical AI**                                  | AI designed and operated in accordance with applicable ethical principles, societal expectations, and organizational values.                                                                                                               |
| **Societal Impact**                             | The effects of an AI system on communities, institutions, society, culture, the economy, or public welfare.                                                                                                                                |
| **Environmental Impact of AI**                  | The environmental effects associated with AI development and operation, including energy consumption, resource use, emissions, and hardware lifecycle impacts.                                                                             |
| **AI Sustainability**                           | The practice of developing and operating AI while considering environmental, social, economic, and long-term sustainability impacts.                                                                                                       |
| **AI Accountability Framework**                 | A structured framework defining responsibility, oversight, evidence, decision rights, and mechanisms for addressing AI-related failures or harms.                                                                                          |

# Core Responsible AI Principles

A GRC professional should understand the major principles commonly associated with responsible and trustworthy AI:

| Principle                         | Meaning                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fairness**                      | AI should avoid unjustified discriminatory or inequitable outcomes.                                            |
| **Transparency**                  | Relevant stakeholders should receive meaningful information about AI systems and their use.                    |
| **Explainability**                | Appropriate explanations should be available for AI outputs or decisions.                                      |
| **Accountability**                | Organizations and responsible individuals should be answerable for AI outcomes.                                |
| **Privacy**                       | AI should protect personal and sensitive information.                                                          |
| **Security**                      | AI systems should resist and recover from cybersecurity threats.                                               |
| **Safety**                        | AI should operate within acceptable safety boundaries.                                                         |
| **Reliability**                   | AI should perform consistently and as intended.                                                                |
| **Robustness**                    | AI should remain effective under expected variations and challenging conditions.                               |
| **Human Oversight**               | Humans should retain appropriate control over consequential AI systems.                                        |
| **Inclusiveness**                 | AI should consider diverse users and affected populations.                                                     |
| **Accountability & Traceability** | Decisions, data, models, and actions should be sufficiently documented to support investigation and assurance. |

# AI Risk Categories

For the GRC knowledge base, AI risks can be grouped into several major categories:

### 1. Strategic Risk

Risk that AI initiatives fail to support organizational strategy or create unacceptable business consequences.

### 2. Operational Risk

Risk arising from failures in AI processes, systems, people, models, or operations.

### 3. Cybersecurity Risk

Risk arising from attacks against AI models, data, applications, infrastructure, or interfaces.

### 4. Privacy Risk

Risk associated with unauthorized or inappropriate collection, processing, disclosure, or inference of personal information.

### 5. Safety Risk

Risk that AI causes physical, psychological, environmental, or other unacceptable harm.

### 6. Ethical Risk

Risk that AI behavior or outcomes conflict with ethical principles, organizational values, or societal expectations.

### 7. Compliance Risk

Risk of violating laws, regulations, contractual requirements, or organizational policies.

### 8. Model Risk

Risk that an AI model is inaccurate, unreliable, biased, poorly designed, improperly implemented, or unsuitable for its intended purpose.

### 9. Third-Party Risk

Risk introduced through external AI models, platforms, vendors, cloud providers, datasets, APIs, or other suppliers.

### 10. Reputational Risk

Risk that AI failures, misuse, bias, security incidents, or harmful outcomes damage stakeholder trust or organizational reputation.

# AI Governance vs. AI Security vs. Responsible AI

These concepts should **not be treated as synonyms**.

| Concept                | Primary Focus                                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **AI Governance**      | How the organization directs, controls, oversees, and manages AI                                      |
| **Responsible AI**     | How AI is developed and used in an ethical, lawful, safe, secure, fair, and accountable manner        |
| **AI Security**        | Protecting AI systems, models, data, infrastructure, and interfaces against security threats          |
| **AI Safety**          | Preventing unacceptable harm resulting from AI behavior or failure                                    |
| **AI Ethics**          | Addressing moral, societal, human-rights, and value-based concerns                                    |
| **AI Risk Management** | Identifying, evaluating, treating, and monitoring AI-related risks                                    |
| **AI Assurance**       | Providing evidence and confidence that AI governance, controls, and systems meet defined expectations |
