# 18.5 GRC Process and Workflow Diagrams

## Part 1 – GRC Process Lifecycle Diagram

A **GRC Process Lifecycle Diagram** shows how governance, risk management, and compliance activities operate as an interconnected and continuous process rather than as isolated activities.

The fundamental GRC relationship is:

```text
Governance
     ↓
Risk Management
     ↓
Compliance
     ↓
Monitoring
     ↓
Reporting
     ↓
Management Decision
     ↓
Continuous Improvement
     ↺
```

GRC connects organizational objectives with risk and compliance requirements.

```text
Business Objectives
       ↓
Governance
       ↓
Risk Management
       ↓
Controls
       ↓
Compliance
       ↓
Assurance
       ↓
Reporting
       ↓
Management Decisions
```

The purpose of the GRC lifecycle is to help an organization answer three fundamental questions:

```text
Governance:
Are we making the right decisions?

Risk:
Are we managing uncertainty appropriately?

Compliance:
Are we meeting applicable requirements?
```

These three areas should work together.

```text
              GOVERNANCE
                 /   \
                /     \
               ↓       ↓
             RISK ←→ COMPLIANCE
```

A mature GRC program integrates all three.

```text
Governance
     ↓
Defines Direction
     ↓
Risk Management
     ↓
Identifies and Manages Uncertainty
     ↓
Compliance
     ↓
Demonstrates Requirements Are Met
     ↓
Assurance
     ↓
Provides Confidence
```

The complete GRC lifecycle can be represented as:

```text
                  BUSINESS OBJECTIVES
                         ↓
                    GOVERNANCE
                         ↓
                  RISK IDENTIFICATION
                         ↓
                   RISK ASSESSMENT
                         ↓
                 RISK TREATMENT
                         ↓
                    CONTROLS
                         ↓
                 COMPLIANCE ASSESSMENT
                         ↓
                      MONITORING
                         ↓
                     REPORTING
                         ↓
                  MANAGEMENT DECISION
                         ↓
                  CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
                         ↺
```

Governance establishes the organization's direction.

```text
Board / Executive Management
          ↓
Strategy
          ↓
Policies
          ↓
Risk Appetite
          ↓
Accountability
```

This creates the foundation for risk management.

```text
Governance
     ↓
Risk Appetite
     ↓
Risk Management
```

Risk management then identifies uncertainty that could affect organizational objectives.

```text
Business Objective
       ↓
Potential Threat / Opportunity
       ↓
Risk Identification
       ↓
Risk Assessment
       ↓
Risk Treatment
```

The organization then implements controls to manage identified risks.

```text
Risk
 ↓
Risk Treatment
 ↓
Control Objective
 ↓
Control
 ↓
Control Operation
```

Compliance provides another layer.

```text
External Requirements
        ↓
Internal Policies
        ↓
Controls
        ↓
Compliance Assessment
        ↓
Compliance Status
```

These processes should not operate independently.

For example:

```text
New Regulation
      ↓
Compliance Requirement
      ↓
Risk Assessment
      ↓
Control Requirement
      ↓
Control Implementation
      ↓
Compliance Testing
      ↓
Management Reporting
```

This demonstrates how a regulatory change can affect the entire GRC lifecycle.

A GRC process can also begin with a business change.

```text
Business Change
      ↓
Risk Assessment
      ↓
Control Review
      ↓
Compliance Review
      ↓
Management Approval
      ↓
Implementation
      ↓
Monitoring
```

Examples include:

```text
New Technology
New Product
New Supplier
Cloud Migration
Acquisition
New Regulation
New Geographic Market
Organizational Restructuring
```

Each may trigger multiple GRC activities.

The GRC lifecycle should therefore include **change management**.

```text
Change
  ↓
Impact Assessment
  ↓
Risk Assessment
  ↓
Compliance Assessment
  ↓
Control Assessment
  ↓
Approval
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Post-Implementation Review
```

Another important component is **policy management**.

```text
Regulation / Risk
       ↓
Policy Requirement
       ↓
Policy Development
       ↓
Policy Approval
       ↓
Policy Communication
       ↓
Policy Implementation
       ↓
Policy Review
```

Policies should be connected to controls.

```text
Policy
  ↓
Control Objective
  ↓
Control
  ↓
Control Activity
```

And controls should be connected to risks.

```text
Risk
 ↓
Control
 ↓
Control Effectiveness
 ↓
Residual Risk
```

This creates the broader relationship:

```text
Requirement
     ↓
Policy
     ↓
Risk
     ↓
Control
     ↓
Evidence
     ↓
Assessment
     ↓
Reporting
```

The GRC lifecycle also requires **accountability**.

A RACI structure can support this.

```text
Governance
     ↓
Accountability
     ↓
RACI
     ↓
Responsible
Accountable
Consulted
Informed
```

For example:

```text
Risk Assessment

Risk Owner       → Accountable
Risk Analyst     → Responsible
Security         → Consulted
Management       → Informed
```

Clear accountability prevents GRC activities from becoming "everyone's responsibility and nobody's responsibility."

The lifecycle also includes **evidence management**.

```text
Control
   ↓
Control Activity
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Evidence Validation
   ↓
Assessment
```

Evidence should be traceable.

```text
Evidence
   ↓
Control
   ↓
Requirement
   ↓
Assessment
```

This creates auditability.

The GRC lifecycle should also include **issue and remediation management**.

```text
Assessment
    ↓
Finding
    ↓
Risk
    ↓
Remediation
    ↓
Action Owner
    ↓
Due Date
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Closure
```

An issue should not simply disappear after remediation is marked complete.

```text
Remediation Complete
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Effective?
     ↙      ↘
   Yes       No
    ↓         ↓
  Close     Reopen
```

Monitoring provides continuous visibility.

```text
Controls
   ↓
Metrics
   ↓
KPIs / KRIs
   ↓
GRC Dashboard
   ↓
Management Reporting
```

For example:

```text
Control Testing
      ↓
95% Effective

Open High Risks
      ↓
8

Overdue Actions
      ↓
4

Compliance
      ↓
91%
```

These metrics can then be escalated according to thresholds.

```text
Metric
  ↓
Threshold
  ↓
Threshold Breached?
 ↙              ↘
No               Yes
 ↓                 ↓
Monitor          Escalate
```

This is where GRC becomes an active management system rather than simply a documentation function.

The lifecycle also includes **risk appetite**.

```text
Risk Assessment
      ↓
Risk Rating
      ↓
Risk Appetite
      ↓
Management Decision
```

For example:

```text
Risk:
High

Risk Appetite:
Medium

        ↓

Outside Appetite
        ↓
Treatment Required
```

If the risk is within appetite:

```text
Risk:
Medium

Risk Appetite:
Medium

        ↓

Management May Accept
        ↓
Monitor
```

Risk appetite therefore influences treatment and escalation.

The relationship can be shown as:

```text
Risk
 ↓
Risk Rating
 ↓
Risk Appetite
 ↓
Treatment / Acceptance
 ↓
Residual Risk
 ↓
Monitoring
```

Compliance requirements can also affect risk appetite decisions.

```text
Regulatory Requirement
       ↓
Mandatory Obligation
       ↓
Risk Treatment
       ↓
Control
```

In some cases, the organization cannot simply accept the risk because the underlying requirement is mandatory.

For example:

```text
Legal Requirement
       ↓
Mandatory Control
       ↓
Non-Compliance
       ↓
Remediation Required
```

This is an important distinction between ordinary business risk and regulatory obligations.

The GRC lifecycle also connects to **internal audit**.

```text
GRC Processes
      ↓
Control Environment
      ↓
Internal Audit
      ↓
Independent Assurance
      ↓
Audit Findings
      ↓
Remediation
```

External audits and regulatory examinations may follow a similar pathway.

```text
Control Environment
      ↓
External Assessment
      ↓
Findings
      ↓
Management Response
      ↓
Remediation
      ↓
Validation
```

The overall assurance model can therefore be represented as:

```text
First Line
Operations
     ↓
Second Line
GRC / Risk / Compliance
     ↓
Third Line
Internal Audit
     ↓
External Assurance
     ↓
Board / Governing Body
```

The GRC lifecycle should also incorporate **security incidents**.

```text
Security Incident
       ↓
Incident Management
       ↓
Root Cause Analysis
       ↓
Control Weakness
       ↓
Risk Reassessment
       ↓
Remediation
       ↓
Control Improvement
```

For example:

```text
Data Breach
    ↓
Investigation
    ↓
Root Cause
    ↓
Weak Access Control
    ↓
Risk Increased
    ↓
Additional Controls
    ↓
Retesting
```

This allows lessons learned from incidents to improve the GRC environment.

The same applies to audit findings.

```text
Audit Finding
      ↓
Control Weakness
      ↓
Risk
      ↓
Remediation
      ↓
Control Improvement
```

And to changes in threat intelligence.

```text
New Threat
    ↓
Threat Assessment
    ↓
Risk Assessment
    ↓
Control Review
    ↓
Additional Treatment
```

This creates a dynamic GRC environment.

A mature GRC lifecycle therefore looks like:

```text
                    BUSINESS OBJECTIVES
                           ↓
                       GOVERNANCE
                           ↓
                      RISK APPETITE
                           ↓
                     RISK MANAGEMENT
                           ↓
                       CONTROLS
                           ↓
                      COMPLIANCE
                           ↓
                     MONITORING
                           ↓
                      ASSURANCE
                           ↓
                       REPORTING
                           ↓
                  MANAGEMENT DECISIONS
                           ↓
                  CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
                           ↓
                        REASSESS
                           ↺
```

The lifecycle is supported by several interconnected GRC processes:

```text
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │      GOVERNANCE      │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │   RISK MANAGEMENT    │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │   CONTROL MANAGEMENT │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │     COMPLIANCE       │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │      ASSURANCE       │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │      REPORTING       │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
              ┌──────────────────────┐
              │ MANAGEMENT DECISION  │
              └──────────┬───────────┘
                         ↓
                 CONTINUOUS
                 IMPROVEMENT
                         │
                         └──────────────→ REASSESS
```

The most important concept is that GRC is **cyclical rather than linear**.

```text
Assess
  ↓
Decide
  ↓
Implement
  ↓
Monitor
  ↓
Report
  ↓
Improve
  ↓
Reassess
  ↺
```

The cycle continues because the organization, technology, threats, regulations, and business objectives continuously change.

A mature GRC professional should therefore understand the complete connection:

```text
Business Strategy
      ↓
Governance
      ↓
Risk Appetite
      ↓
Risk
      ↓
Controls
      ↓
Compliance
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Assessment
      ↓
Findings
      ↓
Remediation
      ↓
Assurance
      ↓
Reporting
      ↓
Management Decision
      ↓
Continuous Improvement
      ↺
```

The key principle is:

> **GRC is an integrated management cycle that connects business objectives, governance, risk, controls, compliance, assurance, reporting, and decision-making into a continuous process of managing and improving organizational performance and resilience.**
