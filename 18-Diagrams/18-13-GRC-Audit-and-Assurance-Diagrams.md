# 18.13 GRC Audit and Assurance Diagrams

## Part 1 – Internal Audit Lifecycle

Internal audit is a fundamental component of an organization's governance, risk management, and control environment. While management owns risks and controls, internal audit provides **independent and objective assurance** that governance, risk management, and control processes are designed appropriately and operating effectively.

A visual representation of the internal audit lifecycle helps GRC professionals understand how an audit moves from **risk-based planning through execution, reporting, remediation monitoring, and closure**.

The basic lifecycle can be represented as:

```text
                  INTERNAL AUDIT LIFECYCLE

                       Audit Universe
                            ↓
                     Risk Assessment
                            ↓
                    Audit Planning
                            ↓
                  Audit Engagement
                            ↓
                  Scope & Objectives
                            ↓
                   Audit Fieldwork
                            ↓
              Evidence Collection & Testing
                            ↓
                    Finding Evaluation
                            ↓
                   Audit Reporting
                            ↓
                Management Response
                            ↓
                 Remediation Tracking
                            ↓
                 Follow-Up Validation
                            ↓
                      Audit Closure
                            ↓
                    Assurance Reporting
```

This lifecycle provides a structured approach for determining **what should be audited, why it should be audited, how it should be tested, what was identified, and whether identified weaknesses were appropriately addressed**.

# 1. Purpose of Internal Audit

The primary purpose of internal audit is to provide independent assurance and advisory services designed to add value and improve organizational operations.

Internal audit generally evaluates:

```text
Governance
Risk Management
Internal Controls
Compliance
Security
Financial Processes
Operational Effectiveness
Information Technology
Third-Party Risk
Business Resilience
```

In a GRC environment, internal audit provides an independent perspective on whether the organization's governance and control mechanisms are functioning as intended.

# 4. Risk-Based Audit Planning

Internal audit should not simply audit everything equally.

Audit resources are limited, so audit priorities should generally reflect organizational risk.

A simplified model is:

```text
                    AUDIT UNIVERSE
                           ↓
                    Risk Assessment
                           ↓
              ┌────────────┼────────────┐
              ↓            ↓            ↓
             High        Medium         Low
             Risk         Risk          Risk
              ↓            ↓            ↓
           Priority     Planned       Lower
            Audit        Audit        Priority
```

Higher-risk areas generally receive greater audit attention.

# 10. Audit Objective

The audit objective defines **what the audit is intended to determine**.

Examples:

> Determine whether access management controls are appropriately designed and operating effectively.

Or:

> Determine whether the organization's third-party risk management process adequately identifies, assesses, and monitors supplier risk.

A clear objective prevents the audit from becoming unnecessarily broad.

# 18. Design Effectiveness vs Operating Effectiveness

A key concept in internal audit is the distinction between:

**Design Effectiveness**

and

**Operating Effectiveness**.

The model is:

```text
              CONTROL

                 ↓
        Is the control properly
             designed?
                 ↓
        Design Effectiveness
                 ↓
        Does it operate as
             intended?
                 ↓
        Operating Effectiveness
```

A control may be well designed but poorly executed.

Conversely, employees may consistently perform a poorly designed control.

Therefore both dimensions may need to be evaluated.

# 41. Practical Example – Cybersecurity Internal Audit

Consider an organization conducting an audit of privileged access management.

### Step 1 – Risk Identification

```text

       ↓

       ↓

```

### Step 2 – Audit Planning


```text

     ↓
Inspect Configuration
     ↓
Sample Accounts
     ↓
Review Approvals
     ↓

     ↓

```

```text

   ↓

   ↓
Condition
   ↓
Evidence missing for sampled accounts
   ↓
Risk
   ↓
Unauthorized or excessive privileged access
```

### Step 5 – Remediation

```text

       ↓
Implement automated review process
       ↓

       ↓

       ↓

```

```text

       ↓

       ↓
Yes → Close Finding

```

This demonstrates how the audit lifecycle converts a risk concern into an independently tested conclusion and, where necessary, measurable remediation.

# 46. Key GRC Takeaways

The **Internal Audit Lifecycle** can be summarized through the following principles:

1. **Start with risk.**
   Audit priorities should reflect the organization's risk profile.

2. **Define the audit clearly.**
   Objectives, scope, and criteria establish the foundation for meaningful testing.

3. **Test evidence, not assumptions.**
   Management statements should be supported by appropriate evidence.

4. **Evaluate both design and operation.**
   A control can be well designed but poorly operated.

5. **Develop evidence-based findings.**
   Findings should clearly connect criteria, condition, cause, and risk.

6. **Require accountable remediation.**
   Every significant finding should have an owner and appropriate corrective action.

7. **Validate remediation.**
   Closure should be based on sufficient evidence rather than simply management confirmation.

8. **Look for systemic themes.**
   Multiple individual findings may indicate a broader governance or control problem.

9. **Maintain independence.**
   Internal audit provides assurance; management owns risks and corrective actions.

10. **Use GRC integration.**
    Connecting risks, controls, evidence, audits, findings, and remediation creates stronger traceability.

The fundamental model is:

```text
                    RISK
                     ↓
               AUDIT PRIORITY
                     ↓
                AUDIT PLAN
                     ↓
              AUDIT ENGAGEMENT
                     ↓
                  TESTING
                     ↓
                 EVIDENCE
                     ↓
                 FINDINGS
                     ↓
               MANAGEMENT
                RESPONSE
                     ↓
                REMEDIATION
                     ↓
              VALIDATION
                     ↓
                 CLOSURE
                     ↓
                ASSURANCE
                     ↓
               IMPROVEMENT
```

A well-designed internal audit lifecycle transforms audit from a periodic compliance exercise into a **risk-based assurance mechanism that continuously strengthens governance, risk management, and internal controls**.
