## Introduction

An organization's ability to respond effectively to cybersecurity incidents begins long before an actual incident occurs. While skilled personnel and advanced security technologies are important, they are only effective when supported by clear governance, well-defined responsibilities, and documented procedures. The foundation of this preparedness is the **Incident Response Policy**, which establishes management's expectations, defines organizational responsibilities, and provides the strategic direction for responding to security incidents.

An Incident Response Policy is a high-level governance document approved by senior management that defines how the organization prepares for, detects, responds to, recovers from, and learns from information security incidents. It establishes the authority of the incident response function, identifies key stakeholders, outlines reporting and escalation requirements, and ensures that incident management activities align with business objectives, legal obligations, regulatory requirements, and organizational risk appetite.

Unlike detailed operational procedures or technical playbooks, an Incident Response Policy focuses on **what** the organization expects to achieve rather than **how** individual tasks are performed. Operational details are documented separately in incident response plans, standard operating procedures (SOPs), and playbooks, which are covered in later lessons of this chapter.

International standards and frameworks emphasize the importance of establishing an incident response policy. **ISO/IEC 27001** requires organizations to establish information security policies and supporting processes, while **ISO/IEC 27035** provides guidance for implementing an effective incident management capability. Similarly, **NIST SP 800-61 Revision 2** recommends formal incident response policies that define roles, responsibilities, authority, communication, and reporting mechanisms. These frameworks recognize that effective incident management begins with strong governance rather than technology alone.

Within Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC), the Incident Response Policy serves as the governing document that integrates cybersecurity operations with enterprise governance, risk management, legal compliance, business continuity, and executive oversight.

# What is an Incident Response Policy?

An **Incident Response Policy** is a formal, high-level document that establishes the organization's approach to managing information security incidents.

It provides strategic direction by defining:

- The purpose of incident response.
- Organizational objectives.
- Scope of the policy.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Reporting requirements.
- Governance structure.
- Compliance obligations.
- Management commitment.

The policy serves as the foundation for all incident response activities across the organization.

---

# Purpose of an Incident Response Policy

The primary purpose of the policy is to ensure that security incidents are managed consistently, efficiently, and in alignment with business objectives.

Specific objectives include:

- Protect organizational assets.
- Minimize business disruption.
- Support business continuity.
- Reduce cybersecurity risks.
- Meet legal and regulatory requirements.
- Protect customers and stakeholders.
- Preserve digital evidence.
- Enable continual improvement.

The policy establishes expectations for all personnel involved in incident management.

---

# Scope of the Policy

The policy should clearly define what it applies to.

Typical scope includes:

- Employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Third-party service providers.
- Business partners.
- Information systems.
- Cloud services.
- Corporate networks.
- Mobile devices.
- Critical business applications.

Clearly defining scope helps eliminate ambiguity during incident response.

---

# Key Components of an Incident Response Policy

A comprehensive policy typically includes the following elements.

### Purpose

Explains why the policy exists and what it intends to achieve.

---

### Scope

Defines who and what the policy applies to.

---

### Roles and Responsibilities

Identifies responsibilities for:

- Executive Management.
- Incident Response Team (CSIRT/CIRT).
- Security Operations Center (SOC).
- IT Operations.
- Legal.
- Human Resources.
- Compliance.
- Privacy Office.
- Business Units.

---

### Incident Reporting

Defines:

- How incidents are reported.
- Reporting channels.
- Escalation procedures.
- Reporting timelines.
- Incident severity levels.

---

### Incident Classification

Provides high-level guidance for categorizing incidents based on:

- Business impact.
- Data sensitivity.
- Operational disruption.
- Regulatory implications.

---

### Communication

Defines communication requirements for:

- Internal stakeholders.
- Executive management.
- Customers.
- Regulators.
- Third-party vendors.
- Law enforcement where appropriate.

---

### Compliance Requirements

Identifies applicable:

- Laws.
- Regulations.
- Industry standards.
- Contractual obligations.
- Internal governance requirements.

---

### Policy Review

Specifies:

- Review frequency.
- Approval authority.
- Version control.
- Update process.

Policies should be reviewed regularly to remain effective.

---

# Roles of Senior Management

Executive leadership is responsible for:

- Approving the policy.
- Providing governance oversight.
- Allocating resources.
- Supporting incident response capabilities.
- Promoting organizational awareness.
- Reviewing major incidents.
- Driving continual improvement.

Visible management commitment is essential for a successful incident response program.

# Benefits of an Incident Response Policy

A well-defined policy provides:

- Clear governance.
- Consistent decision-making.
- Defined accountability.
- Faster incident response.
- Improved coordination.
- Better regulatory compliance.
- Stronger executive oversight.
- Enhanced organizational resilience.

Policies reduce uncertainty during high-pressure situations.

---

# Best Practices

Organizations should:

- Obtain executive approval.
- Align the policy with business objectives.
- Reference applicable standards and regulations.
- Clearly define responsibilities.
- Establish reporting and escalation procedures.
- Communicate the policy throughout the organization.
- Review the policy annually or after significant incidents.
- Test supporting incident response procedures regularly.

A policy is only effective when it is understood, implemented, and supported across the organization.

# GRC Perspective

The Incident Response Policy is a cornerstone of Governance, Risk, and Compliance.

### Governance

Governance responsibilities include:

- Establishing incident response objectives.
- Approving organizational policies.
- Assigning accountability.
- Providing executive oversight.
- Monitoring program effectiveness.
- Supporting continual improvement.

### Risk Management

Risk management activities include:

- Identifying cyber risks.
- Defining risk tolerance.
- Prioritizing critical assets.
- Supporting risk-based decision-making.
- Improving resilience.
- Integrating incident information into enterprise risk management.

### Compliance

The policy supports compliance with:

- ISO/IEC 27001 Information Security Management System (ISMS)
- ISO/IEC 27035 Information Security Incident Management
- NIST SP 800-61 Computer Security Incident Handling Guide
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)
- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- NIS2 Directive
- Industry-specific legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements

A formally approved Incident Response Policy demonstrates management commitment, establishes organizational accountability, and provides the governance foundation for effective incident management.

# Practical Example

A global financial institution develops a new Incident Response Policy following the adoption of ISO/IEC 27001. The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) collaborates with representatives from Information Security, Legal, Compliance, Privacy, Human Resources, Business Continuity, and Executive Management to define organizational responsibilities, reporting requirements, incident classification criteria, and communication procedures. The Board of Directors formally approves the policy, making compliance mandatory across all business units. Following implementation, the organization develops detailed incident response plans, ransomware playbooks, and tabletop exercises that align with the policy. During an actual cybersecurity incident, all stakeholders understand their responsibilities, enabling a coordinated and timely response.

This example demonstrates how a well-defined Incident Response Policy provides governance, establishes accountability, and serves as the foundation for consistent and effective incident management.

## Key Takeaways

- An Incident Response Policy is a high-level governance document that defines the organization's strategic approach to managing cybersecurity incidents.
- The policy establishes objectives, scope, responsibilities, reporting requirements, communication expectations, and compliance obligations.
- Executive management is responsible for approving, supporting, and overseeing the Incident Response Policy.
- The policy serves as the foundation for incident response plans, standard operating procedures, and technical playbooks.
- From a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) perspective, the Incident Response Policy strengthens governance, supports risk management, ensures regulatory compliance, and enables a consistent and resilient organizational response to security incidents.
