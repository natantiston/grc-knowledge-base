# 19.8 Regulatory Compliance Case Studies

## Part 1 – Building a Regulatory Compliance Program

## 1. Case Study Overview

**IberiaCloud Digital Services** operates across several European markets and provides cloud, telecommunications, and digital services to enterprise and consumer customers.

As the organization expands, its regulatory obligations increase significantly.

The organization must manage requirements arising from areas such as:

* data protection;
* cybersecurity;
* digital resilience;
* telecommunications;
* outsourcing and third-party risk;
* financial and contractual obligations;
* industry-specific regulations.

Previously, compliance activities were managed independently by different departments.

Management now wants to establish a **centralized, risk-based Regulatory Compliance Program**.

The objective is not simply to "be compliant."

The objective is to establish a repeatable system that can:

> **Identify → Interpret → Assess → Implement → Monitor → Report → Improve**

regulatory requirements across the enterprise.

# 3. The Compliance Problem

The organization discovers that regulatory obligations are distributed across several departments.

### Legal

Tracks legislation.

### Privacy

Tracks data-protection requirements.

### Cybersecurity

Tracks security-related obligations.

### Procurement

Tracks contractual supplier requirements.

### Business Units

Manage industry-specific requirements.

### Internal Audit

Tests selected compliance controls.

The problem is that these activities are not centrally coordinated.

---

# 4. Initial Compliance Problems

The assessment identifies several issues.

### Problem 1

No enterprise-wide regulatory inventory.

### Problem 2

Different departments interpret requirements differently.

### Problem 3

Some regulations are mapped directly to controls while others are not.

### Problem 4

Regulatory changes are sometimes communicated late.

### Problem 5

Compliance evidence is distributed across multiple systems.

### Problem 6

Management does not have a consolidated compliance view.

---

# 5. Management Objective

The Board asks the CISO, General Counsel, and Chief Risk Officer to establish an enterprise compliance program.

The program must provide:

* regulatory visibility;
* clear accountability;
* consistent interpretation;
* control mapping;
* compliance assessments;
* evidence management;
* remediation tracking;
* executive reporting.

---

# 6. What Is a Regulatory Compliance Program?

A regulatory compliance program is a structured governance system used to ensure that an organization:

* identifies applicable obligations;
* understands requirements;
* assigns ownership;
* implements appropriate controls;
* evaluates compliance;
* manages gaps;
* monitors regulatory changes;
* reports compliance status.

A mature program connects:

**Regulation → Requirement → Risk → Control → Evidence → Assessment → Remediation → Reporting**

# 8. Step 1 – Obtain Executive Sponsorship

The organization first establishes executive sponsorship.

The executive sponsors are:

* Chief Risk Officer;
* General Counsel;
* CISO;
* Chief Compliance Officer.

Their responsibilities include:

* approving the program;
* providing resources;
* resolving conflicts;
* approving risk decisions;
* reporting significant issues to the Board.

Without executive sponsorship, regulatory compliance can become fragmented and reactive.

The organization establishes an enterprise regulatory compliance policy.

The policy defines:

* compliance objectives;
* responsibilities;
* regulatory identification;
* assessment requirements;
* evidence expectations;
* exception management;
* reporting;
* escalation;
* enforcement.

The policy establishes the governance foundation.

# 10. Step 3 – Define Compliance Scope

The program initially covers:

### Regulatory Compliance

Applicable laws and regulations.

### Contractual Compliance

Customer and supplier obligations.

### Industry Requirements

Sector-specific requirements.

### Internal Requirements

Corporate policies and standards where relevant to regulatory compliance.

The organization clearly distinguishes between:

**mandatory legal requirements**

and

**voluntary frameworks or good practices**.

# 15. Step 6 – Interpret Regulatory Requirements

A regulation is rarely implemented directly as a single control.

The compliance team breaks the regulation into individual obligations.

For example:

### Regulation

Cybersecurity regulation.

### Requirement

Organizations must implement appropriate access controls.

### Compliance Objective

Prevent unauthorized access.

### Control

Identity and access management.

This decomposition makes the requirement actionable.

* compliance gaps;
* overdue remediation;
* regulatory examinations;
* significant incidents;
* compliance risks;
* accepted risks;

# 22. Step 12 – Establish Compliance Risk Assessment

Compliance requirements are assessed according to risk.

The organization evaluates:

### Regulatory Impact

How serious would non-compliance be?

### Financial Impact

Could penalties or financial losses occur?

### Business Impact

Could operations be disrupted?

### Customer Impact

Could customers be affected?

### Reputational Impact

Could the organization's reputation be damaged?

### Enforcement Probability

How likely is regulatory enforcement?

# 25. Step 14 – Build a Common Control Framework

IberiaCloud already has:

* ISO 27001 controls;
* NIST CSF outcomes;
* internal security controls;
* privacy controls;
* operational controls.

# 26. Step 15 – Assess Existing Controls

The team determines whether mapped controls are:

* designed appropriately;
* implemented;
* operating effectively;
* supported by evidence.

A control existing on paper is not necessarily an effective compliance control.

# 28. Step 16 – Define Compliance Status

The organization establishes standard statuses.

### Compliant

Requirement fully satisfied.

### Partially Compliant

Some requirements or coverage remain incomplete.

### Non-Compliant

Required capability is absent or materially deficient.

### Not Applicable

Requirement does not apply.

### Under Assessment

Applicability or implementation is still being evaluated.

This creates consistency across the enterprise.

* gap;
* risk;
* root cause;
* owner;

* target date;
* status;

# 35. Example Compliance Gap

### Requirement

Critical incidents must be reported within the applicable regulatory timeframe.

### Finding

One business unit lacks a documented regulatory notification workflow.

### Risk

### Root Cause

No centralized regulatory incident process.

### Action

### Owner

CISO + Legal.

### Priority

High.

* compliance impact;

* management approval;

Exceptions should not become permanent substitutes for compliance.

# 48. Common Regulatory Compliance Program Mistakes

## Mistake 1 – Treating Compliance as a Legal-Only Responsibility

Legal interpretation is critical, but operational teams must implement and operate controls.

## Mistake 2 – Building a Spreadsheet Without Governance

A regulatory inventory alone does not constitute a compliance program.

## Mistake 3 – Mapping Regulations Directly to Policies

Requirements should normally be translated into specific control objectives and controls.

## Mistake 4 – Focusing Only on Documentation

Actual control operation matters more than documentation alone.

---

# 49. Additional Mistakes

## Mistake 5 – Creating Duplicate Controls

Different regulations should use common controls where practical.

## Mistake 6 – Ignoring Risk

Not all compliance issues have the same business impact.

## Mistake 7 – No Evidence Strategy

Compliance assertions need objective evidence.

## Mistake 8 – No Regulatory Change Process

A compliance program must detect changes in the regulatory environment.

# 52. Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Compliance Requires Enterprise Governance

Regulatory obligations often cross multiple business functions.

## Lesson 2 – Applicability Must Be Established First

The organization should determine what actually applies before building controls.

## Lesson 3 – Requirements Must Become Actionable

Regulatory language must be translated into requirements, control objectives and operational controls.

## Lesson 4 – Common Controls Reduce Complexity

One effective control can often satisfy multiple regulatory obligations.

---

# 53. Additional Lessons

## Lesson 5 – Evidence Is Essential

An organization must be able to demonstrate compliance, not merely claim it.

## Lesson 6 – Compliance and Risk Are Connected

A compliance gap should be evaluated according to its potential business consequences.

## Lesson 7 – Ownership Must Remain With the Business

Compliance functions coordinate and oversee, but control owners remain responsible for implementation.

## Lesson 8 – Compliance Is Continuous

Regulatory obligations, business operations and technology continuously change.

---

# 54. Final Case Study Conclusion

The IberiaCloud case demonstrates how an organization can transform fragmented regulatory activities into a structured enterprise compliance program.

The mature model is:

**Regulation → Applicability → Requirement → Risk → Control → Evidence → Assessment → Remediation → Reporting → Monitoring**

The objective is not simply to maintain a large regulatory spreadsheet.

A mature GRC professional should be able to demonstrate:

> **What regulations apply?**

> **What exactly do they require?**

> **Who owns each requirement?**

> **Which controls satisfy the requirement?**

> **What evidence demonstrates compliance?**

> **Where are the gaps?**

> **What risks do those gaps create?**

> **Who is responsible for remediation?**

> **How does management know whether the organization remains compliant?**

When integrated with **ISO 27001, NIST CSF, enterprise risk management, internal audit, privacy governance and third-party risk management**, the regulatory compliance program becomes a core component of the organization's overall **GRC operating model**.
