# 17.18 GRC Assessment and Gap Analysis Toolkit

## Part 1 – GRC Maturity Assessment Template

A GRC maturity assessment evaluates how effectively an organization governs cybersecurity, manages risk, and maintains compliance. It provides a structured view of the organization's current capabilities and identifies opportunities for improvement.

The purpose of a GRC maturity assessment is to determine:

* What capabilities currently exist
* How consistently those capabilities operate
* Whether processes are documented
* Whether responsibilities are clearly assigned
* Whether activities are measured
* Whether processes are integrated
* Where significant weaknesses exist
* What the target maturity should be
* Which improvements should be prioritized

A maturity assessment should evaluate **capability**, not simply the existence of documents.

For example, an organization may have a risk management policy but still have a weak risk management capability if risk assessments are performed inconsistently and risks are not actively monitored.

A typical GRC maturity assessment evaluates:

```text
Governance

Risk Management

Compliance Management

Policy Management

Control Management

Audit Management

Third-Party Risk Management

Issue and Remediation Management

Data Protection and Privacy

Business Continuity and Resilience

Security Awareness

Metrics and Reporting

GRC Technology and Automation
```

The assessment should begin by defining the **assessment scope**.

The scope may cover:

```text
Entire Organization

Specific Business Unit

Specific Country

Specific Regulatory Environment

Specific GRC Function

Specific Framework

Specific Technology Environment
```

For example:

```text
Assessment Scope:

Enterprise Cybersecurity GRC

Geographic Scope:

Global

Functions:

Risk, Compliance, Control Management,
Audit, Third-Party Risk
```

The assessment should identify the **objectives**.

Examples include:

```text
Evaluate Current GRC Maturity

Identify Capability Gaps

Determine Target Maturity

Support GRC Transformation

Prepare for Regulatory Requirements

Support ISO/IEC 27001 Implementation

Support NIS2 Compliance

Improve Executive Visibility

Prioritize GRC Investments
```

The assessment should define the **maturity model** before the assessment begins.

A practical five-level model is:

```text
Level 1 – Initial

Level 2 – Developing

Level 3 – Defined

Level 4 – Managed

Level 5 – Optimized
```

### Level 1 – Initial

Processes are largely informal, reactive, or inconsistent.

Characteristics include:

```text
Limited Documentation

Informal Responsibilities

Reactive Risk Management

Inconsistent Processes

Limited Metrics

Manual Activities

Limited Management Oversight
```

Example:

```text
Risk assessments are performed only
when required by an audit or customer.
```

### Level 2 – Developing

Some processes exist, but implementation is inconsistent.

Characteristics include:

```text
Basic Documentation

Some Defined Responsibilities

Partial Process Standardization

Manual Tracking

Limited Monitoring

Inconsistent Business Unit Adoption
```

Example:

```text
A corporate risk assessment methodology
exists, but different business units
apply it differently.
```

### Level 3 – Defined

Processes are formally documented and consistently implemented.

Characteristics include:

```text
Documented Processes

Defined Roles

Standard Methodologies

Formal Governance

Consistent Execution

Defined Metrics

Regular Management Review
```

Example:

```text
All business units use the same approved
enterprise risk assessment methodology.
```

### Level 4 – Managed

Processes are measured, monitored, and actively managed.

Characteristics include:

```text
Performance Metrics

Risk-Based Prioritization

Automated Monitoring

Management Dashboards

Trend Analysis

Control Performance Monitoring

Continuous Oversight
```

Example:

```text
Management receives regular risk dashboards
showing risk trends, control performance,
and overdue remediation activities.
```

### Level 5 – Optimized

GRC capabilities are continuously improved and highly integrated.

Characteristics include:

```text
Advanced Automation

Continuous Monitoring

Predictive Analytics

Integrated GRC Data

Risk-Based Decision Making

Advanced Metrics

Continuous Improvement

Strong Business Integration
```

Example:

```text
GRC data is integrated across risk,
compliance, controls, audit, and security
operations to provide near-real-time
enterprise risk visibility.
```

The maturity levels can be summarized as:

| Level | Name       | General Characteristics              |
| ----- | ---------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 1     | Initial    | Reactive and informal                |
| 2     | Developing | Partially defined                    |
| 3     | Defined    | Standardized and documented          |
| 4     | Managed    | Measured and monitored               |
| 5     | Optimized  | Integrated and continuously improved |

The assessment should define **assessment domains**.

A GRC maturity model can use the following domains:

| Domain             | Purpose                                                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Governance         | Governance structure and decision-making                      |
| Risk Management    | Identification, assessment, treatment, and monitoring of risk |
| Compliance         | Regulatory and contractual compliance                         |
| Policy Management  | Policy development, approval, and maintenance                 |
| Control Management | Control design, implementation, and monitoring                |
| Audit              | Audit planning, evidence, findings, and remediation           |
| Third-Party Risk   | Supplier and vendor security risk                             |
| Issue Management   | Findings, corrective actions, and remediation                 |
| Privacy            | Data protection and privacy governance                        |
| Resilience         | Business continuity and disaster recovery                     |
| Awareness          | Security awareness and training                               |
| Metrics            | GRC measurement and reporting                                 |
| Technology         | GRC platforms, automation, and integration                    |

Each domain should contain specific assessment criteria.

For example:

### Governance

```text
GRC governance structure exists.

Roles and responsibilities are defined.

GRC committees are established.

Decision authority is documented.

Management reporting exists.

GRC strategy is formally approved.
```

### Risk Management

```text
Risk methodology is documented.

Risk appetite is defined.

Risk assessments are performed.

Risk owners are assigned.

Risk treatment is documented.

Risk acceptance is governed.

Risks are monitored.

Risk reporting is provided to management.
```

### Compliance

```text
Compliance obligations are identified.

Regulatory requirements are mapped.

Compliance assessments are performed.

Compliance gaps are documented.

Remediation is tracked.

Compliance reporting is performed.
```

### Control Management

```text
Controls are documented.

Control owners are assigned.

Controls are mapped to requirements.

Control assessments are performed.

Evidence is collected.

Control effectiveness is evaluated.

Control deficiencies are remediated.
```

The assessment should use a consistent scoring method.

A simple scoring model is:

```text
1 = Initial

2 = Developing

3 = Defined

4 = Managed

5 = Optimized
```

Each assessment criterion receives a score.

For example:

```text
Criterion:

Risk methodology is formally documented.

Score:

4
```

Another:

```text
Criterion:

Risk monitoring is automated.

Score:

2
```

The assessor should provide evidence supporting each score.

For example:

```text
Score:

4

Evidence:

Approved Enterprise Risk Management Policy

Enterprise Risk Methodology

Risk Register

Quarterly Risk Committee Minutes
```

The assessment should not rely only on interviews.

Evidence should include:

```text
Policies

Standards

Procedures

Risk Registers

Control Registers

Audit Reports

Assessment Results

Meeting Minutes

Metrics

Dashboards

System Records

GRC Platform Data

Training Records

Compliance Reports
```

The assessment should use multiple evidence sources.

A practical assessment process is:

```text
Define Scope
      ↓
Define Assessment Criteria
      ↓
Collect Evidence
      ↓
Interview Stakeholders
      ↓
Evaluate Capabilities
      ↓
Assign Maturity Scores
      ↓
Validate Results
      ↓
Identify Gaps
      ↓
Define Target Maturity
      ↓
Prioritize Improvements
      ↓
Create Improvement Roadmap
```

Stakeholder interviews should include appropriate representatives.

Examples include:

```text
CISO

GRC Director

Risk Manager

Compliance Manager

Privacy Officer

Control Owners

IT Management

Security Operations

Internal Audit

Business Owners

Procurement

Legal
```

The assessment should distinguish between **documented capability** and **operational capability**.

For example:

```text
Documentation:

Risk Management Procedure exists.

Operational Reality:

Only 40% of business units follow it.
```

The maturity score should reflect actual operating effectiveness rather than documentation alone.

The assessment should evaluate **process consistency**.

Questions may include:

```text
Is the process documented?

Is the process approved?

Is the process consistently applied?

Is the process monitored?

Is performance measured?

Is the process reviewed?

Is the process continuously improved?
```

The assessment should evaluate **ownership**.

Questions include:

```text
Is an owner assigned?

Does the owner have appropriate authority?

Are responsibilities documented?

Are responsibilities understood?

Is accountability measured?
```

The assessment should evaluate **governance**.

Questions include:

```text
Is there executive sponsorship?

Are governance committees established?

Are decisions documented?

Are risks escalated?

Are exceptions governed?

Are management reviews performed?
```

The assessment should evaluate **technology enablement**.

Questions include:

```text
Are GRC activities automated?

Is GRC data centralized?

Are workflows automated?

Is evidence collected electronically?

Are systems integrated?

Are dashboards available?

Is continuous monitoring possible?
```

The assessment should evaluate **metrics and reporting**.

Questions include:

```text
Are KPIs defined?

Are KRIs defined?

Are metrics regularly collected?

Are trends analyzed?

Are dashboards available?

Are metrics reported to management?

Are metrics used for decision-making?
```

The assessment should evaluate **continuous improvement**.

Questions include:

```text
Are lessons learned documented?

Are processes periodically reviewed?

Are assessment results used for improvement?

Are automation opportunities identified?

Are maturity assessments repeated?

Are improvement initiatives tracked?
```

A GRC maturity assessment template can be structured as:

```text
GRC MATURITY ASSESSMENT

Organization:

________________________

Assessment Scope:

________________________

Assessment Period:

________________________

Assessment Lead:

________________________

Executive Sponsor:

________________________

Assessment Framework:

________________________

Assessment Date:

________________________
```

The assessment criteria can then be documented:

| Domain           | Assessment Criterion            | Current Score | Evidence           | Gap                           | Target Score |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------: | ------------------ | ----------------------------- | -----------: |
| Governance       | Formal GRC governance structure |             3 | Governance Charter | Limited executive integration |            4 |
| Risk             | Enterprise risk methodology     |             4 | Risk Methodology   | Minor inconsistencies         |            5 |
| Compliance       | Regulatory obligations register |             2 | Spreadsheet        | Manual and incomplete         |            4 |
| Controls         | Control ownership               |             3 | Control Register   | Some ownership gaps           |            4 |
| Audit            | Audit remediation tracking      |             3 | Audit Register     | Limited automation            |            4 |
| Third-Party Risk | Vendor risk assessments         |             2 | Vendor Records     | Inconsistent assessments      |            4 |

The **current score** represents the organization's present capability.

The **target score** represents the desired future capability.

The difference represents the maturity gap.

For example:

```text
Current:

2

Target:

4

Maturity Gap:

2 Levels
```

A maturity gap should not automatically mean that the organization must reach Level 5.

The appropriate target depends on:

```text
Business Risk

Regulatory Requirements

Business Objectives

Organizational Size

Technology

Resources

Industry Requirements

Customer Requirements

Risk Appetite
```

For example:

```text
Capability:

Regulatory Compliance

Current:

2

Target:

4
```

A regulated organization may need a higher target than a low-risk organization.

The assessment should identify **priority gaps**.

Example:

```text
HIGH PRIORITY

Regulatory obligations not fully identified.

Risk owners not consistently assigned.

Critical controls lack effective monitoring.

High-risk audit findings remain overdue.
```

Gaps should be categorized according to risk.

A practical classification is:

```text
Critical

High

Medium

Low
```

A gap should be rated using factors such as:

```text
Business Impact

Security Impact

Regulatory Impact

Likelihood

Customer Impact

Operational Impact
```

For example:

```text
Gap:

No centralized regulatory obligations register.

Regulatory Impact:

High

Operational Impact:

High

Priority:

Critical
```

The assessment should identify **strengths** as well as weaknesses.

Examples include:

```text
Strong Executive Sponsorship

Mature Risk Methodology

Effective Internal Audit

Strong Security Governance

Centralized Control Framework

Established Compliance Function
```

A maturity assessment should not become purely a gap-finding exercise.

It should show:

```text
Strengths

Weaknesses

Opportunities

Risks

Improvement Priorities
```

The assessment should calculate domain-level maturity where appropriate.

For example:

```text
Governance:

3.8

Risk Management:

4.1

Compliance:

2.7

Control Management:

3.4

Audit:

3.6

Third-Party Risk:

2.5
```

An overall maturity score may then be calculated.

For example:

```text
Overall GRC Maturity:

3.35 / 5
```

However, the overall score should not replace domain-level analysis.

An organization with an overall maturity of 3.4 could still have a critical weakness in regulatory compliance.

The assessment should therefore focus on the **maturity profile**, not only the average score.

A maturity profile may look like:

```text
Governance             3.8
Risk Management        4.1
Compliance             2.7
Control Management     3.4
Audit                  3.6
Third-Party Risk       2.5
Privacy                3.2
Metrics                2.9
Technology              2.4
```

This immediately highlights areas requiring attention.

The assessment should define **target maturity by domain**.

For example:

```text
Domain                Current    Target

Governance              3.8        4
Risk Management         4.1        4
Compliance              2.7        4
Control Management      3.4        4
Third-Party Risk        2.5        4
Technology              2.4        4
```

The organization can then prioritize the largest and most important gaps.

The assessment should create a **maturity improvement roadmap**.

For example:

```text
Current State
      ↓
Maturity Assessment
      ↓
Gap Identification
      ↓
Target Maturity
      ↓
Prioritization
      ↓
Improvement Initiatives
      ↓
Implementation Roadmap
      ↓
Future Assessment
```

Improvement initiatives may include:

```text
Develop GRC Governance Framework

Centralize Risk Management

Establish Compliance Obligations Register

Implement Control Library

Strengthen Third-Party Risk Management

Implement GRC Platform

Automate Evidence Collection

Improve Executive Reporting
```

Each improvement initiative should have:

```text
Owner

Priority

Target Date

Required Resources

Dependencies

Expected Benefit

Success Criteria
```

The assessment should define **success criteria**.

For example:

```text
Objective:

Improve Compliance Management from Level 2 to Level 4.

Success Criteria:

100% of applicable regulatory obligations identified.

100% assigned to responsible owners.

Quarterly compliance assessments completed.

High-risk gaps tracked to remediation.

Management dashboard implemented.
```

The assessment should be repeated periodically.

A typical cycle may be:

```text
Initial Assessment
       ↓
Improvement Program
       ↓
Implementation
       ↓
Measurement
       ↓
Reassessment
       ↓
Updated Maturity Score
       ↓
New Improvement Priorities
```

Annual reassessment is common, although higher-risk environments may require more frequent reviews.

The maturity assessment can also be aligned with recognized frameworks.

Examples include:

```text
ISO/IEC 27001

NIST Cybersecurity Framework

COBIT

NIST SP 800-53

CIS Controls

NIS2

DORA
```

The assessment should distinguish between **framework compliance** and **maturity**.

An organization can be compliant with a requirement while still having a relatively immature capability.

For example:

```text
Requirement:

Risk assessment must be performed.

Organization:

Risk assessment performed annually.

Maturity:

Level 3
```

A more mature organization may have:

```text
Continuous Risk Monitoring

Automated Risk Indicators

Integrated Risk Data

Risk-Based Decision Support

Continuous Improvement
```

This could represent Level 4 or Level 5 depending on the defined maturity model.

The assessment should therefore answer two different questions:

```text
Compliance:

Are we meeting the requirement?

Maturity:

How capable and sustainable is the process?
```

The GRC professional should ensure that maturity scores are **evidence-based**.

A useful scoring rule is:

```text
No Evidence
→ Level 1

Limited Evidence
→ Level 2

Documented and Consistent
→ Level 3

Measured and Managed
→ Level 4

Integrated and Continuously Improved
→ Level 5
```

The assessor should avoid assigning a high maturity score simply because senior management believes the process is mature.

Evidence should support the assessment conclusion.

A mature GRC maturity assessment should produce the following outputs:

```text
Current-State Maturity Profile

Domain Scores

Evidence Register

Identified Gaps

Strengths

Risk Priorities

Target Maturity

Maturity Gap Analysis

Improvement Initiatives

GRC Improvement Roadmap
```

The GRC professional should verify that:

```text
Assessment Scope Is Defined

Assessment Objectives Are Defined

Maturity Model Is Approved

Assessment Domains Are Defined

Assessment Criteria Are Defined

Evidence Requirements Are Defined

Stakeholders Are Identified

Interviews Are Conducted

Evidence Is Reviewed

Scores Are Evidence-Based

Current Maturity Is Documented

Target Maturity Is Defined

Maturity Gaps Are Identified

Strengths Are Documented

Weaknesses Are Documented

Risks Are Considered

Priorities Are Established

Improvement Actions Are Defined

Owners Are Assigned

Target Dates Are Defined

Success Criteria Are Defined

Results Are Reported to Management

Assessment Results Are Integrated Into the GRC Roadmap

Future Reassessment Is Planned
```

Common maturity assessment mistakes include:

```text
Scoring Without Evidence

Focusing Only on Documentation

Treating Compliance as Maturity

Using an Undefined Scoring Model

Assigning Level 5 Too Easily

Ignoring Business Risk

Ignoring Regulatory Requirements

Using the Same Target Maturity for Every Domain

Ignoring Organizational Context

Failing to Validate Results

Producing Scores Without Improvement Actions

Treating the Assessment as a One-Time Exercise
```

The most important principle is that a maturity assessment should lead to action.

The objective is not simply to say:

```text
"Our GRC maturity is 3.2."
```

The useful conclusion is:

```text
"Our GRC maturity is 3.2.

Our weakest capabilities are:

Third-Party Risk – 2.5
Compliance – 2.7
GRC Technology – 2.4

These capabilities create significant
regulatory and operational exposure.

The target maturity is Level 4.

The organization will therefore prioritize
compliance automation, third-party risk
standardization, and GRC platform
implementation during the next 12 months."
```

The key principle is:

> **A GRC maturity assessment provides an evidence-based evaluation of current governance, risk, and compliance capabilities, identifies maturity gaps, establishes realistic target states, and converts the assessment results into prioritized improvement initiatives.**
