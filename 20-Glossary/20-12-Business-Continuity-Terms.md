# 20.12 Business Continuity Terms

This section defines the core terminology associated with **Business Continuity Management (BCM), Business Continuity Management Systems (BCMS), Disaster Recovery (DR), Operational Resilience, Business Impact Analysis (BIA), Crisis Management, Incident Management, Recovery Planning, and ISO 22301**.

> **Terminology note:** The terminology below is primarily aligned with **ISO 22301**, **ISO 22313**, and common business continuity and resilience practices. Some organizations use terms such as *disaster recovery*, *business continuity*, and *operational resilience* differently, so definitions should always be interpreted within the applicable framework.

---

# Business Continuity Glossary

| Term                                                    | Definition                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Business Continuity (BC)**                            | The capability of an organization to continue delivering products and services at predefined acceptable levels following a disruptive incident.                               |
| **Business Continuity Management (BCM)**                | A holistic management process that identifies potential threats and their impacts on business operations and provides a framework for organizational resilience and response. |
| **Business Continuity Management System (BCMS)**        | A management system used to establish, implement, maintain, and continually improve business continuity capabilities.                                                         |
| **Business Continuity Plan (BCP)**                      | Documented procedures and information used to guide an organization in responding to and recovering from a disruption.                                                        |
| **Business Continuity Strategy**                        | A high-level approach defining how an organization will continue or recover critical activities following disruption.                                                         |
| **Business Continuity Framework**                       | The governance structure, policies, processes, roles, and controls supporting business continuity.                                                                            |
| **Business Continuity Policy**                          | A formally approved statement establishing the organization's commitment, objectives, principles, and responsibilities for business continuity.                               |
| **Business Continuity Objectives**                      | Defined outcomes that the BCMS is intended to achieve.                                                                                                                        |
| **Business Continuity Capability**                      | The organization's ability to prepare for, respond to, and recover from disruptive incidents.                                                                                 |
| **Organizational Resilience**                           | The ability of an organization to absorb and adapt to a changing environment and continue achieving its objectives.                                                           |
| **Operational Resilience**                              | The ability of an organization to continue delivering important business services through disruption within predefined impact tolerances.                                     |
| **Resilience**                                          | The ability to withstand, adapt to, respond to, and recover from disruption.                                                                                                  |
| **Disruption**                                          | An event that interrupts or threatens normal business operations.                                                                                                             |
| **Disruptive Incident**                                 | An occurrence that can cause interruption, loss, emergency, or crisis and potentially affect business operations.                                                             |
| **Business Interruption**                               | A temporary inability to perform normal business activities or deliver services.                                                                                              |
| **Crisis**                                              | A situation requiring significant management attention and coordinated response because it threatens organizational objectives, people, assets, reputation, or operations.    |

| **Emergency**                                           | A serious situation requiring immediate action to protect people, property, operations, or the environment.                                                                   |
| **Emergency Response**                                  | Immediate actions taken to protect people, assets, and operations following an emergency.                                                                                     |
| **Emergency Response Plan (ERP)**                       | A documented plan describing immediate response procedures for emergencies.                                                                                                   |
| **Incident**                                            | An event or occurrence that could cause or has caused disruption, loss, damage, or degradation of operations.                                                                 |
| **Incident Management**                                 | Processes for identifying, responding to, managing, and resolving incidents.                                                                                                  |

| **Incident Response Plan**                              | Documented procedures for responding to defined incidents.                                                                                                                    |

| **Critical Incident**                                   | An incident that threatens critical operations, people, safety, regulatory obligations, or organizational viability.                                                          |
| **Event**                                               | An occurrence that may be planned or unplanned and may potentially lead to an incident or disruption.                                                                         |
| **Threat**                                              | A potential cause of an unwanted incident or disruption.                                                                                                                      |
| **Hazard**                                              | A source or condition with the potential to cause harm, disruption, or loss.                                                                                                  |
| **Risk**                                                | The effect of uncertainty on objectives, considering relevant threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood, and consequences.                                                         |
| **Business Continuity Risk**                            | Risk that a disruption prevents an organization from continuing important activities or delivering products and services.                                                     |
| **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**                      | A process for analyzing business activities and the effects that disruption could have on them over time.                                                                     |
| **BIA**                                                 | Abbreviation for Business Impact Analysis.                                                                                                                                    |
| **BIA Workshop**                                        | A structured session used to gather information from business stakeholders about processes, dependencies, impacts, and recovery requirements.                                 |

| **Process Owner**                                       | Individual accountable for the performance, management, and continuity of a business process.                                                                                 |
| **Business Service Owner**                              | Individual accountable for the availability, performance, and continuity of a business service.                                                                               |

| **BCM Manager**                                         | Person responsible for managing the organization's business continuity management program.                                                                                    |

# Key Business Continuity Metrics

Several terms are especially important for GRC professionals:

### RTO — Recovery Time Objective

**Question:**

> How quickly must the service/system/process be restored?

Example:

**RTO = 4 hours**

The organization targets restoration within four hours.

---

### RPO — Recovery Point Objective

**Question:**

> How much data can we afford to lose?

Example:

**RPO = 30 minutes**

The organization should be able to recover data to a point no more than approximately 30 minutes before the disruption.

---

### MTPD — Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption

**Question:**

> How long can we tolerate the disruption before the impact becomes unacceptable?

Example:

**MTPD = 24 hours**

---

### MBCO — Minimum Business Continuity Objective

**Question:**

> At what minimum level must the service continue during disruption?

Example:

Normal operation:

**100%**

Disruption:

**40% minimum service level**

Therefore:

**MBCO = 40%**

---

# RTO vs. RPO vs. MTPD

| Term     | Main Question                                                     |    Example |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------: |
| **RTO**  | How quickly must we recover?                                      |    4 hours |
| **RPO**  | How much data can we lose?                                        | 30 minutes |
| **MTPD** | How long can disruption be tolerated?                             |   24 hours |
| **MBCO** | What minimum level must continue?                                 |        40% |
| **WRT**  | How long after technical recovery to restore business operations? |    2 hours |

A useful relationship is:

**RTO + WRT ≤ MTPD**

For example:

**RTO = 4 hours**
**WRT = 2 hours**
**MTPD = 24 hours**

The organization has a six-hour recovery requirement, which remains within the maximum tolerable disruption period.

# Business Continuity vs. Disaster Recovery

These terms are frequently confused.

| Business Continuity                                         | Disaster Recovery                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Broader discipline                                          | More technology-focused                                       |
| Covers people, processes, facilities, suppliers, technology | Primarily systems, infrastructure, applications, and data     |
| Focuses on maintaining important business activities        | Focuses on restoring technology and IT services               |
| Includes crisis management                                  | Usually operates as part of the broader continuity capability |
| Includes manual workarounds                                 | Includes backups, failover, restoration                       |
| Includes workforce continuity                               | Includes infrastructure recovery                              |
| Includes supplier continuity                                | Includes IT recovery                                          |
| Governed through BCM/BCMS                                   | Often governed through ITDR/DR program                        |

**Simple relationship:**

> **Disaster Recovery is generally a component of Business Continuity.**

---

# Business Continuity vs. Operational Resilience

| Business Continuity                                    | Operational Resilience                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Focuses heavily on preparation, response, and recovery | Focuses on maintaining important services through disruption          |
| Often uses BIA and recovery plans                      | Often uses important business services and impact tolerances          |
| Emphasizes recovery capabilities                       | Emphasizes resilience across the entire service ecosystem             |
| Strong focus on planning                               | Strong focus on continuous resilience                                 |
| ISO 22301 is a major reference                         | DORA and modern resilience frameworks strongly emphasize this concept |

A useful conceptual model is:

**Business Continuity**

→ Prepare
→ Respond
→ Recover
→ Improve

**Operational Resilience**

→ Prevent
→ Adapt
→ Absorb
→ Respond
→ Recover
→ Continue delivering important services

# Business Continuity and Third-Party Risk

Modern organizations cannot evaluate continuity only within their own infrastructure.

A critical service may depend on:

**Organization**

↓
**Cloud Provider**

↓
**Data Center**

↓
**Network Provider**

↓
**Software Provider**

↓
**Subcontractor**

↓
**Fourth Party**

Therefore:

> **Business continuity must include critical third-party and fourth-party dependencies.**

This is particularly important for:

* cloud services;
* telecommunications;
* financial services;
* healthcare;
* critical infrastructure;
* SaaS platforms;
* payment services;
* managed security services; and
* outsourced business processes.

# Business Continuity and Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity incidents can become business continuity events.

For example:

**Ransomware Attack**

→ Endpoint compromise
→ Network disruption
→ Application unavailable
→ Data unavailable
→ Business process interrupted
→ Customer service disrupted
→ Regulatory obligations triggered
→ Crisis management activated
→ Disaster recovery initiated
→ Business continuity procedures activated

Therefore:

> **Cybersecurity incident response and business continuity should be integrated rather than treated as completely separate disciplines.**

### Key GRC takeaway

The central business continuity relationship is:

> **Business Context → Risk Assessment → BIA → Recovery Requirements → Continuity Strategy → BCP/DRP → Testing → Assurance → Continual Improvement**

> **BIA tells you what matters and how quickly it must be recovered; the continuity and recovery strategy tells you how you will achieve that requirement.**
