# 20.2 Risk Management Terms

This section defines the core terminology used in **risk management, enterprise risk management (ERM), cybersecurity risk management, information security risk management, third-party risk management, and GRC programs**.

| Term                                  | Definition                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Risk**                              | The effect of uncertainty on objectives. Risk may result in positive or negative outcomes depending on the circumstances.                                             |
| **Risk Management**                   | The coordinated activities used to direct and control an organization with regard to risk.                                                                            |
| **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)**  | An integrated approach for identifying, assessing, responding to, monitoring, and reporting risks across the organization.                                            |
| **Risk Governance**                   | The structures, authorities, responsibilities, policies, and processes through which an organization oversees and directs risk management.                            |
| **Risk Management Framework (RMF)**   | A structured methodology for identifying, assessing, treating, monitoring, and communicating risk.                                                                    |
| **Risk Strategy**                     | The organization's overall approach to managing risk in support of its strategic objectives.                                                                          |
| **Risk Policy**                       | A formally approved statement establishing the organization's requirements and expectations for managing risk.                                                        |
| **Risk Appetite**                     | The amount and type of risk an organization is willing to pursue or retain in order to achieve its objectives.                                                        |
| **Risk Tolerance**                    | The acceptable level of variation around objectives or within a defined risk boundary.                                                                                |
| **Risk Capacity**                     | The maximum level of risk an organization can absorb before its viability, objectives, or ability to operate is seriously threatened.                                 |
| **Risk Threshold**                    | A defined point at which a risk requires additional management action, escalation, or intervention.                                                                   |
| **Risk Criteria**                     | The terms of reference against which the significance of risk is evaluated.                                                                                           |
| **Risk Objective**                    | A desired outcome related to managing risk and supporting organizational objectives.                                                                                  |
| **Risk Identification**               | The process of finding, recognizing, and describing risks that could affect objectives.                                                                               |
| **Risk Assessment**                   | The overall process of risk identification, risk analysis, and risk evaluation.                                                                                       |
| **Risk Analysis**                     | The process of understanding the nature of risk and determining its level.                                                                                            |
| **Risk Evaluation**                   | The process of comparing analyzed risk against established risk criteria to determine its significance and required treatment.                                        |
| **Risk Level**                        | The magnitude of a risk expressed using defined criteria, often based on likelihood and impact.                                                                       |
| **Inherent Risk**                     | The level of risk that exists before considering the effect of controls or other risk treatments.                                                                     |
| **Residual Risk**                     | The level of risk remaining after controls and other risk treatments have been applied.                                                                               |
| **Target Risk**                       | The level of risk an organization expects or intends to achieve after planned risk treatment activities are implemented.                                              |
| **Current Risk**                      | The risk level currently assessed based on existing conditions and implemented controls.                                                                              |
| **Risk Exposure**                     | The degree to which an organization, asset, process, system, or objective is exposed to a particular risk.                                                            |
| **Risk Event**                        | An occurrence or circumstance that could cause an impact on objectives.                                                                                               |
| **Threat**                            | A potential cause of an unwanted incident that may result in harm to an asset, organization, system, or objective.                                                    |
| **Vulnerability**                     | A weakness or condition that can be exploited by a threat or contribute to an adverse event.                                                                          |
| **Threat Actor**                      | An individual, group, organization, or other entity capable of intentionally causing harm or exploiting vulnerabilities.                                              |
| **Risk Scenario**                     | A structured description of a potential event, its causes, affected assets or processes, consequences, and relevant conditions.                                       |
| **Risk Cause**                        | A condition, circumstance, or factor that can contribute to the occurrence of a risk event.                                                                           |
| **Risk Consequence**                  | The outcome or effect of a risk event on organizational objectives.                                                                                                   |
| **Impact**                            | The magnitude of the consequences resulting from a risk event.                                                                                                        |
| **Likelihood**                        | The probability or frequency with which a risk event may occur.                                                                                                       |
| **Frequency**                         | The number or rate of occurrences of a risk event within a defined period.                                                                                            |
| **Probability**                       | The likelihood that a particular event or outcome will occur.                                                                                                         |
| **Risk Rating**                       | A standardized representation of risk significance, typically derived from likelihood and impact.                                                                     |
| **Risk Score**                        | A numerical or categorical value assigned to represent the assessed level of risk.                                                                                    |
| **Risk Matrix**                       | A visual tool used to evaluate and communicate risk based on dimensions such as likelihood and impact.                                                                |
| **Qualitative Risk Assessment**       | Risk assessment using descriptive categories such as low, medium, high, or critical.                                                                                  |
| **Quantitative Risk Assessment**      | Risk assessment using numerical values, financial estimates, probabilities, frequencies, or statistical models.                                                       |
| **Semi-Quantitative Risk Assessment** | An assessment approach that uses numerical scales or scoring systems without claiming precise statistical measurement.                                                |
| **Risk Modeling**                     | The use of mathematical, statistical, analytical, or simulation techniques to understand and estimate risk.                                                           |
| **Scenario Analysis**                 | The evaluation of potential outcomes under defined hypothetical circumstances.                                                                                        |
| **Sensitivity Analysis**              | Analysis of how changes in assumptions or variables affect risk results.                                                                                              |
| **Monte Carlo Simulation**            | A computational technique that uses repeated random sampling to estimate possible outcomes and their probabilities.                                                   |
| **Risk Treatment**                    | The process of selecting and implementing measures to modify risk.                                                                                                    |
| **Risk Response**                     | The action selected to address a particular risk.                                                                                                                     |
| **Risk Avoidance**                    | Changing or eliminating an activity so that the associated risk is no longer present or is substantially removed.                                                     |
| **Risk Reduction**                    | Implementing measures to reduce the likelihood and/or impact of a risk.                                                                                               |
| **Risk Mitigation**                   | Actions taken to reduce the likelihood, impact, or overall exposure associated with a risk.                                                                           |
| **Risk Transfer**                     | Shifting or sharing some portion of risk with another party, such as through insurance, contracts, or outsourcing arrangements.                                       |
| **Risk Sharing**                      | Distributing risk exposure among multiple parties through contractual, financial, operational, or other arrangements.                                                 |
| **Risk Acceptance**                   | A decision to retain a risk based on defined authority and an understanding that the risk is within acceptable boundaries or that further treatment is not justified. |
| **Risk Retention**                    | The decision to retain responsibility for a risk rather than avoiding, transferring, or otherwise treating it.                                                        |
| **Risk Elimination**                  | Removing the source or condition that creates a particular risk.                                                                                                      |
| **Risk Treatment Plan**               | A documented plan defining actions, responsibilities, resources, deadlines, and expected outcomes for treating identified risks.                                      |
| **Risk Action**                       | A specific activity undertaken to address or manage an identified risk.                                                                                               |
| **Risk Owner**                        | The individual or organizational unit accountable for managing a particular risk.                                                                                     |
| **Risk Coordinator**                  | A person responsible for coordinating risk management activities, assessments, reporting, and follow-up within a defined scope.                                       |
| **Risk Function**                     | The organizational function responsible for establishing or supporting risk management practices, methodologies, reporting, and oversight.                            |
| **Risk Register**                     | A structured record containing identified risks, their assessments, owners, treatments, status, and related information.                                              |
| **Risk Inventory**                    | A comprehensive collection of identified risks maintained across an organization, business unit, process, or domain.                                                  |
| **Risk Profile**                      | The overall description and distribution of an organization's risks at a particular point in time.                                                                    |
| **Enterprise Risk Profile**           | The consolidated view of significant risks affecting the organization and its strategic objectives.                                                                   |
| **Top Risk**                          | A risk considered sufficiently significant to require heightened executive or board attention.                                                                        |
| **Emerging Risk**                     | A new, evolving, or poorly understood risk that may have a significant future impact on organizational objectives.                                                    |
| **Risk Trend**                        | The direction in which a risk's level or exposure is changing over time.                                                                                              |
| **Risk Velocity**                     | The speed at which a risk can develop or cause significant impact after a risk event begins.                                                                          |
| **Risk Persistence**                  | The length of time a risk or its consequences may continue to affect the organization.                                                                                |
| **Risk Concentration**                | The accumulation of significant risk exposure within a particular asset, geography, supplier, technology, process, or other dependency.                               |
| **Risk Interdependency**              | The relationship in which one risk can influence, trigger, amplify, or be affected by another risk.                                                                   |
| **Risk Aggregation**                  | The process of combining multiple risks to understand their collective exposure.                                                                                      |
| **Risk Correlation**                  | The degree to which the occurrence or magnitude of different risks is related.                                                                                        |
| **Risk Dependency**                   | A relationship in which achieving an objective or managing one risk depends on another process, resource, system, supplier, or risk condition.                        |
| **Risk Exposure Limit**               | A defined maximum level of exposure permitted for a particular risk or risk category.                                                                                 |
| **Risk Escalation**                   | The process of raising a risk to a higher level of authority when it exceeds defined thresholds, tolerances, or decision rights.                                      |
| **Risk Acceptance Authority**         | The person or governance body authorized to formally accept a specified level of risk.                                                                                |
| **Risk Acceptance Statement**         | A formal record documenting the decision to accept a particular risk, including rationale, scope, authority, and conditions.                                          |
| **Risk Treatment Owner**              | The individual responsible for coordinating and completing actions associated with a risk treatment plan.                                                             |
| **Risk-Based Decision Making**        | Decision-making that explicitly considers relevant risks, uncertainties, potential impacts, and risk appetite.                                                        |
| **Risk-Based Approach**               | An approach that prioritizes resources, controls, monitoring, and decisions according to the level and significance of risk.                                          |
| **Risk Prioritization**               | The process of ranking risks according to their significance, urgency, potential impact, or other defined criteria.                                                   |
| **Risk Ranking**                      | The ordering of risks according to a defined scoring or evaluation methodology.                                                                                       |
| **Risk Aggregation Level**            | The organizational level at which individual risks are consolidated, such as project, department, business unit, enterprise, or group level.                          |
| **Risk Monitoring**                   | The continuous or periodic observation and review of risk conditions, risk levels, controls, and treatment activities.                                                |
| **Risk Review**                       | A formal examination of risk assessments, assumptions, controls, treatments, and risk status.                                                                         |
| **Risk Reporting**                    | The communication of risk information to appropriate management, governance bodies, regulators, or other stakeholders.                                                |
| **Risk Communication**                | The exchange of information and perspectives concerning risk among decision-makers and stakeholders.                                                                  |
| **Risk Escalation Criteria**          | Defined conditions requiring a risk to be reported or elevated to a higher authority.                                                                                 |
| **Risk Indicator**                    | A measure used to provide information about the status, level, trend, or potential change of a risk.                                                                  |
| **Key Risk Indicator (KRI)**          | A metric used to provide an early indication of increasing or decreasing exposure to a particular risk.                                                               |
| **Leading Indicator**                 | A measure that provides an early signal of a potential future condition or risk outcome.                                                                              |
| **Lagging Indicator**                 | A measure that reflects an outcome that has already occurred.                                                                                                         |
| **Risk Trigger**                      | A predefined condition or event indicating that a risk may be materializing or requires additional action.                                                            |
| **Risk Appetite Statement**           | A formal statement describing the organization's willingness to accept specified categories and levels of risk.                                                       |
| **Risk Culture**                      | The shared values, attitudes, behaviors, and practices influencing how people perceive, discuss, and manage risk.                                                     |
| **Risk Awareness**                    | The understanding of relevant risks, their potential consequences, and the responsibilities associated with managing them.                                            |
| **Risk Ownership Culture**            | An organizational environment in which individuals and management actively recognize and take responsibility for managing risks.                                      |
| **Risk Taxonomy**                     | A structured classification system used to organize risks into categories and subcategories.                                                                          |
| **Risk Category**                     | A defined grouping of related risks, such as strategic, operational, financial, compliance, technology, or cybersecurity risk.                                        |
| **Risk Universe**                     | The complete set of risks that could potentially affect an organization's objectives.                                                                                 |
| **Risk Ontology**                     | A formal representation of relationships among risk concepts, entities, attributes, causes, events, consequences, and controls.                                       |
| **Risk Methodology**                  | The defined approach, criteria, procedures, scoring methods, and techniques used to perform risk management activities.                                               |
| **Risk Assessment Methodology**       | The documented method used to identify, analyze, evaluate, score, and prioritize risks.                                                                               |
| **Risk Criteria**                     | The benchmarks and rules used to determine whether a risk is acceptable, tolerable, significant, or requires treatment.                                               |
| **Risk Appetite Framework**           | The governance structure, policies, processes, limits, metrics, and responsibilities used to establish and manage organizational risk appetite.                       |
| **Risk-Based Resource Allocation**    | The allocation of people, funding, technology, and other resources according to assessed risk and organizational priorities.                                          |
| **Risk Optimization**                 | The process of modifying risk to achieve an appropriate balance between potential benefits, opportunities, costs, and adverse consequences.                           |
| **Opportunity Risk**                  | Uncertainty that may result in a beneficial outcome or opportunity if appropriately managed.                                                                          |
| **Downside Risk**                     | Risk associated with a potential negative effect on organizational objectives.                                                                                        |
| **Strategic Risk**                    | Risk arising from decisions, strategies, external conditions, or changes that may affect the organization's strategic objectives.                                     |
| **Operational Risk**                  | Risk arising from inadequate or failed processes, people, systems, or external events.                                                                                |
| **Financial Risk**                    | Risk that may negatively affect financial performance, liquidity, capital, revenue, costs, or financial stability.                                                    |
| **Compliance Risk**                   | Risk of legal, regulatory, contractual, or internal-policy consequences resulting from failure to meet applicable requirements.                                       |
| **Legal Risk**                        | Risk arising from legal disputes, laws, regulations, contractual obligations, or other legal matters.                                                                 |
| **Reputational Risk**                 | Risk that an event or organizational action negatively affects stakeholder trust, reputation, or public perception.                                                   |
| **Technology Risk**                   | Risk arising from technology failures, weaknesses, dependencies, changes, or misuse that could affect organizational objectives.                                      |
| **Cybersecurity Risk**                | Risk arising from the compromise of information, systems, networks, applications, or digital services through cyber threats or weaknesses.                            |
| **Information Security Risk**         | Risk associated with threats or vulnerabilities that could compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information.                                 |
| **Privacy Risk**                      | Risk that personal data processing may negatively affect individuals, privacy rights, or regulatory compliance.                                                       |
| **Third-Party Risk**                  | Risk arising from an organization's dependence on external suppliers, service providers, partners, contractors, or other third parties.                               |
| **Supply Chain Risk**                 | Risk arising from dependencies, vulnerabilities, disruptions, or compromises within an organization's supply chain.                                                   |
| **Cloud Risk**                        | Risk associated with the use, management, configuration, dependency, or security of cloud computing services.                                                         |
| **AI Risk**                           | Risk arising from the development, deployment, use, or misuse of artificial intelligence systems.                                                                     |
| **Model Risk**                        | Risk that a model is incorrectly designed, implemented, interpreted, or used, resulting in inaccurate or harmful decisions.                                           |
| **AI Safety Risk**                    | Risk that an AI system may cause unintended physical, societal, operational, or other significant harm.                                                               |
| **Risk Scenario Analysis**            | The structured evaluation of specific potential events and their effects to determine risk exposure and appropriate responses.                                        |
| **Stress Testing**                    | The evaluation of how an organization, system, process, or portfolio performs under severe but plausible adverse conditions.                                          |
| **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**    | A structured process for determining the potential effects of disruption on business activities and identifying recovery requirements.                                |
| **Single Point of Failure (SPOF)**    | A component, dependency, person, process, or resource whose failure could cause significant disruption because there is insufficient redundancy.                      |
| **Risk Event Frequency**              | The estimated or observed rate at which a particular risk event occurs.                                                                                               |
| **Loss Event**                        | An actual occurrence that produces a measurable negative consequence or loss.                                                                                         |
| **Near Miss**                         | An event that could have resulted in loss, harm, or disruption but did not produce the expected adverse outcome.                                                      |
| **Lessons Learned**                   | Knowledge derived from experience that can be used to improve future risk identification, assessment, treatment, or response.                                         |
| **Risk Treatment Effectiveness**      | The degree to which implemented risk treatment measures successfully reduce or otherwise modify the intended risk.                                                    |
| **Control Effectiveness**             | The degree to which a control operates as designed and achieves its intended risk-reduction objective.                                                                |
| **Risk Reduction Effectiveness**      | The measurable degree to which implemented controls or treatments reduce risk exposure.                                                                               |
| **Residual Risk Acceptance**          | Formal approval to retain the remaining risk after risk treatment has been implemented.                                                                               |
| **Risk Closure**                      | The formal completion of risk treatment and associated monitoring when defined closure criteria have been satisfied.                                                  |

## Key Risk Management Distinctions

### 1. Inherent Risk vs. Residual Risk

**Inherent risk** represents the exposure before considering controls or treatments.

**Residual risk** represents the exposure remaining after existing controls and treatments are considered.

A simplified relationship is:

**Inherent Risk → Controls/Treatment → Residual Risk**

For example:

> A critical internet-facing application has a high inherent cybersecurity risk. MFA, network segmentation, vulnerability management, secure configuration, and monitoring reduce the exposure. The remaining exposure is the residual risk.

---

### 2. Risk Appetite vs. Risk Tolerance vs. Risk Capacity

These three concepts operate at different levels:

**Risk Capacity**
→ How much risk the organization *can withstand*.

**Risk Appetite**
→ How much risk the organization *is willing to take*.

**Risk Tolerance**
→ How much variation from the desired level is *acceptable*.

A useful conceptual relationship is:

**Risk Capacity > Risk Appetite > Risk Tolerance boundaries**

The exact numerical relationship depends on the organization's methodology.

---

### 3. Risk Owner vs. Control Owner

A **Risk Owner** is accountable for managing the risk.

A **Control Owner** is accountable for ensuring that a particular control is appropriately designed, implemented, maintained, and monitored.

For example:

* CISO → cybersecurity risk owner
* IAM Manager → MFA control owner
* Security Operations Manager → monitoring control owner

Multiple controls may therefore support a single risk.

---

### 4. Threat vs. Vulnerability vs. Risk

These concepts should not be confused:

**Threat**
→ Something capable of causing harm.

**Vulnerability**
→ A weakness that can be exploited.

**Risk**
→ The potential effect of uncertainty on objectives.

Example:

> **Threat:** ransomware actor
> **Vulnerability:** unpatched server
> **Event:** ransomware compromises server
> **Impact:** operational disruption and data loss
> **Risk:** potential business impact resulting from ransomware exploiting the vulnerability.

---

### 5. Risk Identification → Assessment → Treatment → Monitoring

A mature risk management lifecycle can generally be represented as:

**Identify → Analyze → Evaluate → Treat → Monitor → Report → Improve**

This lifecycle should operate continuously rather than as a one-time annual exercise.

---

## Risk Management in GRC

Risk management is one of the three core components of **GRC**:

**Governance**
→ Establishes direction, accountability, authority, and oversight.

**Risk Management**
→ Identifies and manages uncertainty that could affect objectives.

**Compliance**
→ Ensures applicable requirements are identified, implemented, monitored, and demonstrated.

In a mature GRC program, these activities are integrated rather than operating as independent silos.

For example:

**Regulation → Requirement → Risk → Control → Evidence → Testing → Finding → Remediation → Governance Reporting**

This chain is particularly important in **cybersecurity GRC**, where regulatory requirements and organizational objectives must be translated into measurable risks and effective controls.
