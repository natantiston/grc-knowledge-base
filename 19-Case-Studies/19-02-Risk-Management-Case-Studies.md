# 19.2 Risk Management Case Studies

## Part 1 – Enterprise Risk Identification and Assessment

## 1. Case Study Overview

This case study demonstrates how an organization establishes an **enterprise risk identification and assessment process** across business, operational, cybersecurity, compliance, financial, third-party, and strategic risk domains.

The objective is to show how a GRC professional transforms scattered risk observations into a **structured enterprise risk register**, applies consistent assessment criteria, and produces information that executives can use for decision-making.

The organization in this case is fictional but reflects a realistic multinational enterprise.

---

### Company

# 3. Business Situation

NovaTel has traditionally managed risk within individual departments.

For example:

* Finance manages financial risks.
* IT manages technology risks.
* Cybersecurity manages security risks.
* Legal manages legal risks.
* Procurement manages supplier risks.
* Operations manages operational risks.

Each function uses slightly different methodologies.

Management therefore lacks a consolidated view of enterprise risk.

---

# 4. Management Concern

The CEO asks the Chief Risk Officer:

> **"What are our ten most important enterprise risks, and which of them could prevent us from achieving our strategic objectives?"**

The existing risk registers cannot answer the question reliably.

Different departments use:

* different scoring scales;
* different risk definitions;
* different assessment periods;
* different impact categories;
* different ownership models.

The organization decides to establish a common enterprise risk assessment methodology.

---

# 5. Assessment Objectives

The program has five objectives:

1. Identify significant enterprise risks.
2. Establish a common risk taxonomy.
3. Assess risks consistently.
4. Determine risk exposure relative to risk appetite.
5. Prioritize risk treatment and executive attention.

The process must cover both **traditional enterprise risks and cybersecurity/GRC risks**.

# 7. Risk Categories

Examples:

Examples:

* service interruption;

Risks involving technology platforms.

Examples:

* system failure;

Examples:

Examples:

# 10. Risk Statement Construction

NovaTel introduces a standard risk statement format:

> **Because of [cause], [risk event] may occur, resulting in [impact].**

Example:

> Because of excessive dependency on a single cloud provider, a major cloud service outage may occur, resulting in prolonged disruption of enterprise digital services and financial loss.

This produces more useful risk statements than simply writing:

> "Cloud outage."

# 13. Risk Assessment Framework

NovaTel uses two primary dimensions:

### Likelihood

How likely is the risk event to occur?

### Impact

How severe would the consequences be?

The basic model is:

**Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

The organization uses a 1–5 scale.

---

# 14. Likelihood Scale

| Score | Description    |
| ----: | -------------- |
|     1 | Rare           |
|     2 | Unlikely       |
|     3 | Possible       |
|     4 | Likely         |
|     5 | Almost Certain |

The definitions are supported by quantitative ranges where appropriate.

For example:

* Rare: less than 5% probability;
* Unlikely: 5–20%;
* Possible: 21–50%;
* Likely: 51–80%;
* Almost Certain: above 80%.

These ranges are illustrative and should be calibrated to the organization's risk environment.

---

# 17. Inherent Risk

The first assessment is **inherent risk**.

Inherent risk represents exposure before considering existing controls.

Example:

```text id="uy6g8d"
Cloud Dependency
      |
      v
Major Cloud Outage
      |
      v
High Business Impact
      |
      v
INHERENT RISK
```

The organization initially assesses the risk without assuming that controls will prevent the event.

---

# 18. Control Assessment

NovaTel then identifies existing controls.

For the cloud outage risk:

* multi-region architecture;
* disaster recovery;
* backup connectivity;
* service-level agreements;
* cloud monitoring;
* incident response;
* business continuity planning.

The effectiveness of these controls is then considered.

---

# 19. Residual Risk

After considering existing controls, NovaTel calculates residual risk.

```text id="5du4sk"
Inherent Risk
      |
      v
Existing Controls
      |
      v
Control Effectiveness
      |
      v
Residual Risk
```

Example:

| Risk             | Inherent | Residual |
| ---------------- | -------: | -------: |
| Cloud outage     |       20 |       12 |
| Ransomware       |       20 |       15 |
| Supplier failure |       16 |       10 |
| Data breach      |       20 |       12 |

Residual risk is what management ultimately needs to understand.

# 23. Risk Appetite

Risk assessment becomes meaningful only when compared with risk appetite.

NovaTel establishes:

> **"The organization has very low tolerance for risks that could cause prolonged interruption of critical customer services or significant compromise of regulated customer information."**

Therefore, a residual risk of 15 may exceed appetite.

---

Risks above the defined appetite require management attention.

# 25. Risk Treatment Decision

For risks exceeding appetite, management chooses a treatment strategy.

The four classic approaches are:

### Avoid

Stop the activity creating the risk.

### Reduce

Implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact.

### Transfer

Transfer part of the risk through mechanisms such as insurance or contractual arrangements.

### Accept

Explicitly accept the residual risk.

---

# 26. Example Treatment

For the ransomware risk:


The CISO becomes responsible for coordinating treatment actions, while the designated risk owner remains accountable.

However, the combined dependency creates significant **concentration risk**.

# 37. Risk Assessment Quality Review

The GRC team performs quality checks.

It asks:

* Is the risk statement clear?
* Is the correct owner assigned?
* Is likelihood supported by evidence?
* Is impact supported by business information?
* Are controls correctly considered?
* Is residual risk realistic?
* Does the rating align with risk appetite?

This prevents arbitrary scoring.

---

# 38. Common Assessment Errors

NovaTel identifies several common problems.

### Error 1 – Risk = Control Failure

A failed control is not automatically the enterprise risk.

### Error 2 – Risk = Threat

"Ransomware" alone is not a complete risk statement.

### Error 3 – Risk = Impact

"Revenue loss" describes an impact, not necessarily the risk.

### Error 4 – Inflated Ratings

Some managers rate every risk as critical.

### Error 5 – Ignoring Controls

Others underestimate residual risk by failing to assess control effectiveness.

---

# 39. Improved Risk Statement

Instead of:

> "Ransomware."

NovaTel documents:

> **Because of exploitation of vulnerable internet-facing systems and compromised credentials, ransomware may disrupt critical business services, resulting in customer impact, financial loss, regulatory exposure, and reputational damage.**

This provides:

* cause;
* event;
* consequence.

# 41. Case Study Results

After implementing the common methodology, NovaTel achieves:

* 100% of significant risks assigned to owners;
* one enterprise risk taxonomy;
* one common scoring methodology;
* consistent risk reporting;
* improved risk aggregation;
* better executive visibility;
* clearer treatment accountability.

The number of reported risks initially increases because previously undocumented risks become visible.

This is not necessarily a negative outcome.

It demonstrates improved **risk visibility**.

---

# 42. Key Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Start with Objectives

Risk identification should be connected to what the organization is trying to achieve.

## Lesson 2 – Use a Common Language

A common taxonomy and methodology make enterprise aggregation possible.

## Lesson 3 – Separate Inherent and Residual Risk

Management needs to understand both the original exposure and the remaining exposure after controls.

## Lesson 4 – Risk Appetite Drives Decisions

A risk score by itself does not determine what management should do.

## Lesson 5 – Risk Is Dynamic

Risk assessments must change when business, technology, threat, or regulatory conditions change.

## Lesson 6 – Look for Interdependencies

Individual risks may appear acceptable while their combined exposure is significant.

## Case Study Conclusion

The NovaTel case demonstrates that enterprise risk assessment is not simply a process of assigning numbers to risks.

A mature GRC organization connects **business objectives, risk identification, risk assessment, controls, risk appetite, treatment, monitoring, and executive decision-making**.

The central principle is:

> **Identify risk in the context of business objectives, assess it consistently, understand the effect of controls, compare residual exposure with risk appetite, and continuously reassess as conditions change.**

This approach transforms risk registers from static documentation into an **active management tool for enterprise decision-making**.
