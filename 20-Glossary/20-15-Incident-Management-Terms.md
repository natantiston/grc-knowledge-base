# 20.15 Incident Management Terms

This section defines the core terminology associated with **Incident Management, Cybersecurity Incident Response, Security Operations, Incident Handling, Crisis Management, Breach Management, Digital Forensics, Recovery, and GRC Incident Governance**.

> **Terminology note:** This glossary is designed for the GRC knowledge base and aligns broadly with concepts found in **ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27035, NIST incident-response guidance, ITIL practices, business continuity, privacy breach management, and modern Security Operations (SecOps).**

---

# Incident Management Glossary

| Term                                                 | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Incident**                                         | An event or occurrence that may disrupt operations, compromise security, violate policy, or require investigation and response.                                                                                               |
| **Security Incident**                                | An event that adversely affects or threatens the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or information systems.                                                                                           |
| **Cybersecurity Incident**                           | An occurrence that actually or potentially jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or information systems or constitutes a violation or imminent threat of violation of security policies. |
| **Information Security Incident**                    | An event indicating a possible breach of information security or failure of security controls.                                                                                                                                |
| **IT Incident**                                      | An unplanned interruption or reduction in quality of an IT service.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Operational Incident**                             | Event that disrupts or threatens normal business or operational activities.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Privacy Incident**                                 | Event that may compromise personal data, privacy, or data-subject rights.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Personal Data Breach**                             | A breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data.                                                                                 |
| **Data Breach**                                      | Unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, loss, destruction, or compromise of data.                                                                                                                                        |
| **Major Incident**                                   | Incident with significant actual or potential impact requiring elevated management attention and coordinated response.                                                                                                        |
| **Critical Incident**                                | Incident presenting severe or potentially catastrophic impact to the organization, its people, customers, operations, information, or reputation.                                                                             |
| **Minor Incident**                                   | Incident with limited impact that can normally be resolved using standard operational procedures.                                                                                                                             |
| **Security Event**                                   | Observable occurrence in a system, service, network, or environment that may be relevant to security.                                                                                                                         |
| **Alert**                                            | Notification generated by a security or monitoring system indicating potentially suspicious or abnormal activity.                                                                                                             |
| **Event**                                            | Observable occurrence within a system, application, network, process, or business environment.                                                                                                                                |
| **Threat**                                           | Potential cause of an unwanted incident that may result in harm to an organization or its assets.                                                                                                                             |
| **Threat Event**                                     | Observable occurrence associated with a potential or actual threat.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Indicator of Compromise (IoC)**                    | Evidence suggesting that a system, account, network, or environment may have been compromised.                                                                                                                                |
| **Indicator of Attack (IoA)**                        | Evidence of behaviors or activities associated with an ongoing or attempted attack.                                                                                                                                           |
| **Security Signal**                                  | Data or observation suggesting potentially malicious, abnormal, or security-relevant activity.                                                                                                                                |
| **Anomaly**                                          | Activity or behavior that deviates from an established baseline or expected pattern.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Incident Management**                              | Structured process for identifying, assessing, responding to, resolving, recovering from, and learning from incidents.                                                                                                        |
| **Incident Response (IR)**                           | Coordinated process for detecting, analyzing, containing, eradicating, and recovering from security incidents.                                                                                                                |
| **Cyber Incident Response**                          | Incident-response activities specifically addressing cybersecurity threats and incidents.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Incident Handling**                                | Practical activities performed to identify, analyze, contain, eradicate, and recover from incidents.                                                                                                                          |
| **Incident Response Lifecycle**                      | Structured sequence of activities used to manage an incident from preparation and detection through recovery and lessons learned.                                                                                             |
| **Incident Response Plan (IRP)**                     | Documented plan defining how an organization responds to cybersecurity or security incidents.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Incident Response Policy**                         | Policy establishing organizational requirements, responsibilities, authority, and expectations for incident response.                                                                                                         |
| **Incident Response Procedure**                      | Detailed instructions for performing specific incident-response activities.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Incident Playbook**                                | Prescriptive response procedure for a specific incident scenario or threat type.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Runbook**                                          | Detailed operational instructions for performing a repeatable technical or operational task.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Incident Response Framework**                      | Structured model used to organize incident-response capabilities and activities.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Incident Response Team (IRT)**                     | Team responsible for coordinating and performing incident-response activities.                                                                                                                                                |
| **Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT)** | Specialized team responsible for coordinating and responding to cybersecurity incidents.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT)**          | Organization or team responsible for responding to significant cybersecurity incidents and coordinating security response.                                                                                                    |
| **Security Operations Center (SOC)**                 | Function or facility responsible for continuous security monitoring, detection, investigation, and response.                                                                                                                  |
| **SOC Analyst**                                      | Security professional responsible for monitoring, analyzing, investigating, and escalating security events and incidents.                                                                                                     |
| **Incident Commander (IC)**                          | Person responsible for directing and coordinating incident-response activities.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Incident Coordinator**                             | Person responsible for coordinating activities, communications, stakeholders, and response tasks during an incident.                                                                                                          |
| **Technical Lead**                                   | Person responsible for directing technical investigation, containment, eradication, or recovery activities.                                                                                                                   |
| **Business Owner**                                   | Person accountable for the affected business service, process, application, or asset.                                                                                                                                         |
| **System Owner**                                     | Person accountable for the operation, security, and management of an information system.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Asset Owner**                                      | Person accountable for a particular information asset or technology resource.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Incident Owner**                                   | Person accountable for managing an incident through resolution or formal closure.                                                                                                                                             |
| **Incident Manager**                                 | Person responsible for managing incident processes, coordination, escalation, communications, and resolution.                                                                                                                 |
| **Crisis Management Team (CMT)**                     | Group responsible for managing major organizational crises and strategic consequences.                                                                                                                                        |
| **Crisis Manager**                                   | Individual responsible for coordinating organizational response to a major crisis.                                                                                                                                            |
| **Executive Sponsor**                                | Senior leader providing authority, resources, and executive oversight during significant incidents.                                                                                                                           |
| **Stakeholder**                                      | Person, group, organization, customer, regulator, supplier, or other party affected by or involved in an incident.                                                                                                            |
| **First Responder**                                  | Person who initially identifies, receives, investigates, or responds to an incident.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Incident Reporter**                                | Person or system that reports a suspected or actual incident.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Incident Triage**                                  | Initial assessment used to determine whether an event constitutes an incident and how urgently it must be handled.                                                                                                            |
| **Incident Validation**                              | Process of confirming whether a suspected event is a genuine incident.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **False Positive**                                   | Alert or event incorrectly identified as malicious or requiring incident response.                                                                                                                                            |
| **True Positive**                                    | Alert or detection correctly identifying malicious, suspicious, or security-relevant activity.                                                                                                                                |
| **False Negative**                                   | Malicious or security-relevant activity that was not detected.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **True Negative**                                    | Activity correctly identified as benign.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Incident Classification**                          | Categorization of an incident according to type, severity, impact, cause, affected asset, or other criteria.                                                                                                                  |
| **Incident Categorization**                          | Assignment of an incident to a defined category for reporting, routing, analysis, and trend management.                                                                                                                       |
| **Incident Severity**                                | Degree of seriousness associated with an incident based on factors such as impact, scope, urgency, and business consequences.                                                                                                 |
| **Incident Priority**                                | Relative order in which an incident should be addressed based on impact and urgency.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Incident Impact**                                  | Actual or potential effect of an incident on business operations, people, information, systems, customers, or reputation.                                                                                                     |
| **Incident Urgency**                                 | Speed with which an incident must be addressed to prevent further harm or business impact.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Incident Risk Rating**                             | Overall assessment of incident risk based on likelihood, impact, scope, and other relevant factors.                                                                                                                           |
| **Incident Escalation**                              | Process of transferring an incident to a higher level of authority, expertise, or management.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Functional Escalation**                            | Escalation to a team or specialist with additional technical or functional expertise.                                                                                                                                         |
| **Hierarchical Escalation**                          | Escalation to a higher management or decision-making level.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Executive Escalation**                             | Escalation of a significant incident to senior management or executives.                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Regulatory Escalation**                            | Escalation involving regulatory authorities or legally required notifications.                                                                                                                                                |
| **Incident Notification**                            | Communication informing relevant parties about an incident.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Incident Communication**                           | Internal or external communication associated with an incident.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Incident Communications Plan**                     | Defined approach for communicating with employees, management, customers, regulators, suppliers, media, and other stakeholders.                                                                                               |
| **Crisis Communication**                             | Strategic communication performed during a major incident or crisis to maintain accurate information and stakeholder confidence.                                                                                              |
| **Communication Lead**                               | Person responsible for coordinating incident or crisis communications.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Incident Ticket**                                  | Formal record used to document and manage an incident.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Incident Record**                                  | Structured record containing information about an incident, including detection, analysis, actions, evidence, communications, and resolution.                                                                                 |
| **Incident Register**                                | Central repository of incidents and their status, classification, ownership, and outcomes.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Incident Log**                                     | Chronological record of events, decisions, actions, and observations during an incident.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Incident Timeline**                                | Chronological reconstruction of events and response actions.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Incident Documentation**                           | Records generated throughout the incident lifecycle.                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Incident Evidence**                                | Information collected to support investigation, analysis, attribution, decision-making, or legal/regulatory proceedings.                                                                                                      |
| **Evidence Preservation**                            | Measures taken to prevent alteration, destruction, or contamination of incident evidence.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Chain of Custody**                                 | Documented history showing the collection, handling, transfer, storage, and disposition of evidence.                                                                                                                          |
| **Digital Evidence**                                 | Information stored or transmitted in digital form that may support an investigation.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Digital Forensics**                                | Process of collecting, preserving, examining, analyzing, and reporting digital evidence.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Forensic Image**                                   | Bit-for-bit or appropriately preserved copy of digital storage used for forensic examination.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Forensic Analysis**                                | Examination of digital evidence to determine what occurred, how it occurred, and potentially who or what was involved.                                                                                                        |
| **Volatile Data**                                    | Information that can disappear when a system is shut down or changes state, such as memory contents and active network connections.                                                                                           |
| **Memory Forensics**                                 | Analysis of volatile memory to identify processes, credentials, malware, connections, and other evidence.                                                                                                                     |
| **Malware Analysis**                                 | Examination of malicious software to understand its behavior, capabilities, indicators, and impact.                                                                                                                           |
| **Threat Hunting**                                   | Proactive search for malicious or suspicious activity that may have bypassed existing security controls.                                                                                                                      |
| **Detection Engineering**                            | Development, testing, tuning, and maintenance of security detections.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Security Analytics**                               | Analysis of security data to identify threats, anomalies, patterns, and potential incidents.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Incident Investigation**                           | Structured examination of an incident to determine what happened, how it happened, its impact, and contributing factors.                                                                                                      |
| **Incident Analysis**                                | Evaluation of incident evidence and information to understand scope, cause, impact, and appropriate response.                                                                                                                 |
| **Root Cause Analysis (RCA)**                        | Structured analysis used to identify the underlying causes of an incident.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Contributing Cause**                               | Factor that contributed to the occurrence or impact of an incident but may not represent its fundamental root cause.                                                                                                          |
| **Root Cause**                                       | Fundamental underlying reason that allowed an incident to occur or recur.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Attack Vector**                                    | Method or pathway used by an attacker to gain access, compromise systems, or cause harm.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Attack Surface**                                   | Total set of exposed systems, applications, identities, interfaces, services, and other potential entry points that attackers could target.                                                                                   |
| **Threat Actor**                                     | Individual, group, organization, or other entity capable of conducting malicious activity.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Tactics, Techniques and Procedures (TTPs)**        | Behaviors and methods used by threat actors to conduct attacks.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Compromise**                                       | Unauthorized control, access, alteration, or exposure of a system, account, application, or data.                                                                                                                             |
| **Initial Access**                                   | Stage of an attack in which an adversary first gains access to a target environment.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Persistence**                                      | Techniques used by attackers to maintain access to a compromised environment.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Privilege Escalation**                             | Process of obtaining higher levels of access or privileges than initially available.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Lateral Movement**                                 | Movement by an attacker from one system, account, or network segment to another after initial compromise.                                                                                                                     |
| **Command and Control (C2)**                         | Communication channel through which an attacker controls compromised systems.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Exfiltration**                                     | Unauthorized transfer of data from an organization's environment.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Impact**                                           | Stage or consequence of an attack in which the adversary disrupts, damages, manipulates, or destroys systems or data.                                                                                                         |
| **Containment**                                      | Actions taken to limit the spread, impact, or progression of an incident.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Short-Term Containment**                           | Immediate actions designed to stop or limit an incident while preserving longer-term response options.                                                                                                                        |
| **Long-Term Containment**                            | Measures used to maintain control of an incident while a permanent solution is developed.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Isolation**                                        | Separating a compromised system, account, network segment, or workload to prevent further harm.                                                                                                                               |
| **Network Isolation**                                | Restricting network connectivity of a compromised or high-risk system.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Account Disablement**                              | Temporarily or permanently disabling a compromised or suspicious account.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Blocking**                                         | Preventing malicious IP addresses, domains, hashes, accounts, traffic, or other indicators from accessing resources.                                                                                                          |
| **Eradication**                                      | Removing the cause or components of an incident from the environment.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Malware Removal**                                  | Removal of malicious software and associated persistence mechanisms.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Credential Reset**                                 | Changing or revoking credentials following suspected compromise.                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Vulnerability Remediation**                        | Correcting vulnerabilities that contributed to or enabled an incident.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Patch Remediation**                                | Applying security updates or patches to address vulnerabilities.                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Recovery**                                         | Restoring systems, services, data, and operations to an acceptable state following an incident.                                                                                                                               |
| **System Recovery**                                  | Restoration of affected systems to a secure and operational state.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Service Recovery**                                 | Restoration of affected IT or business services.                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Data Recovery**                                    | Restoration of lost, corrupted, encrypted, or compromised data.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Business Recovery**                                | Restoration of business operations following an incident or disruption.                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Recovery Point Objective (RPO)**                   | Maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Recovery Time Objective (RTO)**                    | Target time within which a system, service, or process should be restored.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption (MTPD)**    | Maximum period an activity or service can be unavailable before unacceptable consequences arise.                                                                                                                              |
| **Workaround**                                       | Temporary method used to restore or maintain service while a permanent solution is being developed.                                                                                                                           |
| **Restoration**                                      | Returning a system, service, or process to operational status.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Validation**                                       | Confirmation that recovered systems and services operate securely and correctly.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Return to Normal Operations**                      | Transition from incident response to standard operational processes.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Post-Incident Review (PIR)**                       | Structured review conducted after an incident to evaluate response effectiveness and identify improvements.                                                                                                                   |
| **Lessons Learned**                                  | Knowledge derived from an incident to improve controls, processes, preparedness, and future response.                                                                                                                         |
| **Corrective Action**                                | Action taken to eliminate the cause of an identified problem or prevent recurrence.                                                                                                                                           |
| **Preventive Action**                                | Action designed to reduce the likelihood of future incidents or failures.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Remediation**                                      | Corrective activity used to address identified vulnerabilities, weaknesses, or causes.                                                                                                                                        |
| **Continuous Improvement**                           | Ongoing improvement of incident-response capabilities based on lessons learned, testing, metrics, and changing threats.                                                                                                       |
| **Incident Closure**                                 | Formal completion of incident-response activities after required actions, documentation, validation, and approvals have been completed.                                                                                       |
| **Incident Reopening**                               | Reopening a closed incident because new evidence, recurrence, unresolved issues, or additional impact has been identified.                                                                                                    |
| **Incident Recurrence**                              | Repetition of the same or substantially similar incident.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Recurring Incident**                               | Incident pattern occurring repeatedly due to unresolved or systemic causes.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Problem Management**                               | Process for identifying and addressing underlying causes of recurring incidents.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Known Error**                                      | Problem with a documented root cause or workaround that has been identified but may not yet have been permanently resolved.                                                                                                   |
| **Known Issue**                                      | Identified condition with documented characteristics, impact, or workaround.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Corrective Action Plan (CAP)**                     | Documented plan defining actions, owners, deadlines, priorities, and evidence required to remediate identified issues.                                                                                                        |
| **Incident SLA**                                     | Service-level commitment defining expected response or resolution times for incidents.                                                                                                                                        |
| **Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)**                       | Average time between occurrence of an incident and its detection.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Mean Time to Acknowledge (MTTA)**                  | Average time between alert or incident notification and acknowledgment by the responsible team.                                                                                                                               |
| **Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)**                      | Time metric measuring how quickly a team responds to an incident.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Mean Time to Contain (MTTC)**                      | Average time required to contain an incident.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Mean Time to Recover (MTTR)**                      | Average time required to restore affected services or systems after an incident.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Mean Time to Remediate (MTTR)**                    | Average time required to fully remediate an identified issue or vulnerability.                                                                                                                                                |
| **Time to Detect (TTD)**                             | Time required to detect an incident after it occurs.                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Time to Respond (TTR)**                            | Time required to begin or complete appropriate response activities, depending on organizational definition.                                                                                                                   |
| **Time to Contain (TTC)**                            | Time required to prevent an incident from continuing or spreading.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Time to Recover (TTR)**                            | Time required to restore affected services or systems.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Incident Volume**                                  | Number of incidents recorded during a defined period.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Incident Rate**                                    | Frequency of incidents relative to a defined population, period, system, or business unit.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Incident Trend**                                   | Pattern of incident frequency, severity, type, cause, or impact over time.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Incident Aging**                                   | Length of time an incident remains unresolved or open.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Incident Backlog**                                 | Collection of unresolved or outstanding incidents.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Incident Recurrence Rate**                         | Percentage or frequency of incidents that recur after previous resolution.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Containment Effectiveness**                        | Degree to which containment measures successfully limit incident spread or impact.                                                                                                                                            |
| **Response Effectiveness**                           | Degree to which incident-response activities achieve intended objectives.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Detection Effectiveness**                          | Ability of security controls and processes to identify incidents accurately and promptly.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Recovery Effectiveness**                           | Ability to restore systems, services, and operations within defined recovery requirements.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Incident Readiness**                               | Organizational preparedness to detect, respond to, and recover from incidents.                                                                                                                                                |
| **Incident Response Readiness Assessment**           | Evaluation of the organization's ability to effectively respond to incidents.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Tabletop Exercise**                                | Discussion-based exercise used to test incident-response plans, roles, decisions, and communications.                                                                                                                         |
| **Simulation Exercise**                              | Exercise designed to simulate incident conditions and test response capabilities.                                                                                                                                             |

| **Purple Team Exercise**                             | Collaborative exercise involving offensive and defensive teams to improve detection and response capabilities.                                                                                                                |
| **Blue Team**                                        | Defensive security team responsible for protecting systems and detecting and responding to attacks.                                                                                                                           |
| **Red Team**                                         | Authorized team that simulates adversary behavior to test defenses.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Purple Team**                                      | Collaborative function combining offensive and defensive activities to improve security effectiveness.                                                                                                                        |

# Incident Severity Model

A practical GRC model can use four levels:

| Severity                  | Description                                                 | Typical Response              |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Severity 1 – Critical** | Severe or potentially catastrophic business/security impact | Executive + crisis response   |
| **Severity 2 – High**     | Significant impact requiring coordinated response           | Incident Response Team        |
| **Severity 3 – Medium**   | Limited impact requiring structured response                | Security/IT team              |
| **Severity 4 – Low**      | Minimal impact                                              | Standard operational handling |

The exact thresholds should be defined by the organization.

# Incident vs. Problem vs. Risk

These terms are frequently confused.

| Concept           | Core Question                                                                    |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Event**         | What happened?                                                                   |
| **Incident**      | What happened that requires response?                                            |
| **Problem**       | Why does this keep happening?                                                    |
| **Risk**          | What could happen in the future?                                                 |
| **Threat**        | What could cause harm?                                                           |
| **Vulnerability** | What weakness could be exploited?                                                |
| **Control**       | What prevents, detects, or mitigates the risk?                                   |
| **Crisis**        | Has the situation reached a level requiring strategic organizational management? |

A useful relationship is:

**Threat**

*

**Vulnerability**

*

**Exposure**

↓

**Potential Risk**

↓

**Incident Occurs**

↓

**Incident Response**

↓

**Root Cause**

↓

**Problem Management**

↓

# Incident Response Roles

A mature organization should define:

### Strategic Level

**Executive Management**

→ Business decisions
→ Risk acceptance
→ External communication
→ Regulatory decisions

### Tactical Level

**Incident Commander**

→ Coordinates response
→ Sets priorities
→ Manages escalation
→ Coordinates stakeholders

### Technical Level

**SOC / CSIRT / IT / Forensics**

# Incident Management Metrics

For a GRC dashboard, useful metrics include:

### Detection

**MTTD**

How quickly incidents are detected.

### Acknowledgment

**MTTA**

How quickly alerts are acknowledged.

### Response

**MTTR**

How quickly response begins or completes, depending on organizational definition.

### Containment

**MTTC**

How quickly the incident is contained.

### Recovery

**MTTR / Mean Time to Recover**

How quickly systems return to normal.

### Recurrence

**Incident Recurrence Rate**

How frequently similar incidents happen again.

### Effectiveness

**False Positive Rate**

How many alerts do not represent real incidents.

### Backlog

**Open Incident Count**

Number of unresolved incidents.

### Compliance

**Incident Notification SLA Compliance**

Percentage of required notifications completed within defined regulatory or contractual deadlines.

# Incident Management and Business Continuity

Incident management and business continuity are related but different.

### Incident Management

Focuses primarily on:

> **Detecting and responding to the incident.**

### Business Continuity

Focuses primarily on:

> **Maintaining or restoring critical business operations.**

### Disaster Recovery

Focuses primarily on:

> **Recovering technology, infrastructure, applications, and data.**

The relationship can be represented as:

**Cybersecurity Incident**

↓

**Incident Response**

↓

If significant disruption:

**Crisis Management**

↓

**Business Continuity**

*

**Disaster Recovery**

↓

**Business Recovery**

### Key GRC takeaway

The central incident-management relationship is:

> **Prepare → Detect → Triage → Analyze → Contain → Eradicate → Recover → Review → Improve**

And from a **GRC perspective**, an incident is not simply a technical problem. It is also a **risk, governance, compliance, business continuity, privacy, third-party, and assurance event**.

A mature GRC program therefore treats every significant incident as an opportunity to answer five questions:

**What happened?**
**Why did it happen?**
**What was the impact?**
**Were our controls effective?**
**What must change to prevent recurrence?**
