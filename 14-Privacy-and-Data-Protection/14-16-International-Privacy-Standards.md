## Introduction

As organizations collect, process, store, and share increasing volumes of personal information, they face growing expectations from customers, regulators, and business partners to demonstrate responsible privacy management. While information security standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 help organizations protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information, they do not provide comprehensive guidance for managing privacy-specific requirements. Organizations therefore need a structured framework that integrates privacy governance into their existing Information Security Management System (ISMS).

**ISO/IEC 27701:2019**, formally known as the **Privacy Information Management System (PIMS)**, is an international standard that extends ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 27002 by providing requirements and guidance for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving a Privacy Information Management System. It enables organizations to manage personally identifiable information (PII) responsibly while demonstrating compliance with privacy regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and other global privacy laws.

ISO/IEC 27701 applies to organizations acting as **PII Controllers**, **PII Processors**, or both. It defines additional privacy controls, governance requirements, risk management practices, operational procedures, and accountability mechanisms that complement an organization's existing information security management program. Rather than replacing ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701 builds upon it by extending security governance into comprehensive privacy management.

Implementing ISO/IEC 27701 provides numerous business benefits. It strengthens customer trust, improves regulatory compliance, enhances privacy governance, supports international business relationships, reduces privacy risks, and demonstrates organizational commitment to protecting personal information. Many organizations adopt ISO/IEC 27701 alongside ISO/IEC 27001 to create an integrated management system that addresses both information security and privacy requirements.

This lesson introduces ISO/IEC 27701, explains its structure, core concepts, implementation approach, relationship with ISO/IEC 27001, and its significance within Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC).

# What is a Privacy Information Management System (PIMS)?

A **Privacy Information Management System (PIMS)** is a structured framework of policies, processes, procedures, controls, and governance activities designed to manage privacy risks associated with personal information.

A PIMS helps organizations:

- Govern personal information.
- Define privacy responsibilities.
- Manage privacy risks.
- Support legal compliance.
- Protect data subjects.
- Monitor privacy performance.
- Maintain documentation.
- Demonstrate accountability.

A PIMS complements an Information Security Management System rather than replacing it.

- # ISO/IEC 29100 Privacy Framework

As organizations operate across multiple jurisdictions and process increasing volumes of personal information, they require a common set of privacy principles that can be applied consistently regardless of industry, technology, or geographic location. Different countries have different privacy laws, but many of these regulations are built upon similar privacy concepts such as transparency, accountability, purpose limitation, data minimization, and individual rights. A globally recognized privacy framework helps organizations establish a consistent approach to managing personal information while supporting compliance with diverse regulatory requirements.

**ISO/IEC 29100**, officially titled **Information Technology — Security Techniques — Privacy Framework**, is an international standard that defines a high-level privacy framework for protecting Personally Identifiable Information (PII). Rather than prescribing specific technical controls or management system requirements, ISO/IEC 29100 provides a common privacy vocabulary, identifies key privacy stakeholders, and establishes internationally accepted privacy principles that organizations can use when designing, implementing, and managing privacy programs.

Unlike ISO/IEC 27701, which specifies requirements for a Privacy Information Management System (PIMS), ISO/IEC 29100 serves as a conceptual framework that guides organizations in developing privacy policies, governance structures, operational processes, and technical safeguards. It is technology-neutral and can be applied to cloud services, mobile applications, artificial intelligence, healthcare systems, financial institutions, government agencies, and virtually any environment where personal information is processed.

ISO/IEC 29100 complements other international standards, including ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701, and the NIST Privacy Framework. Many of its privacy principles also align closely with regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), making it a valuable reference for organizations implementing global privacy programs.

- # NIST Privacy Framework

As organizations increasingly rely on digital technologies, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and connected devices, protecting personal information has become a critical business objective. While cybersecurity frameworks help organizations defend against threats targeting information systems, privacy requires a broader approach that considers how personal information is collected, used, shared, retained, and disposed of throughout its lifecycle. Organizations must therefore manage privacy risks in addition to cybersecurity risks.

The **NIST Privacy Framework (NIST PF)** is a voluntary framework developed by the **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)** to help organizations identify, assess, manage, and communicate privacy risks. It provides a flexible, risk-based approach that organizations of all sizes and sectors can use to integrate privacy into business operations, technology development, and enterprise risk management.

Unlike privacy regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the NIST Privacy Framework does not create legal obligations. Instead, it offers practical guidance that organizations can tailor to their business environment while supporting compliance with applicable laws and regulations. The framework complements other internationally recognized standards, including ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701, ISO/IEC 29100, and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF).

One of the framework's strengths is its integration of privacy risk management with organizational governance. It encourages organizations to view privacy as a strategic business function rather than simply a legal or compliance requirement. By embedding privacy into governance, risk management, and system development, organizations can build trust with customers, improve transparency, and reduce privacy-related risks.

This lesson introduces the NIST Privacy Framework, explains its structure, Core Functions, Profiles, and Implementation Tiers, and demonstrates its role within Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC).

- # Mapping Privacy Standards

Modern organizations rarely rely on a single privacy standard or regulatory framework. Instead, they often implement multiple international standards, national regulations, contractual obligations, and industry-specific requirements simultaneously. For example, a multinational organization may maintain an ISO/IEC 27001 Information Security Management System (ISMS), implement an ISO/IEC 27701 Privacy Information Management System (PIMS), align with the NIST Privacy Framework, comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and satisfy sector-specific regulations such as HIPAA or PCI DSS. Managing these requirements independently can result in duplicated controls, inconsistent processes, increased costs, and unnecessary administrative effort.

**Privacy standards mapping** is the process of identifying relationships, similarities, and differences between various privacy standards and regulations. By mapping requirements across frameworks, organizations can develop a unified privacy program that satisfies multiple compliance obligations using a common set of governance structures, policies, controls, processes, and evidence.

Standards mapping is a fundamental activity within Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC). It allows organizations to streamline compliance efforts, reduce duplication, improve audit readiness, simplify control implementation, and strengthen enterprise-wide governance. Rather than treating each framework as an isolated requirement, organizations build integrated management systems where one set of controls supports multiple standards.

International frameworks such as ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701, ISO/IEC 29100, and the NIST Privacy Framework complement one another because each addresses different aspects of privacy and information management. While GDPR establishes legal obligations, ISO standards provide management system guidance, and NIST frameworks offer practical risk management methodologies. Mapping these frameworks creates a comprehensive and efficient privacy program.

This lesson explains the purpose of privacy standards mapping, compares major privacy frameworks, discusses implementation strategies, and demonstrates its value from a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) perspective.

# Practical Example

A multinational financial services organization operates across Europe, North America, and Asia. It must comply with GDPR, maintain an ISO/IEC 27001-certified Information Security Management System, implement ISO/IEC 27701 for privacy management, and use the NIST Privacy Framework to support enterprise privacy risk management. Instead of managing four separate compliance programs, the organization develops a centralized control matrix that maps common requirements across all frameworks. Unified privacy policies, risk assessments, training programs, internal audits, and evidence repositories are established to satisfy multiple standards simultaneously. During external audits, the organization demonstrates how a single privacy control supports several regulatory and framework requirements, reducing audit effort and improving consistency.

By mapping international privacy standards into one integrated program, the organization streamlines compliance, reduces duplication, strengthens governance, improves regulatory readiness, and enhances overall privacy management.

## Key Takeaways

- Privacy standards mapping is the process of identifying relationships and common requirements across multiple privacy standards, frameworks, and regulations.
- Mapping enables organizations to implement a unified privacy program that reduces duplication, simplifies compliance, improves governance, and strengthens audit readiness.
- Major privacy frameworks—including GDPR, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701, ISO/IEC 29100, and the NIST Privacy Framework—share many common governance, risk management, and privacy principles.
- An integrated approach allows organizations to use common policies, controls, documentation, and evidence to satisfy multiple compliance obligations efficiently.
- From a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) perspective, standards mapping strengthens organizational accountability, improves regulatory compliance, reduces operational complexity, and supports mature enterprise privacy management.
