# 19.20 Executive GRC Master Case Studies

## Part 1 – Building GRC for a Large Enterprise from the Ground Up

## 1. Case Study Overview

GlobalOne is a large multinational enterprise operating across multiple countries, business units, and technology environments.

The organization has grown rapidly through:

* Organic expansion
* Acquisitions
* New digital services
* Cloud adoption
* International expansion
* Outsourcing
* Technology modernization

However, GRC capabilities have not evolved at the same pace.

The organization has:

* Multiple risk registers
* Separate compliance programs
* Inconsistent control frameworks
* Fragmented audit processes
* Different security standards
* Decentralized third-party assessments
* Limited executive visibility

The Board therefore decides to establish:

> **An enterprise-wide GRC program from the ground up.**

The objective is not simply to create another compliance function.

The objective is to build an integrated capability that enables management to understand:

> **What can prevent the organization from achieving its objectives, how those risks are controlled, whether controls are effective, where gaps exist, and what management should do about them.**

# 3. The Initial GRC Problem

When the Board commissions the assessment, management discovers that GRC activities are highly fragmented.

Different departments maintain their own:

* Risk assessments
* Control libraries
* Policies
* Compliance registers
* Audit findings
* Supplier assessments

The same risk may therefore appear several times.

For example:

```text id="9g0x2d"
Cybersecurity Risk
      ↓
Security Risk Register

Same Risk
      ↓
Enterprise Risk Register

Same Risk
      ↓
Compliance Register

Same Risk
      ↓
Audit Register
```

There is no common relationship between these records.

### First Line

* Own risks

* Establish frameworks

* Monitor risk
* Provide guidance

### Third Line

# 52. Lessons Learned

### 1. GRC transformation starts with governance.

Technology alone cannot solve fragmented accountability.

### 2. Common frameworks create consistency.

A common taxonomy and control framework allow different functions to operate together.

### 3. Business ownership is essential.

Risk functions should not become the owners of operational risk.

### 4. Integration should not eliminate specialization.

Cybersecurity, privacy, compliance, risk, and audit still require specialized expertise.

### 5. Risk must connect to business objectives.

The organization should understand why each risk matters to strategy and operations.

### 6. Controls should be rationalized.

Thousands of duplicate controls create unnecessary complexity.

### 7. Technology should enable GRC.

The GRC platform should support processes rather than become the objective itself.

### 8. Automation should focus on high-value activities.

Continuous monitoring is particularly valuable for dynamic technology environments.

### 9. Executive reporting must be decision-oriented.

Boards need actionable information rather than large volumes of GRC data.

### 10. GRC maturity is a continuous journey.

The organization should continuously improve its governance, risk, controls, compliance, assurance, and technology capabilities.

# 54. Final Case Study Conclusion

GlobalOne's experience demonstrates that building GRC from the ground up requires more than implementing policies, creating risk registers, or purchasing a GRC platform.

The organization must establish:

* Clear governance
* Executive sponsorship
* Risk ownership
* Common taxonomy
* Common controls
* Regulatory traceability
* Integrated assurance
* Third-party governance
* Technology enablement
* Continuous monitoring
* Risk-based decision-making

The ultimate objective is to create a system in which:

> **Business objectives → Risks → Controls → Evidence → Assurance → Remediation → Residual Risk → Executive Decisions**

are connected.

A mature enterprise GRC program therefore becomes part of how the organization is managed—not simply a compliance function.

The key executive lesson is:

> **The purpose of GRC is not to eliminate all risk. It is to ensure that the organization understands its risks, manages them within approved appetite, meets its obligations, maintains effective controls, and makes informed decisions about where to accept, reduce, transfer, or avoid risk.**
