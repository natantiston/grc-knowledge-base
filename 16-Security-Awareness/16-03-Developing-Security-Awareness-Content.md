## Introduction

The effectiveness of a Security Awareness Program depends not only on how training is delivered but also on the quality and relevance of its content. Awareness materials should address the cybersecurity risks employees are most likely to encounter in their daily work while supporting the organization's business objectives, risk management strategy, and regulatory obligations. Developing meaningful and practical content helps employees understand not only **what** they should do, but also **why** secure behaviors are important.

Security awareness content should never be static. As cyber threats, technologies, regulations, and business processes evolve, awareness materials must be reviewed and updated regularly to remain relevant. Emerging threats such as ransomware, cloud security risks, artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled attacks, deepfake scams, and software supply chain compromises require organizations to continually adapt their awareness content to address new attack techniques.

Organizations should also recognize that awareness content must be audience-focused rather than technology-focused. Employees are more likely to engage with training that reflects real-world situations they encounter in their daily responsibilities. Using practical examples, case studies, interactive scenarios, and lessons learned from actual security incidents makes awareness training more relatable and memorable.

International standards emphasize the importance of providing awareness that reflects organizational risks. **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** requires personnel to be aware of information security policies, procedures, and their responsibilities. **ISO/IEC 27002:2022** recommends developing awareness content that supports organizational security objectives, while **NIST SP 800-50** encourages organizations to tailor awareness materials to current threats and business needs.

From a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) perspective, selecting relevant awareness topics ensures that security education addresses the organization's highest risks, supports governance objectives, and demonstrates compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.

# Practical Example

A logistics company performs its annual enterprise risk assessment and identifies phishing, ransomware, remote work security, and cloud data protection as its highest human-related cybersecurity risks. Based on these findings, the Information Security team updates the Security Awareness Program to include new training modules on Business Email Compromise (BEC), secure collaboration in cloud environments, safe use of AI-powered productivity tools, and ransomware prevention. Quarterly reviews ensure that the content continues to reflect changes in the threat landscape and organizational priorities.

This example demonstrates how selecting relevant awareness topics based on organizational risk results in a more focused, practical, and effective Security Awareness Program.

## Key Takeaways

- Selecting relevant topics is the foundation of effective Security Awareness content development.
- Awareness topics should align with organizational risks, business objectives, and evolving cyber threats.
- Risk assessments, threat intelligence, regulatory requirements, and previous incidents should guide topic selection.
- Regularly updating awareness materials ensures employees remain prepared for emerging cybersecurity challenges.
- Practical, role-based, and engaging content improves employee participation, knowledge retention, and secure behavior.
- From a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) perspective, selecting relevant awareness topics strengthens governance through strategic alignment, reduces enterprise cyber risk by addressing priority threats, and supports compliance through documented, risk-based awareness planning.

- One of the most effective ways to improve employee engagement and learning outcomes is to tailor Security Awareness content to the specific roles and responsibilities of different workforce groups. Employees across an organization perform different functions, use different technologies, access different types of information, and face different cybersecurity risks. As a result, a single standardized awareness program is unlikely to address the unique challenges associated with every role.

Role-based awareness content focuses on the cybersecurity knowledge, behaviors, and responsibilities most relevant to a particular job function. Rather than providing the same examples and scenarios to all employees, organizations develop targeted content that reflects the day-to-day activities and risks associated with each audience. This approach makes awareness training more meaningful, practical, and effective.

For example, finance personnel should receive additional guidance on Business Email Compromise (BEC), invoice fraud, and payment authorization procedures, while software developers require awareness of secure coding, software supply chain security, and DevSecOps practices. Similarly, executives benefit from awareness covering cyber risk governance, regulatory obligations, and crisis management, whereas system administrators require content related to privileged access management, secure configuration, and infrastructure protection.

Role-based content not only improves learning effectiveness but also supports risk-based security management. By focusing awareness efforts on users with greater access privileges or higher exposure to cyber threats, organizations can significantly reduce the likelihood and impact of security incidents.
