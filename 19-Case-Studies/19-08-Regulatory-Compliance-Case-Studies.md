# 19.8 Regulatory Compliance Case Studies

## Part 1 – Building a Regulatory Compliance Program

## 1. Case Study Overview

**IberiaCloud Digital Services** operates across several European markets and provides cloud, telecommunications, and digital services to enterprise and consumer customers.

As the organization expands, its regulatory obligations increase significantly.

The organization must manage requirements arising from areas such as:

* data protection;
* cybersecurity;
* digital resilience;
* telecommunications;
* outsourcing and third-party risk;
* financial and contractual obligations;
* industry-specific regulations.

Previously, compliance activities were managed independently by different departments.

Management now wants to establish a **centralized, risk-based Regulatory Compliance Program**.

The objective is not simply to "be compliant."

The objective is to establish a repeatable system that can:

> **Identify → Interpret → Assess → Implement → Monitor → Report → Improve**

regulatory requirements across the enterprise.

**IberiaCloud Digital Services**

# 3. The Compliance Problem

The organization discovers that regulatory obligations are distributed across several departments.

### Legal

Tracks legislation.

### Privacy

Tracks data-protection requirements.

### Cybersecurity

Tracks security-related obligations.

### Procurement

Tracks contractual supplier requirements.

### Business Units

Manage industry-specific requirements.

### Internal Audit

Tests selected compliance controls.

The problem is that these activities are not centrally coordinated.

---

# 4. Initial Compliance Problems

The assessment identifies several issues.

### Problem 1

No enterprise-wide regulatory inventory.

### Problem 2

Different departments interpret requirements differently.

### Problem 3

Some regulations are mapped directly to controls while others are not.

### Problem 4

Regulatory changes are sometimes communicated late.

### Problem 5

Compliance evidence is distributed across multiple systems.

### Problem 6

Management does not have a consolidated compliance view.

---

# 5. Management Objective

The Board asks the CISO, General Counsel, and Chief Risk Officer to establish an enterprise compliance program.

The program must provide:

* regulatory visibility;
* clear accountability;
* consistent interpretation;
* control mapping;
* compliance assessments;
* evidence management;
* remediation tracking;
* executive reporting.

---

# 6. What Is a Regulatory Compliance Program?

A regulatory compliance program is a structured governance system used to ensure that an organization:

* identifies applicable obligations;
* understands requirements;
* assigns ownership;
* implements appropriate controls;
* evaluates compliance;
* manages gaps;
* monitors regulatory changes;
* reports compliance status.

A mature program connects:

**Regulation → Requirement → Risk → Control → Evidence → Assessment → Remediation → Reporting**

# 48. Common Regulatory Compliance Program Mistakes

## Mistake 1 – Treating Compliance as a Legal-Only Responsibility

Legal interpretation is critical, but operational teams must implement and operate controls.

## Mistake 2 – Building a Spreadsheet Without Governance

A regulatory inventory alone does not constitute a compliance program.

## Mistake 3 – Mapping Regulations Directly to Policies

Requirements should normally be translated into specific control objectives and controls.

## Mistake 4 – Focusing Only on Documentation

Actual control operation matters more than documentation alone.

---

# 49. Additional Mistakes

## Mistake 5 – Creating Duplicate Controls

Different regulations should use common controls where practical.

## Mistake 6 – Ignoring Risk

Not all compliance issues have the same business impact.

## Mistake 7 – No Evidence Strategy

Compliance assertions need objective evidence.

## Mistake 8 – No Regulatory Change Process

A compliance program must detect changes in the regulatory environment.

# 52. Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Compliance Requires Enterprise Governance

Regulatory obligations often cross multiple business functions.

## Lesson 2 – Applicability Must Be Established First

The organization should determine what actually applies before building controls.

## Lesson 3 – Requirements Must Become Actionable

Regulatory language must be translated into requirements, control objectives and operational controls.

## Lesson 4 – Common Controls Reduce Complexity

One effective control can often satisfy multiple regulatory obligations.

---

# 53. Additional Lessons

## Lesson 5 – Evidence Is Essential

An organization must be able to demonstrate compliance, not merely claim it.

## Lesson 6 – Compliance and Risk Are Connected

A compliance gap should be evaluated according to its potential business consequences.

## Lesson 7 – Ownership Must Remain With the Business

Compliance functions coordinate and oversee, but control owners remain responsible for implementation.

## Lesson 8 – Compliance Is Continuous

Regulatory obligations, business operations and technology continuously change.

---

# 54. Final Case Study Conclusion

The IberiaCloud case demonstrates how an organization can transform fragmented regulatory activities into a structured enterprise compliance program.

The mature model is:

**Regulation → Applicability → Requirement → Risk → Control → Evidence → Assessment → Remediation → Reporting → Monitoring**

The objective is not simply to maintain a large regulatory spreadsheet.

A mature GRC professional should be able to demonstrate:

> **What regulations apply?**

> **What exactly do they require?**

> **Who owns each requirement?**

> **Which controls satisfy the requirement?**

> **What evidence demonstrates compliance?**

> **Where are the gaps?**

> **What risks do those gaps create?**

> **Who is responsible for remediation?**

> **How does management know whether the organization remains compliant?**

When integrated with **ISO 27001, NIST CSF, enterprise risk management, internal audit, privacy governance and third-party risk management**, the regulatory compliance program becomes a core component of the organization's overall **GRC operating model**.

# 19.8 Regulatory Compliance Case Studies

## Part 2 – Mapping Regulatory Requirements to Controls

## 1. Case Study Overview

Following the establishment of its enterprise regulatory compliance program, **IberiaCloud Digital Services** now faces a critical practical challenge:

> **How do we translate hundreds of regulatory requirements into specific controls that can actually be implemented, tested, monitored, and evidenced?**

The organization has identified **247 applicable regulatory requirements** across privacy, cybersecurity, telecommunications, resilience, outsourcing, and other areas.

However, the requirements are currently documented in different systems and interpreted by different teams.

The GRC team therefore launches a **Regulatory Requirement-to-Control Mapping Program**.

The objective is to create a defensible chain:

**Regulation → Requirement → Control Objective → Control → Evidence → Assessment**

---

# 2. Why Requirement-to-Control Mapping Matters

Regulations normally describe **what an organization must achieve**, rather than specifying exactly how the organization must implement the requirement.

For example:

> Protect sensitive information from unauthorized access.

This statement does not automatically tell the organization:

* which technology to deploy;
* which procedure to create;
* who should own the control;
* what evidence should be collected;
* how frequently the control should be tested.

The GRC function must translate the requirement into an actionable control environment.

---

# 3. Organization Profile

### Organization

**IberiaCloud Digital Services**

### Industry

Cloud and telecommunications services

### Employees

Approximately 1,800

### Regulatory Environment

The organization operates under multiple regulatory and contractual obligations.

### Existing Frameworks

* ISO 27001;
* NIST CSF;
* ISO 27005;
* COBIT;
* enterprise risk management.

### Existing GRC Platform

The organization maintains regulatory, risk, control, evidence, and remediation information in its GRC platform.

---

# 4. Initial Problem

The organization discovers that the same requirement is being addressed differently by different departments.

For example:

### Privacy Team

Uses a privacy control.

### Security Team

Uses an access-control standard.

### IT

Uses an IAM procedure.

### Internal Audit

Uses an audit test.

All four may actually address the same underlying control objective.

The organization therefore needs a **common control model**.

---

# 5. What Is Regulatory Requirement-to-Control Mapping?

Requirement-to-control mapping establishes relationships between:

1. regulatory obligations;
2. specific requirements;
3. control objectives;
4. organizational controls;
5. evidence;
6. testing procedures;
7. compliance results.

The core model is:

```text id="mapcore1"
REGULATION
    |
    v
REQUIREMENT
    |
    v
CONTROL OBJECTIVE
    |
    v
COMMON CONTROL
    |
    v
CONTROL IMPLEMENTATION
    |
    v
EVIDENCE
    |
    v
CONTROL TEST
    |
    v
COMPLIANCE RESULT
```

---

# 6. Step 1 – Define the Mapping Scope

The organization initially prioritizes:

* cybersecurity regulations;
* data protection;
* digital resilience;
* telecommunications requirements;
* critical outsourcing;
* contractual cybersecurity obligations.

The first phase focuses on high-risk requirements.

---

# 7. Step 2 – Establish Mapping Principles

The GRC team establishes several rules.

### Principle 1

Map requirements to **outcomes and controls**, not simply documents.

### Principle 2

Reuse common controls wherever possible.

### Principle 3

Maintain traceability to the original regulatory source.

### Principle 4

Do not assume that one control automatically provides complete compliance.

### Principle 5

Validate mappings with subject-matter experts.

---

# 8. Step 3 – Build the Regulatory Requirement Register

The regulatory team extracts individual obligations.

Example:

| Requirement ID | Requirement                              |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| REG-001        | Manage access to sensitive information   |
| REG-002        | Monitor security events                  |
| REG-003        | Report qualifying incidents              |
| REG-004        | Maintain appropriate recovery capability |
| REG-005        | Assess critical suppliers                |

Each requirement receives a unique identifier.

---

# 9. Step 4 – Normalize Regulatory Language

Different regulations may use different terminology for essentially similar concepts.

For example:

* access control;
* authorization;
* identity management;
* restricted access;
* least privilege.

The GRC team normalizes these into common control concepts.

This prevents duplicate controls.

---

# 10. Step 5 – Identify the Control Objective

The next step is to determine what the requirement is trying to achieve.

### Regulatory Requirement

Access to sensitive systems must be restricted to authorized individuals.

### Control Objective

> Ensure that access to sensitive systems is authorized, appropriate, and periodically reviewed.

The control objective is more stable than the exact regulatory wording.

---

# 11. Step 6 – Identify Existing Controls

The GRC team searches the common control library.

# 13. Step 8 – Create a Common Control

Suppose several regulatory requirements relate to access control.

Instead of creating five different controls:

**ACCESS-01**

can become a common enterprise control:

> **Access to information systems shall be authorized based on business need, least privilege, role requirements, and approved access-management procedures.**

This control can support multiple regulations.

# 14. Step 9 – Map One Control to Multiple Regulations

The common control may support:

* GDPR-related security requirements;
* cybersecurity regulations;
* telecommunications obligations;
* contractual security requirements;
* ISO 27001;
* NIST CSF.

This creates a **many-to-many relationship**.

```text id="manymany2"

 Regulation D -----/

```

This is one of the most valuable concepts in mature GRC.

# 15. Step 10 – Avoid Over-Mapping

More mappings do not necessarily mean better compliance.

A common mistake is to map a control to a requirement simply because the wording appears similar.

For example:

**Regulation**

Requires timely incident notification to a regulator.

**Control**

Security event monitoring.

Monitoring alone does not satisfy notification requirements.

Additional controls may be required for:

* incident classification;
* legal assessment;
* regulatory notification;
* approval;
* reporting;
* documentation.

---

# 16. Step 11 – Determine Mapping Coverage

The GRC team measures how much of each requirement is addressed.

Example:

### Full Coverage

The control completely addresses the requirement.

### Partial Coverage

The control addresses only part of the requirement.

### No Coverage

No existing control adequately addresses the requirement.

This distinction is critical.

| Incident notification | Partial  |

| Supplier monitoring   | None     |

The organization can now identify control deficiencies.

# 18. Step 12 – Assess Control Design

A mapped control may exist but still be poorly designed.

The GRC team evaluates:

* objective;
* scope;
* frequency;
* ownership;
* procedures;
* inputs;
* outputs;
* evidence;
* dependencies.

A control should be capable of achieving its intended objective.

---

# 19. Example Control Design Review

### Control

Privileged access review.

### Frequency

Annual.

### Regulatory Expectation

Risk-based periodic review.

### Assessment

Annual review may be insufficient for highly privileged accounts.

### Result

**Control design gap**

The control needs to be strengthened.

# 20. Step 13 – Assess Operating Effectiveness

A properly designed control may still fail operationally.

Example:

### Control Requirement

Privileged accounts must be reviewed quarterly.

### Actual Performance

Only 80% of accounts were reviewed.

The control exists, but its operating effectiveness is insufficient.

The compliance mapping therefore identifies a control effectiveness issue.

* terminated-user reports.

The evidence should demonstrate that the control actually operated.

# 50. Common Mapping Mistakes

## Mistake 1 – Mapping by Keyword

Similar wording does not necessarily mean equivalent requirements.

## Mistake 2 – One-to-One Mapping

One regulation may require multiple controls.

## Mistake 3 – Ignoring Partial Coverage

A control may address only part of a requirement.

## Mistake 4 – Mapping Without Evidence

A theoretical control is not sufficient evidence of compliance.

---

# 51. Additional Mistakes

## Mistake 5 – Ignoring Control Effectiveness

A control can exist but operate poorly.

## Mistake 6 – Creating Duplicate Controls

Multiple frameworks do not necessarily require separate controls.

## Mistake 7 – Failing to Involve Legal

Regulatory interpretation can materially affect mapping.

## Mistake 8 – Treating the Matrix as Static

Requirements, controls, risks and technology continuously change.

# 54. Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Mapping Creates Traceability

Management should be able to trace a requirement all the way to an operational control and evidence.

## Lesson 2 – Common Controls Reduce Complexity

A mature control framework can support many regulations simultaneously.

## Lesson 3 – Coverage Must Be Evaluated

A mapping is not automatically proof of compliance.

## Lesson 4 – Evidence Completes the Chain

Without evidence, the organization may be unable to demonstrate that a control actually operates.

---

# 55. Additional Lessons

## Lesson 5 – Control Effectiveness Matters

A well-designed control that does not operate effectively cannot reliably satisfy a regulatory requirement.

## Lesson 6 – Legal and Operational Perspectives Must Meet

Legal determines what the regulation requires; operational teams determine how the requirement is implemented.

## Lesson 7 – GRC Should Reduce Complexity

The goal of mapping is not to create a bigger spreadsheet.

It is to create a **simpler, integrated and defensible control environment**.

## Lesson 8 – Mapping Supports Multiple Frameworks

A well-designed common control structure can connect:

**Regulations + ISO 27001 + NIST CSF + COBIT + Enterprise Risk Management + Audit**

into a single GRC architecture.

# 56. Final Case Study Conclusion

The IberiaCloud case demonstrates how regulatory requirements can be transformed into an actionable control environment.

The mature relationship is:

**Regulation → Requirement → Control Objective → Common Control → Control Activity → Evidence → Testing → Compliance Result**

The most important principle is:

> **Do not map regulations simply to documents. Map regulatory obligations to the controls and evidence that actually achieve the required outcome.**

When implemented correctly, requirement-to-control mapping enables the organization to:

* reduce duplicate controls;
* improve regulatory traceability;
* identify control gaps;
* improve audit readiness;
* automate compliance monitoring;
* connect compliance with enterprise risk;
* simplify multi-framework compliance;
* provide defensible evidence to regulators and auditors.

Ultimately, the GRC professional should be able to answer a regulator, auditor, executive, or risk committee with a clear chain:

**"Here is the requirement, here is the control that addresses it, here is the evidence that proves it operated, here is our testing result, and here is how we manage any remaining risk."**
