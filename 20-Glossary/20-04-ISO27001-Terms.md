# 20.4 ISO 27001 Terms

This section defines the core terminology associated with **ISO/IEC 27001**, particularly the Information Security Management System (ISMS), information security risk management, Statement of Applicability, controls, audits, certification, and continual improvement.

| Term                                                      | Definition                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **ISO/IEC 27001**                                         | An international standard specifying requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS).               |
| **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**         | A systematic management system used to establish, implement, maintain, and continually improve information security based on organizational context, risks, and applicable requirements. |
| **ISMS Scope**                                            | The defined organizational, technological, geographical, service, and process boundaries within which the ISMS applies.                                                                  |
| **ISMS Policy**                                           | The formally approved policy establishing the organization's overall direction and commitment regarding information security.                                                            |
| **Information Security**                                  | Preservation of the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.                                                                                                         |
| **Confidentiality**                                       | The property that information is not made available or disclosed to unauthorized individuals, entities, or processes.                                                                    |
| **Integrity**                                             | The property of accuracy, completeness, and protection against unauthorized modification of information.                                                                                 |
| **Availability**                                          | The property of being accessible and usable when required by an authorized entity.                                                                                                       |
| **Information Security Objective**                        | A desired result established by the organization relating to information security.                                                                                                       |
| **ISMS Objective**                                        | A defined objective intended to support the effectiveness and continual improvement of the ISMS.                                                                                         |
| **Organizational Context**                                | The internal and external issues that can affect the organization's ability to achieve the intended outcomes of its ISMS.                                                                |
| **Interested Party**                                      | A person or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by a decision or activity relevant to the ISMS.                                              |
| **Interested Party Requirement**                          | A need or expectation of an interested party that the organization determines is relevant to the ISMS.                                                                                   |
| **Internal Issue**                                        | An internal factor that can influence the organization's ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the ISMS.                                                                           |
| **External Issue**                                        | An external factor that can influence the organization's ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the ISMS.                                                                           |
| **ISMS Boundary**                                         | The defined limits within which the ISMS operates.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Leadership**                                            | The organizational leadership responsible for demonstrating commitment, direction, and support for the ISMS.                                                                             |
| **Top Management**                                        | The person or group of people who directs and controls an organization at the highest level.                                                                                             |
| **Information Security Policy**                           | A formally approved statement establishing management's direction and commitment to information security.                                                                                |
| **Roles and Responsibilities**                            | Defined organizational duties, authorities, and accountability associated with operating and maintaining the ISMS.                                                                       |
| **Authority**                                             | The formally granted power to make decisions or direct activities within the ISMS.                                                                                                       |
| **Competence**                                            | The ability to apply knowledge and skills to achieve intended results.                                                                                                                   |
| **Awareness**                                             | Understanding by relevant personnel of the ISMS, information security requirements, responsibilities, and consequences of nonconformity.                                                 |
| **Communication**                                         | The defined processes through which relevant ISMS information is communicated internally and externally.                                                                                 |
| **Documented Information**                                | Information required to be controlled and maintained by the organization, together with the medium on which it is contained.                                                             |
| **Controlled Document**                                   | Documented information subject to defined requirements for creation, approval, access, modification, retention, and disposal.                                                            |
| **Document Control**                                      | Processes used to ensure documented information is appropriately created, approved, protected, updated, distributed, retained, and disposed of.                                          |
| **Information Security Risk**                             | Risk associated with the potential effects of uncertainty on information security objectives.                                                                                            |
| **Risk**                                                  | The effect of uncertainty on objectives.                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Risk Assessment**                                       | The overall process of risk identification, risk analysis, and risk evaluation.                                                                                                          |
| **Risk Identification**                                   | The process of finding, recognizing, and describing information security risks.                                                                                                          |
| **Risk Analysis**                                         | The process of understanding the nature and level of information security risk.                                                                                                          |
| **Risk Evaluation**                                       | The process of comparing analyzed risks against established risk criteria to determine their significance.                                                                               |
| **Risk Criteria**                                         | Terms of reference against which the significance of information security risk is evaluated.                                                                                             |
| **Risk Acceptance Criteria**                              | Defined criteria used to determine whether an information security risk can be accepted.                                                                                                 |
| **Risk Owner**                                            | The person or entity with accountability and authority for managing a particular risk.                                                                                                   |
| **Risk Treatment**                                        | The process of modifying risk through actions such as avoiding, reducing, sharing, or retaining the risk.                                                                                |
| **Risk Treatment Plan**                                   | A documented plan specifying how identified risks will be treated, including actions, responsibilities, priorities, and implementation details.                                          |
| **Risk Treatment Options**                                | Available approaches for modifying risk, including risk avoidance, reduction, sharing, and retention.                                                                                    |
| **Residual Risk**                                         | The risk remaining after risk treatment has been implemented.                                                                                                                            |
| **Risk Acceptance**                                       | An informed decision to retain a particular risk based on defined criteria and appropriate authority.                                                                                    |
| **Information Security Risk Assessment Process**          | The defined methodology used to consistently identify, analyze, and evaluate information security risks.                                                                                 |
| **Information Security Risk Treatment Process**           | The defined methodology used to select and implement appropriate measures for modifying information security risks.                                                                      |
| **Control**                                               | A measure that modifies or maintains risk.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Information Security Control**                          | A control designed to manage information security risks and support information security objectives.                                                                                     |
| **Control Objective**                                     | The intended outcome that one or more controls are designed to achieve.                                                                                                                  |
| **Control Implementation**                                | The process of deploying and operating a selected control within the organization.                                                                                                       |
| **Control Owner**                                         | The person or function responsible for ensuring that a control is appropriately designed, implemented, maintained, and monitored.                                                        |
| **Control Effectiveness**                                 | The degree to which a control operates as intended and achieves its defined objectives.                                                                                                  |
| **Control Design**                                        | The structure and characteristics of a control intended to address a particular risk or requirement.                                                                                     |
| **Preventive Control**                                    | A control designed to prevent an unwanted event or condition from occurring.                                                                                                             |
| **Detective Control**                                     | A control designed to identify an unwanted event or condition after or while it occurs.                                                                                                  |
| **Corrective Control**                                    | A control designed to correct or recover from an unwanted event or condition.                                                                                                            |
| **Annex A**                                               | The reference control set provided by ISO/IEC 27001:2022 containing 93 information security controls organized into four themes.                                                         |
| **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A**                            | The Annex A control reference associated with the 2022 edition of ISO/IEC 27001, organized into Organizational, People, Physical, and Technological controls.                            |
| **Organizational Controls**                               | Annex A controls addressing organizational governance, policies, processes, supplier relationships, legal requirements, information security management, and related areas.              |
| **People Controls**                                       | Annex A controls addressing personnel-related information security matters such as screening, awareness, training, responsibilities, and disciplinary processes.                         |
| **Physical Controls**                                     | Annex A controls addressing physical security of facilities, equipment, assets, and information processing environments.                                                                 |
| **Technological Controls**                                | Annex A controls addressing technical measures such as authentication, access control, malware protection, logging, monitoring, cryptography, and secure development.                    |
| **Statement of Applicability (SoA)**                      | A documented statement describing the necessary controls, justification for inclusion, implementation status, and justification for excluding controls where applicable.                 |
| **Applicable Control**                                    | An information security control determined to be necessary based on the organization's risk assessment, requirements, or other relevant considerations.                                  |
| **Control Justification**                                 | The documented rationale explaining why a control is included, excluded, or otherwise addressed within the ISMS.                                                                         |
| **Control Applicability**                                 | The determination of whether a particular control is relevant and necessary within the organization's ISMS.                                                                              |
| **Control Implementation Status**                         | The documented state indicating whether a selected control has been implemented, partially implemented, planned, or otherwise addressed.                                                 |
| **ISMS Performance**                                      | The measurable results and effectiveness of the ISMS in achieving its intended objectives.                                                                                               |
| **ISMS Effectiveness**                                    | The degree to which the ISMS achieves its intended outcomes.                                                                                                                             |
| **Performance Evaluation**                                | The process of monitoring, measuring, analyzing, and evaluating the performance and effectiveness of the ISMS.                                                                           |
| **Monitoring**                                            | Determining the status of a system, process, activity, or control through observation, measurement, or review.                                                                           |
| **Measurement**                                           | The process of determining a value or result associated with an ISMS activity, objective, process, or control.                                                                           |
| **Analysis**                                              | Examination of collected information to understand performance, trends, causes, relationships, or significance.                                                                          |
| **Evaluation**                                            | Determining the suitability, adequacy, effectiveness, or significance of an ISMS activity or result.                                                                                     |
| **Internal Audit**                                        | A systematic and independent process for obtaining evidence and evaluating whether the ISMS conforms to defined requirements and is effectively implemented and maintained.              |
| **Internal Auditor**                                      | A competent person performing an internal audit of the ISMS.                                                                                                                             |
| **Audit Program**                                         | A set of one or more audits planned for a specific time period and directed toward a specific purpose.                                                                                   |
| **Audit Plan**                                            | A documented description of the activities and arrangements for a specific audit.                                                                                                        |
| **Audit Criteria**                                        | The requirements, policies, procedures, standards, or other criteria against which audit evidence is evaluated.                                                                          |
| **Audit Evidence**                                        | Records, statements of fact, or other verifiable information relevant to the audit criteria.                                                                                             |
| **Audit Finding**                                         | The result of evaluating collected audit evidence against audit criteria.                                                                                                                |
| **Audit Conclusion**                                      | The overall outcome of an audit after considering the audit objectives and findings.                                                                                                     |
| **Audit Scope**                                           | The extent and boundaries of an audit.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Audit Independence**                                    | The condition in which an auditor is free from responsibility or influence that could compromise objective judgment.                                                                     |
| **Management Review**                                     | A formal review by top management of the ISMS to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy, effectiveness, and alignment with organizational direction.                                |
| **Management Review Inputs**                              | Information considered during management review, including performance, audit results, objectives, risks, incidents, changes, and opportunities for improvement.                         |
| **Management Review Outputs**                             | Decisions and actions resulting from management review concerning improvements, changes, resources, objectives, and ISMS effectiveness.                                                  |
| **Nonconformity**                                         | Non-fulfilment of a requirement.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Major Nonconformity**                                   | A significant failure affecting the ISMS's ability to achieve intended outcomes or meet applicable requirements.                                                                         |
| **Minor Nonconformity**                                   | A limited failure to fulfill a requirement that does not substantially compromise the overall ISMS.                                                                                      |
| **Correction**                                            | Action taken to eliminate a detected nonconformity.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Corrective Action**                                     | Action taken to eliminate the cause of a nonconformity and prevent recurrence.                                                                                                           |
| **Root Cause**                                            | The underlying cause or causes responsible for a nonconformity or undesirable condition.                                                                                                 |
| **Corrective Action Plan**                                | A documented plan defining actions, owners, timelines, and verification activities for addressing a nonconformity.                                                                       |
| **Continual Improvement**                                 | A recurring activity undertaken to enhance the suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness of the ISMS.                                                                                     |
| **Improvement Opportunity**                               | A condition or observation indicating that the ISMS could potentially be enhanced even where no nonconformity exists.                                                                    |
| **Information Security Incident**                         | A single or series of unwanted or unexpected information security events that have a significant probability of compromising business operations or information security.                |
| **Information Security Event**                            | An identified occurrence indicating a possible breach of information security or failure of controls.                                                                                    |
| **Incident Management**                                   | Processes for detecting, responding to, managing, documenting, and learning from information security incidents.                                                                         |
| **Incident Response**                                     | Actions taken to detect, contain, eradicate, recover from, and learn from an information security incident.                                                                              |
| **Asset**                                                 | Something that has value to the organization and therefore requires protection.                                                                                                          |
| **Information Asset**                                     | Information or related resources that have value and require appropriate protection.                                                                                                     |
| **Asset Owner**                                           | The individual or function responsible for managing and protecting an asset within its assigned scope.                                                                                   |
| **Information Owner**                                     | The individual or function accountable for determining appropriate protection requirements for information.                                                                              |
| **Asset Inventory**                                       | A maintained record of relevant information assets and associated information.                                                                                                           |
| **Information Classification**                            | The process of categorizing information according to its sensitivity, criticality, value, or protection requirements.                                                                    |
| **Security Classification**                               | A categorization used to determine the level of protection required for information or assets.                                                                                           |
| **Legal Requirement**                                     | A requirement imposed by applicable legislation or law.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Regulatory Requirement**                                | A requirement imposed by a regulator or regulatory framework.                                                                                                                            |
| **Contractual Requirement**                               | An obligation established through a legally binding contract or agreement.                                                                                                               |
| **Interested Party Requirement**                          | A relevant need or expectation of an interested party that the organization determines must be addressed by the ISMS.                                                                    |
| **Supplier**                                              | An external organization or individual providing products, services, resources, or other inputs to the organization.                                                                     |
| **Supplier Relationship**                                 | The formal relationship between an organization and an external supplier.                                                                                                                |
| **Supplier Security**                                     | Measures used to manage information security risks associated with suppliers and external service providers.                                                                             |
| **Third-Party Risk**                                      | Risk arising from dependence on or interaction with external organizations or service providers.                                                                                         |
| **Outsourced Process**                                    | A process that an external organization performs on behalf of the organization.                                                                                                          |
| **Cloud Service**                                         | A service delivered through cloud computing infrastructure or platforms.                                                                                                                 |
| **Secure Development**                                    | The incorporation of information security requirements and practices throughout the development lifecycle.                                                                               |
| **Change Management**                                     | A controlled process for planning, assessing, approving, implementing, and reviewing changes that may affect the ISMS or information security.                                           |
| **Configuration Management**                              | Processes for establishing, maintaining, and controlling approved configurations of systems, devices, and technology components.                                                         |
| **Business Continuity**                                   | The capability of an organization to continue delivery of products or services at acceptable predefined levels following a disruptive event.                                             |
| **Information Security Continuity**                       | The capability to maintain information security during adverse or disruptive conditions.                                                                                                 |
| **Redundancy**                                            | The provision of duplicate or alternative components, systems, resources, or capabilities to improve availability and resilience.                                                        |
| **Cryptography**                                          | The use of mathematical techniques to protect information through mechanisms such as encryption, hashing, digital signatures, and key management.                                        |
| **Cryptographic Key Management**                          | The processes for generating, distributing, storing, using, rotating, revoking, and destroying cryptographic keys.                                                                       |
| **Access Control**                                        | Measures used to restrict access to information and systems according to defined authorization requirements.                                                                             |
| **Identity Management**                                   | Processes for managing identities and associated attributes throughout their lifecycle.                                                                                                  |
| **Authentication**                                        | The process of verifying the identity of an entity.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Authorization**                                         | The process of determining whether an authenticated entity is permitted to access a resource or perform an action.                                                                       |
| **Privileged Access**                                     | Access rights that provide elevated capabilities beyond those granted to ordinary users.                                                                                                 |
| **Least Privilege**                                       | The principle that users, systems, and processes should receive only the access necessary to perform authorized activities.                                                              |
| **Logging**                                               | The recording of events and activities to provide evidence, support monitoring, investigation, and accountability.                                                                       |
| **Monitoring**                                            | The ongoing observation of systems, activities, controls, or events to detect conditions requiring attention.                                                                            |
| **Security Monitoring**                                   | Monitoring activities designed to detect threats, anomalies, control failures, or security events.                                                                                       |
| **Vulnerability Management**                              | The process of identifying, evaluating, prioritizing, remediating, and monitoring vulnerabilities.                                                                                       |
| **Malware Protection**                                    | Measures designed to prevent, detect, contain, and respond to malicious software.                                                                                                        |
| **Backup**                                                | A copy of information or systems maintained to support restoration following loss, corruption, compromise, or disruption.                                                                |
| **Recovery**                                              | The process of restoring systems, services, information, or operations following disruption or failure.                                                                                  |
| **Data Leakage**                                          | Unauthorized disclosure, transmission, exposure, or loss of information.                                                                                                                 |
| **Data Loss Prevention (DLP)**                            | Technologies, controls, and processes designed to prevent unauthorized disclosure or loss of sensitive information.                                                                      |
| **Secure Information Deletion**                           | The controlled removal or destruction of information so that it cannot be improperly recovered or reconstructed.                                                                         |
| **Privacy and Personally Identifiable Information (PII)** | Information security considerations relating to the protection and appropriate handling of information that can identify or relate to individuals.                                       |
| **Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)**                       | A structured assessment of privacy risks and impacts associated with processing personal information.                                                                                    |
| **Information Security Objective**                        | A specific result the organization seeks to achieve through its information security management activities.                                                                              |
| **ISMS Process**                                          | A defined set of interrelated activities used to establish, operate, maintain, monitor, or improve the ISMS.                                                                             |
| **ISMS Documentation**                                    | Documented information necessary to establish, implement, maintain, and demonstrate the operation of the ISMS.                                                                           |
| **Documented Evidence**                                   | Recorded information demonstrating that an activity, control, decision, or requirement has been performed or satisfied.                                                                  |
| **ISMS Record**                                           | Retained documented information providing evidence that a required activity or result occurred.                                                                                          |
| **ISMS Change**                                           | A modification to the ISMS, its scope, processes, controls, requirements, or operating environment.                                                                                      |
| **Planned Change**                                        | A change intentionally designed, assessed, approved, and implemented through controlled processes.                                                                                       |
| **Unplanned Change**                                      | An unexpected change that occurs outside the normal planned change process and may require subsequent assessment and control.                                                            |
| **Continual Improvement Register**                        | A controlled record of identified opportunities and actions for improving the ISMS.                                                                                                      |
| **ISMS Maturity**                                         | The degree to which the ISMS is defined, implemented, measured, integrated, optimized, and continually improved.                                                                         |
| **Certification Audit**                                   | An independent audit performed by a certification body to determine whether an organization meets the requirements for ISO/IEC 27001 certification.                                      |
| **Stage 1 Audit**                                         | The initial certification audit focused primarily on readiness, documented information, scope, context, and preparedness for the Stage 2 audit.                                          |
| **Stage 2 Audit**                                         | The certification audit focused on determining whether the ISMS is implemented and operating effectively against ISO/IEC 27001 requirements.                                             |
| **Surveillance Audit**                                    | A periodic certification-body audit performed after certification to verify continued conformity and effectiveness of the ISMS.                                                          |
| **Recertification Audit**                                 | A comprehensive audit performed at the end of a certification cycle to determine whether certification can be renewed.                                                                   |

| **Accreditation Body**                                    | An organization that formally recognizes the competence of certification bodies to perform certification activities.                                                                     |

## Key ISO/IEC 27001 Distinctions

### 1. ISO/IEC 27001 vs. ISO/IEC 27002

**ISO/IEC 27001** establishes the **requirements for the ISMS**.

**ISO/IEC 27002** provides **guidance and implementation recommendations for information security controls**.

A simple way to remember the distinction:

> **ISO 27001 = Requirements for the management system**
> **ISO 27002 = Guidance for implementing controls**

ISO/IEC 27001 certification is therefore based on conformity with **ISO/IEC 27001 requirements**, not certification against ISO/IEC 27002 alone.

---

### 2. Annex A vs. Statement of Applicability

**Annex A** provides the reference set of **93 controls** in ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

The **Statement of Applicability (SoA)** documents the organization's determination regarding the controls necessary for its ISMS and provides the required justification and implementation status.

Therefore:

> **Annex A = Reference control set**
> **SoA = Organization-specific applicability determination and justification**

An organization should not simply copy Annex A into its SoA without performing its own risk-based determination.

---

### 3. Risk Assessment vs. Risk Treatment

**Risk assessment** determines:

> What risks exist, how significant they are, and which risks require attention.

**Risk treatment** determines:

> What the organization will do about those risks.

The relationship can be represented as:

**Risk Identification → Risk Analysis → Risk Evaluation → Risk Treatment → Residual Risk**

---

### 4. Correction vs. Corrective Action

These terms are particularly important during ISO/IEC 27001 audits.

**Correction**

→ Immediate action to address the detected nonconformity.

**Corrective Action**

→ Action taken to eliminate the underlying cause and prevent recurrence.

Example:

> An audit identifies that an access review was not performed.

**Correction:** Perform the overdue access review.

**Corrective action:** Determine why the review was missed, modify the process or control, assign ownership, establish monitoring, and verify that the problem does not recur.

---

### 5. Nonconformity vs. Observation vs. Opportunity for Improvement

These should not automatically be treated as equivalent.

**Nonconformity**
→ A requirement has not been fulfilled.

**Observation**
→ A condition noted by an auditor that may warrant attention but does not necessarily constitute nonconformity.

**Opportunity for Improvement**
→ A potential enhancement that could improve the ISMS even though the current state may already conform.

---

### 6. ISMS Scope vs. Organizational Scope

The **organizational scope** describes the broader organization.

The **ISMS scope** specifically defines the boundaries and applicability of the information security management system.

The ISMS scope should consider factors such as:

* organizational units;
* locations;
* products and services;
* processes;
* technologies;
* interfaces and dependencies;
* interested-party requirements; and
* internal and external issues.

---

## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Control Structure

The 2022 edition contains **93 Annex A controls** organized into four themes:

| Theme                           | Controls |
| ------------------------------- | -------: |
| **A.5 Organizational Controls** |       37 |
| **A.6 People Controls**         |        8 |
| **A.7 Physical Controls**       |       14 |
| **A.8 Technological Controls**  |       34 |
| **Total**                       |   **93** |

The four themes should not be interpreted as four independent security programs. They collectively provide a reference set of controls that can be selected and implemented based on the organization's risk, requirements, context, and security objectives.

---

## ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS Lifecycle

A practical ISMS lifecycle can be represented as:

**Context → Scope → Leadership → Risk Assessment → Risk Treatment → Control Implementation → SoA → Operation → Monitoring & Measurement → Internal Audit → Management Review → Corrective Action → Continual Improvement**

This creates the foundation for a mature **ISO 27001 GRC operating model**.

The important principle is that ISO/IEC 27001 is **not simply a checklist of 93 security controls**. It is a **management system for governing information security**, with risk management, leadership, objectives, performance evaluation, internal audit, management review, corrective action, and continual improvement forming essential parts of the system.
