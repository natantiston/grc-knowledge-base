# 20.6 ISO 22301 Terms

This section defines the core terminology associated with **ISO 22301**, the international standard for **Business Continuity Management Systems (BCMS)**. The terminology covers business continuity, disruption, business impact analysis, recovery, continuity strategies, exercises, crisis management, and continual improvement.

> **Terminology note:** ISO 22301:2019 uses the ISO management-system vocabulary while introducing business-continuity-specific concepts. Some terms commonly used in business continuity practice—such as **RTO, RPO, MTPD, and BIA**—are closely associated with BCMS implementation even where organizations may define or operationalize them through supporting guidance and methodologies.

---

## ISO 22301 Glossary

| **Business Continuity Management System (BCMS)**  | A management system that enables an organization to prepare for, respond to, and recover from disruptive incidents while maintaining continuity of products and services at acceptable predefined levels.                |
| **Business Continuity**                           | The capability of an organization to continue the delivery of products or services at acceptable predefined levels following a disruptive incident.                                                                      |
| **Business Continuity Management (BCM)**          | The management process that identifies potential threats to an organization and the impacts those threats could cause, providing a framework for building organizational resilience and effective response capabilities. |
| **BCMS Scope**                                    | The defined boundaries and applicability of the Business Continuity Management System.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Business Continuity Policy**                    | A formally approved statement establishing management's direction, commitment, and expectations for business continuity.                                                                                                 |
| **Business Continuity Objective**                 | A result an organization seeks to achieve relating to business continuity.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Organizational Context**                        | The internal and external issues relevant to the organization's purpose and ability to achieve intended BCMS outcomes.                                                                                                   |
| **Interested Party**                              | A person or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by a decision or activity.                                                                                                   |
| **Interested Party Requirement**                  | A need or expectation of an interested party that the organization determines is relevant to the BCMS.                                                                                                                   |
| **Top Management**                                | The person or group of people who directs and controls the organization at the highest level.                                                                                                                            |
| **Business Continuity Roles**                     | Defined responsibilities, authorities, and accountabilities required to establish, operate, maintain, and improve the BCMS.                                                                                              |
| **Competence**                                    | The ability to apply knowledge and skills to achieve intended BCMS results.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Awareness**                                     | Understanding by personnel of the BCMS, business continuity policy, their responsibilities, and the consequences of failing to follow established requirements.                                                          |
| **Documented Information**                        | Information required to be controlled and maintained by the organization and the medium on which it is contained.                                                                                                        |
| **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**                | A process of analyzing activities and the effect that a business disruption might have on them.                                                                                                                          |
| **Business Impact**                               | The consequences of a disruption affecting organizational activities, products, services, objectives, stakeholders, or resources.                                                                                        |
| **Business Impact Analysis Process**              | The systematic process used to determine the impacts of disruption, prioritize activities, and establish continuity and recovery requirements.                                                                           |
| **Business Impact Analysis Methodology**          | The defined approach used to perform BIAs consistently across the organization.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Business Impact Analysis Report**               | Documented results of a BIA, including prioritized activities, impacts, dependencies, and continuity requirements.                                                                                                       |
| **Business Activity**                             | A process, function, or set of tasks performed by an organization to achieve an objective or deliver a product or service.                                                                                               |
| **Prioritized Activity**                          | An activity identified through BIA as requiring priority for continuity or recovery because of its significance to organizational objectives or stakeholders.                                                            |
| **Product**                                       | An output produced by an organization that may be delivered to customers or other interested parties.                                                                                                                    |
| **Service**                                       | An output provided by an organization to meet a need or requirement.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Critical Activity**                             | An activity whose disruption could cause unacceptable impacts and therefore requires prioritized continuity or recovery.                                                                                                 |
| **Critical Business Function**                    | A function considered essential to achieving organizational objectives and requiring priority recovery following disruption.                                                                                             |
| **Disruption**                                    | An event that causes an unplanned, negative deviation from the expected delivery of products or services.                                                                                                                |
| **Disruptive Incident**                           | An event that can interrupt, negatively affect, or prevent normal organizational operations.                                                                                                                             |
| **Incident**                                      | An occurrence or situation that may cause disruption and require response.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Crisis**                                        | A significant situation that threatens organizational objectives, reputation, operations, stakeholders, or viability and requires coordinated management response.                                                       |
| **Emergency**                                     | A serious or unexpected situation requiring immediate action to protect people, assets, operations, or organizational interests.                                                                                         |
| **Crisis Management**                             | The coordinated activities used to manage significant disruptive situations and protect organizational objectives and stakeholders.                                                                                      |
| **Incident Management**                           | The processes used to detect, assess, respond to, control, and recover from incidents.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Emergency Response**                            | Immediate actions taken to protect people, assets, operations, and the environment following an emergency or disruptive event.                                                                                           |
| **Business Continuity Response**                  | Actions taken to maintain or restore prioritized products, services, and activities following disruption.                                                                                                                |
| **Response Structure**                            | The defined organizational arrangement for directing and coordinating response activities during a disruptive incident.                                                                                                  |
| **Response Team**                                 | A designated group responsible for performing defined response, continuity, crisis, or recovery activities.                                                                                                              |
| **Crisis Management Team**                        | A designated group responsible for strategic decision-making and coordination during a significant crisis or disruptive event.                                                                                           |
| **Incident Management Team**                      | A designated group responsible for coordinating the operational response to an incident.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Business Continuity Team**                      | Personnel responsible for planning, coordinating, implementing, exercising, and maintaining business continuity capabilities.                                                                                            |
| **Crisis Communication**                          | The structured communication of information to internal and external stakeholders during a crisis or significant disruptive incident.                                                                                    |
| **Communication Plan**                            | A documented plan defining communication requirements, responsibilities, channels, audiences, and escalation mechanisms during disruption.                                                                               |
| **Stakeholder Communication**                     | Communication with relevant internal and external interested parties before, during, and after a disruptive event.                                                                                                       |
| **Business Continuity Strategy**                  | An approach selected to maintain or restore prioritized activities and products or services following disruption.                                                                                                        |
| **Business Continuity Solution**                  | A defined capability or arrangement implemented to support continuity and recovery requirements.                                                                                                                         |
| **Continuity Strategy**                           | The strategic approach selected to enable continued delivery of prioritized products or services within defined requirements.                                                                                            |
| **Recovery Strategy**                             | An approach for restoring activities, resources, systems, facilities, and services following disruption.                                                                                                                 |
| **Continuity Arrangement**                        | A documented or implemented arrangement supporting the continuity of prioritized activities.                                                                                                                             |
| **Recovery Arrangement**                          | A documented or implemented arrangement supporting restoration following disruption.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Recovery Capability**                           | The ability of an organization to restore prioritized activities and services within defined requirements.                                                                                                               |
| **Resilience**                                    | The ability of an organization to absorb and adapt to a changing environment and continue achieving its objectives.                                                                                                      |
| **Organizational Resilience**                     | The ability of an organization to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and adapt to disruptive events while continuing to operate and achieve objectives.                                                                |

| **Maximum Acceptable Outage**                     | The maximum period for which a product, service, process, or system can remain unavailable before unacceptable consequences occur.                                                                                       |
| **Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MTD)**              | The maximum period a function or service can remain unavailable before unacceptable consequences occur.                                                                                                                  |
| **Dependency**                                    | A relationship in which an activity, service, process, resource, or organization relies upon another component to operate.                                                                                               |
| **Internal Dependency**                           | Dependence on an internal process, function, employee, facility, system, or resource.                                                                                                                                    |
| **External Dependency**                           | Dependence on an external supplier, service provider, utility, regulator, partner, or other external party.                                                                                                              |
| **Interdependency**                               | A relationship in which multiple activities, resources, systems, or organizations depend upon each other.                                                                                                                |

| **Critical Resource**                             | A resource required to maintain or recover a prioritized activity within defined continuity requirements.                                                                                                                |
| **Single Point of Failure (SPOF)**                | A component or dependency whose failure could cause significant disruption because adequate redundancy or alternatives are unavailable.                                                                                  |

| **Hot Site**                                      | A fully or substantially equipped alternate facility capable of supporting rapid continuation or recovery of operations.                                                                                                 |
| **Warm Site**                                     | An alternate facility with some infrastructure and equipment that requires additional configuration or preparation before full operation.                                                                                |
| **Cold Site**                                     | An alternate facility with basic infrastructure that requires significant preparation and installation before operations can resume.                                                                                     |
| **Work Area Recovery**                            | Arrangements that provide alternative workspace for personnel following disruption.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Remote Working**                                | Working arrangements that allow personnel to perform required activities away from the normal workplace.                                                                                                                 |
| **Technology Recovery**                           | Restoration of technology infrastructure, applications, networks, systems, and related capabilities after disruption.                                                                                                    |
| **Data Recovery**                                 | Restoration of information to a usable and required state following loss, corruption, or disruption.                                                                                                                     |
| **Backup**                                        | A copy of information or systems maintained to support recovery following loss, corruption, or disruption.                                                                                                               |
| **Redundancy**                                    | The availability of duplicate or alternative components, resources, systems, or capabilities to improve continuity and resilience.                                                                                       |
| **Geographic Redundancy**                         | Duplication of capabilities across separate geographical locations to reduce exposure to localized disruptions.                                                                                                          |
| **Failover**                                      | The controlled transfer of operations from a failed primary component or environment to an alternative component or environment.                                                                                         |
| **Fallback**                                      | A planned return to an alternative operating arrangement when the preferred or primary arrangement cannot be used.                                                                                                       |
| **Failback**                                      | The process of returning operations from an alternate environment to the restored primary environment.                                                                                                                   |
| **Recovery**                                      | The process of restoring and returning activities, services, resources, systems, or facilities to an acceptable operational state.                                                                                       |
| **Restoration**                                   | The process of returning a system, service, facility, resource, or activity to its required operational condition.                                                                                                       |
| **Resumption**                                    | The process of restarting or continuing an activity after interruption.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Return to Normal Operations**                   | The controlled transition from temporary continuity arrangements back to normal operating arrangements.                                                                                                                  |
| **Workaround**                                    | A temporary alternative method used to maintain or restore an activity when the normal process or system is unavailable.                                                                                                 |
| **Manual Workaround**                             | A temporary manual process used when an automated system or technology-enabled process is unavailable.                                                                                                                   |
| **Alternative Process**                           | A substitute method used to perform an activity when the normal process cannot operate.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Business Continuity Plan (BCP)**                | Documented procedures and information that enable an organization to respond to disruption and continue or recover prioritized activities.                                                                               |
| **Business Continuity Procedure**                 | A documented set of instructions for performing specific continuity or recovery activities.                                                                                                                              |

| **IT Disaster Recovery Plan (ITDRP)**             | A documented plan for recovering technology infrastructure, applications, data, and IT services following disruption.                                                                                                    |
| **Disaster Recovery**                             | The processes and capabilities used to restore technology, infrastructure, and operations following a disruptive event.                                                                                                  |

# Key ISO 22301 Distinctions

## 1. Business Continuity vs. Disaster Recovery

These terms are related but **not interchangeable**.

### Business Continuity

Focuses on:

> **How does the organization continue delivering important products and services despite disruption?**

It covers people, processes, facilities, technology, suppliers, information, communications, and other dependencies.

### Disaster Recovery

Focuses primarily on:

> **How do we restore technology, systems, infrastructure, and data after a disruptive event?**

Therefore:

**Business Continuity ⟶ Broader organizational capability**

**Disaster Recovery ⟶ Important component supporting business continuity**

A mature organization should not treat its IT disaster recovery plan as its entire business continuity program.

---

# 2. BIA vs. Risk Assessment

A **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)** and a **risk assessment** answer different questions.

### Risk Assessment

Asks:

> **What could go wrong, and how significant is the risk?**

### BIA

Asks:

> **What happens to the business if this activity or service is disrupted?**

For example:

**Risk Assessment**

> A telecommunications provider has a risk that its customer authentication platform could become unavailable.

**BIA**

> If the authentication platform is unavailable for four hours, customer transactions may be severely disrupted and regulatory/customer-service consequences may occur.

The two activities complement each other.

---

# 3. MTPD vs. RTO

These are often confused.

### MTPD

**Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption**

→ The maximum duration of disruption that can be tolerated before the resulting impact becomes unacceptable.

### RTO

**Recovery Time Objective**

→ The target time within which the activity, service, or system should be restored.

A practical relationship is:

**RTO < MTPD**

For example:

> MTPD = 24 hours
> RTO = 8 hours

The organization therefore intends to restore the capability within 8 hours, while disruption beyond 24 hours would become unacceptable.

---

# 4. RTO vs. RPO

### RTO

Measures **time**.

> How quickly must the service or system be restored?

### RPO

Measures **data recovery point**.

> How much data loss, measured in time, can be tolerated?

Example:

> **RTO = 4 hours**
> **RPO = 15 minutes**

This means the organization intends to restore the service within four hours and recover information to a point no more than approximately 15 minutes before the disruption, subject to the organization's actual recovery architecture.

---

# 5. Continuity vs. Recovery

**Continuity** focuses on maintaining required operations during disruption.

**Recovery** focuses on restoring capabilities following disruption.

A simplified model is:

**Normal Operations → Disruption → Continuity Response → Recovery → Return to Normal**

---

# 6. Crisis Management vs. Business Continuity Management

### Crisis Management

Focuses on:

* strategic decisions;
* leadership;
* reputation;
* stakeholders;
* external communications;
* regulatory engagement;
* major organizational consequences.

### Business Continuity Management

Focuses on:

* maintaining prioritized services;
* activating continuity arrangements;
* managing dependencies;
* resource allocation;
* alternative processes;
* recovery.

A crisis may therefore trigger both **crisis management** and **business continuity response**.

---

# 7. Exercise vs. Test

A **business continuity exercise** is broader than simply testing whether a technical component works.

Exercises may validate:

* plans;
* roles;
* decision-making;
* communications;
* escalation;
* coordination;
* dependencies;
* technical recovery;
* supplier response.

A technical **test** may focus on whether a particular capability operates as expected.

# ISO 22301 Business Continuity Lifecycle

A practical BCMS lifecycle can be represented as:

**Context & Scope**
↓
**Leadership & Policy**
↓
**Business Impact Analysis**
↓
**Risk Assessment**
↓
**Continuity Requirements**
↓
**Continuity Strategy**
↓
**Business Continuity Plans & Procedures**
↓
**Training & Awareness**
↓
**Exercises & Testing**
↓
**Incident / Disruption Response**
↓
**Recovery**
↓
**Performance Evaluation**
↓
**Management Review**
↓
**Corrective Action & Continual Improvement**

# ISO 22301 in the GRC Context

ISO 22301 connects strongly with the broader GRC ecosystem:

**Business Objectives**
↓
**Business Impact Analysis**
↓
**Continuity Requirements**
↓
**Business Continuity Risk Assessment**
↓
**Continuity Strategy**
↓
**Controls & Capabilities**
↓
**BCP / DRP / Crisis Plans**
↓
**Exercises & Testing**
↓
**Findings & Corrective Actions**
↓
**Management Review**
↓
**Continual Improvement**

It also integrates closely with:

* **ISO 31000** for enterprise risk management;
* **ISO/IEC 27001** for information security;
* **ISO/IEC 27005** for information security risk management;
* **ISO 22301** for business continuity;
* **ISO 20000-1** for IT service management;
* **ISO 9001** for quality management;
* **ISO 14001** for environmental management;
* **ISO 45001** for occupational health and safety; and
* **ISO 42001** for artificial intelligence management.

A mature GRC architecture can therefore use **ISO 31000 as an enterprise risk foundation**, **ISO/IEC 27001 for information security**, and **ISO 22301 for business continuity and organizational resilience**, while connecting their risks, controls, requirements, evidence, audits, and improvement activities through a common GRC platform.
