## Introduction

Successful incident response begins long before a cybersecurity incident occurs. Organizations that respond effectively to security incidents invest significant time and resources in preparation, ensuring that the necessary people, processes, technologies, and governance structures are already in place. One of the most important components of this preparation is establishing a **Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT)**, sometimes referred to as a **Cybersecurity Incident Response Team (CIRT)**.

A CSIRT is a dedicated team responsible for coordinating and managing the organization's response to cybersecurity incidents. The team serves as the central point of coordination throughout the incident lifecycle, from initial detection and analysis to containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review. Rather than operating in isolation, the CSIRT collaborates with Security Operations Centers (SOCs), IT Operations, Legal, Compliance, Privacy, Human Resources, Communications, Executive Management, Business Units, and third-party service providers to ensure that incidents are handled effectively and consistently.

The composition and structure of a CSIRT vary depending on the size, complexity, and risk profile of the organization. Small organizations may assign incident response responsibilities to existing IT or security personnel, while large enterprises often maintain dedicated full-time teams with specialized expertise in digital forensics, malware analysis, threat intelligence, incident coordination, and crisis management. Regardless of size, every organization should establish clear roles, responsibilities, authority, communication channels, and escalation procedures before an incident occurs.

International standards such as **ISO/IEC 27035**, **ISO/IEC 27001**, **NIST SP 800-61 Revision 2**, and the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)** recommend establishing a formal incident response capability with defined governance, documented responsibilities, trained personnel, and regular testing. Within Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC), the CSIRT provides the operational capability that enables organizations to execute incident response policies, manage cyber risks, satisfy regulatory obligations, and support business resilience.

# What is a CSIRT?

A **Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT)** is a designated group of personnel responsible for preparing for, coordinating, investigating, responding to, and recovering from cybersecurity incidents.

The CSIRT serves as the organization's primary incident response capability by:

- Coordinating incident response activities.
- Managing investigations.
- Supporting technical analysis.
- Preserving digital evidence.
- Coordinating communications.
- Supporting recovery.
- Conducting post-incident reviews.
- Driving continual improvement.

The CSIRT acts as the central coordinator throughout the incident response lifecycle.

---

# Objectives of a CSIRT

The primary objectives of a CSIRT include:

- Detect cybersecurity incidents quickly.
- Minimize business disruption.
- Protect critical assets.
- Reduce organizational risk.
- Coordinate response activities.
- Preserve forensic evidence.
- Support regulatory compliance.
- Improve organizational resilience.

These objectives align technical response activities with broader business and governance priorities.

# Types of CSIRT

Organizations may establish different CSIRT models depending on their size and operational requirements.

### Internal CSIRT

An internal CSIRT consists of employees responsible for managing cybersecurity incidents within the organization.

Advantages include:

- Greater organizational knowledge.
- Faster response.
- Better understanding of business operations.
- Improved coordination.

---

### External CSIRT

Some organizations rely on external providers such as Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs) or specialized incident response firms.

Advantages include:

- Specialized expertise.
- 24/7 availability.
- Advanced forensic capabilities.
- Access to threat intelligence.

---

### Hybrid CSIRT

Many organizations combine internal staff with external specialists.

This approach provides:

- Internal business knowledge.
- External technical expertise.
- Flexible resource allocation.
- Enhanced incident response capability.

Hybrid models are increasingly common in medium and large organizations.

# Practical Example

A multinational healthcare provider establishes a dedicated Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT) to improve its cybersecurity preparedness. The team includes an Incident Response Manager, incident handlers, digital forensics specialists, malware analysts, and threat intelligence analysts. Supporting stakeholders include the Security Operations Center (SOC), IT Operations, Legal, Privacy, Compliance, Human Resources, and Corporate Communications. The organization develops incident response playbooks for ransomware, phishing, insider threats, and data breaches, while conducting quarterly tabletop exercises and annual technical simulations. During a ransomware incident, the CSIRT coordinates containment, forensic analysis, executive reporting, regulatory notifications, and service restoration, enabling the organization to recover critical healthcare services with minimal disruption.

This example illustrates how a well-structured CSIRT enables coordinated technical, operational, legal, and business decision-making throughout the incident response lifecycle.

- # Incident Response Training and Awareness

An Incident Response Team (CSIRT) can only be effective if its members—and the broader organization—are adequately prepared to recognize, report, and respond to cybersecurity incidents. Technology alone cannot ensure a successful response. Employees must understand their responsibilities, incident responders must possess the necessary technical and procedural skills, and management must be prepared to make timely business decisions during a crisis. For this reason, **training and awareness** are fundamental components of the preparation phase of incident management.

Cybersecurity threats evolve continuously. New attack techniques, vulnerabilities, ransomware variants, phishing campaigns, and regulatory requirements emerge regularly, requiring organizations to continuously develop the knowledge and capabilities of their workforce. Incident response personnel must remain current with evolving threats, security technologies, investigation techniques, forensic procedures, and organizational response processes. Likewise, all employees should understand how to identify suspicious activities, report potential incidents promptly, and avoid behaviors that increase cybersecurity risk.

Training should not be limited to technical teams. Executive management, business leaders, legal counsel, privacy officers, communications teams, human resources, compliance personnel, and third-party service providers all play important roles during incident response. Each group requires training appropriate to its responsibilities so that coordinated decisions can be made during an incident.

International standards such as **ISO/IEC 27001**, **ISO/IEC 27035**, **ISO/IEC 27002**, **NIST SP 800-61 Revision 2**, and the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)** emphasize the importance of security awareness, role-based training, competency development, and regular exercises. Within Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC), training and awareness reduce operational risk, improve regulatory compliance, strengthen governance, and increase organizational resilience.

# Tabletop Exercises

Tabletop exercises are discussion-based simulations in which participants walk through hypothetical incident scenarios.

Examples include:

- Ransomware attacks.
- Data breaches.
- Insider threats.
- Cloud security incidents.
- Business Email Compromise (BEC).
- Supply chain attacks.

Tabletop exercises validate decision-making, communication, coordination, and governance processes without affecting production systems.

# Practical Example

A global manufacturing company establishes an annual incident response training program for all employees. General staff complete security awareness training covering phishing, password security, social engineering, and incident reporting procedures. Members of the Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT) receive advanced training in digital forensics, malware analysis, and threat hunting, while executive leaders participate in tabletop exercises focused on ransomware decision-making and crisis communication. The organization also conducts quarterly phishing simulations and annual Red Team/Blue Team exercises to validate technical response capabilities. After each exercise, participants review lessons learned and update incident response procedures, playbooks, and training materials based on identified improvement opportunities.

This example demonstrates how continuous training and realistic exercises improve organizational readiness, strengthen coordination, and enable more effective responses to cybersecurity incidents.
