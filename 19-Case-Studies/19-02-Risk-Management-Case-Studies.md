# 19.2 Risk Management Case Studies

## Part 1 – Enterprise Risk Identification and Assessment

## 1. Case Study Overview

This case study demonstrates how an organization establishes an **enterprise risk identification and assessment process** across business, operational, cybersecurity, compliance, financial, third-party, and strategic risk domains.

The objective is to show how a GRC professional transforms scattered risk observations into a **structured enterprise risk register**, applies consistent assessment criteria, and produces information that executives can use for decision-making.

The organization in this case is fictional but reflects a realistic multinational enterprise.

---

# 2. Organization Profile

### Company

**NovaTel Digital Group**

### Industry

Telecommunications and Digital Services

### Employees

Approximately 12,000

### Annual Revenue

Approximately €3.8 billion

### Geographic Presence

* Spain
* France
* Portugal
* Italy
* Qatar
* Saudi Arabia

### Major Business Areas

* Mobile telecommunications
* Fixed broadband
* Enterprise connectivity
* Cloud services
* Digital platforms
* IoT
* Managed cybersecurity services

---

# 3. Business Situation

NovaTel has traditionally managed risk within individual departments.

For example:

* Finance manages financial risks.
* IT manages technology risks.
* Cybersecurity manages security risks.
* Legal manages legal risks.
* Procurement manages supplier risks.
* Operations manages operational risks.

Each function uses slightly different methodologies.

Management therefore lacks a consolidated view of enterprise risk.

---

# 4. Management Concern

The CEO asks the Chief Risk Officer:

> **"What are our ten most important enterprise risks, and which of them could prevent us from achieving our strategic objectives?"**

The existing risk registers cannot answer the question reliably.

Different departments use:

* different scoring scales;
* different risk definitions;
* different assessment periods;
* different impact categories;
* different ownership models.

The organization decides to establish a common enterprise risk assessment methodology.

---

# 5. Assessment Objectives

The program has five objectives:

1. Identify significant enterprise risks.
2. Establish a common risk taxonomy.
3. Assess risks consistently.
4. Determine risk exposure relative to risk appetite.
5. Prioritize risk treatment and executive attention.

The process must cover both **traditional enterprise risks and cybersecurity/GRC risks**.

---

Examples:

* service interruption;

Risks involving technology platforms.

Examples:

### Regulatory Risk

Risks arising from non-compliance.

Examples:

* regulatory penalties;
* licensing issues;
* privacy violations.

### Third-Party Risk

Risks arising from suppliers and partners.

Examples:

* supplier failure;
* concentration risk;
* security weaknesses.

### Cybersecurity Risk

Risks arising from malicious or unauthorized activity.

Examples:

* ransomware;
* data breach;
* account compromise.

# 10. Risk Statement Construction

NovaTel introduces a standard risk statement format:

> **Because of [cause], [risk event] may occur, resulting in [impact].**

Example:

> Because of excessive dependency on a single cloud provider, a major cloud service outage may occur, resulting in prolonged disruption of enterprise digital services and financial loss.

This produces more useful risk statements than simply writing:

> "Cloud outage."

# 13. Risk Assessment Framework

NovaTel uses two primary dimensions:

### Likelihood

How likely is the risk event to occur?

### Impact

How severe would the consequences be?

The basic model is:

**Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

The organization uses a 1–5 scale.

---

# 14. Likelihood Scale

| Score | Description    |
| ----: | -------------- |
|     1 | Rare           |
|     2 | Unlikely       |
|     3 | Possible       |
|     4 | Likely         |
|     5 | Almost Certain |

The definitions are supported by quantitative ranges where appropriate.

For example:

* Rare: less than 5% probability;
* Unlikely: 5–20%;
* Possible: 21–50%;
* Likely: 51–80%;
* Almost Certain: above 80%.

These ranges are illustrative and should be calibrated to the organization's risk environment.

---

NovaTel assesses impact across several dimensions:

* financial;
* operational;
* regulatory;
* customer;
* reputational;
* cybersecurity;

The highest applicable material impact may be used for enterprise prioritization.

# 17. Inherent Risk

The first assessment is **inherent risk**.

Inherent risk represents exposure before considering existing controls.

Example:

```text id="uy6g8d"

      |
      v
Major Cloud Outage
      |
      v
High Business Impact
      |
      v
INHERENT RISK
```

The organization initially assesses the risk without assuming that controls will prevent the event.

---

# 18. Control Assessment

NovaTel then identifies existing controls.

For the cloud outage risk:

* multi-region architecture;
* disaster recovery;
* backup connectivity;
* service-level agreements;
* cloud monitoring;
* incident response;
* business continuity planning.

The effectiveness of these controls is then considered.

# 19. Residual Risk

After considering existing controls, NovaTel calculates residual risk.

```text id="5du4sk"

      |
      v

```

Example:

| ---------------- | -------: | -------: |

| Supplier failure |       16 |       10 |
| Data breach      |       20 |       12 |

Residual risk is what management ultimately needs to understand.

# 23. Risk Appetite

Risk assessment becomes meaningful only when compared with risk appetite.

NovaTel establishes:

> **"The organization has very low tolerance for risks that could cause prolonged interruption of critical customer services or significant compromise of regulated customer information."**

Therefore, a residual risk of 15 may exceed appetite.

# 25. Risk Treatment Decision

For risks exceeding appetite, management chooses a treatment strategy.

The four classic approaches are:

### Avoid

Stop the activity creating the risk.

### Reduce

Implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact.

### Transfer

Transfer part of the risk through mechanisms such as insurance or contractual arrangements.

### Accept

Explicitly accept the residual risk.

# 37. Risk Assessment Quality Review

The GRC team performs quality checks.

It asks:

* Is the risk statement clear?
* Is the correct owner assigned?
* Is likelihood supported by evidence?
* Is impact supported by business information?
* Are controls correctly considered?
* Is residual risk realistic?
* Does the rating align with risk appetite?

This prevents arbitrary scoring.

# 38. Common Assessment Errors

NovaTel identifies several common problems.

### Error 1 – Risk = Control Failure

A failed control is not automatically the enterprise risk.

### Error 2 – Risk = Threat

"Ransomware" alone is not a complete risk statement.

### Error 3 – Risk = Impact

"Revenue loss" describes an impact, not necessarily the risk.

### Error 4 – Inflated Ratings

Some managers rate every risk as critical.

### Error 5 – Ignoring Controls

Others underestimate residual risk by failing to assess control effectiveness.

---

This provides:

* cause;
* event;
* consequence.

* one enterprise risk taxonomy;

# 42. Key Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Start with Objectives

Risk identification should be connected to what the organization is trying to achieve.

## Lesson 2 – Use a Common Language

A common taxonomy and methodology make enterprise aggregation possible.

## Lesson 3 – Separate Inherent and Residual Risk

Management needs to understand both the original exposure and the remaining exposure after controls.

## Lesson 4 – Risk Appetite Drives Decisions

A risk score by itself does not determine what management should do.

## Lesson 5 – Risk Is Dynamic

Risk assessments must change when business, technology, threat, or regulatory conditions change.

## Lesson 6 – Look for Interdependencies

Individual risks may appear acceptable while their combined exposure is significant.

## Case Study Conclusion

The NovaTel case demonstrates that enterprise risk assessment is not simply a process of assigning numbers to risks.

A mature GRC organization connects **business objectives, risk identification, risk assessment, controls, risk appetite, treatment, monitoring, and executive decision-making**.

The central principle is:

> **Identify risk in the context of business objectives, assess it consistently, understand the effect of controls, compare residual exposure with risk appetite, and continuously reassess as conditions change.**

This approach transforms risk registers from static documentation into an **active management tool for enterprise decision-making**.

# 19.2 Risk Management Case Studies

## Part 2 – Risk Treatment, Mitigation and Acceptance

## 1. Case Study Overview

This case study examines how an organization moves from **risk assessment to risk treatment**, including mitigation planning, risk transfer, risk avoidance, and formal risk acceptance.

The objective is to demonstrate how a GRC professional determines **what should be done with identified risks**, who is accountable for the treatment decision, how treatment effectiveness is measured, and when residual risk may legitimately be accepted.

The organization in this case is fictional but reflects a realistic multinational enterprise.

---

# 2. Organization Profile

### Company

**GlobalNet Enterprise Services**

### Industry

Telecommunications, Cloud and Digital Services

### Employees

Approximately 10,500

### Annual Revenue

Approximately €3.2 billion

### Geographic Presence

* Spain
* France
* Germany
* Portugal
* Qatar
* Saudi Arabia

### Critical Services

* Mobile services
* Enterprise connectivity
* Cloud platforms
* Customer portals
* Digital payment services
* Managed technology services

---

# 3. The Business Problem

GlobalNet has completed an enterprise risk assessment.

The organization has identified **62 significant risks**.

However, management discovers that many risks are simply recorded in the risk register without effective treatment plans.

Examples include:

* ransomware;
* cloud outages;
* supplier failure;
* privacy breaches;
* regulatory non-compliance;
* critical infrastructure failure;
* privileged account compromise.

The Chief Risk Officer raises the issue with management:

> **"Identifying a risk is not enough. What are we actually going to do about it?"**

---

# 4. Treatment Objective

The organization establishes a formal objective:

> **Bring residual risk within approved risk appetite where reasonably practicable, while ensuring that treatment costs remain proportionate to the risk reduction achieved.**

This introduces an important principle:

**Risk treatment is a business decision, not simply a cybersecurity or compliance activity.**

---

# 5. Risk Treatment Options

GlobalNet adopts four primary treatment strategies.

```text id="w4xv7s"
                    RISK
                     |
       +-------------+-------------+
       |             |             |
       v             v             v
     Avoid         Reduce       Transfer
       |             |             |
       +-------------+-------------+
                     |
                     v
                   Accept
```

In practice, several strategies may be combined.

---

# 6. Strategy 1 – Risk Avoidance

Risk avoidance means changing or stopping an activity so that the associated risk is eliminated or substantially removed.

Example:

A business plans to deploy an unsupported legacy application.

The security assessment identifies unacceptable exposure.

Management decides:

> Do not deploy the application.

```text id="pm4x1m"
Planned Activity
       |
       v
Risk Assessment
       |
       v
Unacceptable Risk
       |
       v
Activity Cancelled
       |
       v
Risk Avoided
```

---

# 7. Strategy 2 – Risk Reduction

Risk reduction means implementing controls that reduce:

* likelihood;
* impact;
* or both.

Example:

A ransomware risk has an inherent score of:

**20**

The organization introduces:

* EDR;
* MFA;
* network segmentation;
* privileged access management;
* immutable backups;
* vulnerability management.

Residual risk falls to:

**10**

```text id="w6y3yt"
Inherent Risk = 20
       |
       v
Security Controls
       |
       v
Residual Risk = 10
```

---

# 8. Strategy 3 – Risk Transfer

Risk transfer shifts some financial or contractual consequences to another party.

Examples include:

* cyber insurance;
* contractual indemnification;
* supplier agreements;
* outsourcing arrangements.

However, transfer does **not necessarily eliminate the underlying risk**.

For example:

> Cyber insurance may transfer some financial consequences of a breach but does not eliminate the possibility of the breach itself.

This distinction is critical.

---

# 9. Strategy 4 – Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance means management consciously decides to retain the residual risk.

Acceptance should never mean:

> "We forgot about the risk."

A valid acceptance decision requires:

* documented risk;
* known residual exposure;
* identified owner;
* comparison with risk appetite;
* documented rationale;
* appropriate authority;
* defined review date.

---

# 11. Treatment Selection Criteria

Management evaluates:

### Risk Reduction

How much risk will the treatment remove?

### Cost

How much will implementation cost?

### Feasibility

Can the organization realistically implement it?

### Time

How quickly can the risk be reduced?

### Regulatory Requirement

Is the treatment mandatory?

### Business Impact

Will the treatment interfere with business objectives?

---

# 12. Cost-Benefit Consideration

GlobalNet does not automatically select the most expensive control.

The decision considers:

**Cost of Treatment**

versus

**Expected Risk Reduction**

For example:

| Treatment        |  Cost | Risk Reduction |
| ---------------- | ----: | -------------: |
| Basic control    | €100K |       Moderate |
| Enhanced control | €300K |           High |
| Advanced control |   €1M |      Very High |

The organization determines whether the additional reduction justifies the additional cost.

# 45. Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Treatment Must Be Risk-Based

Not every risk requires the same level of investment.

## Lesson 2 – Risk Reduction Must Be Demonstrated

Implementing a control does not automatically prove that risk has decreased.

## Lesson 3 – Acceptance Requires Authority

Employees should not casually accept significant enterprise risk.

## Lesson 4 – Transfer Does Not Mean Elimination

Insurance and contracts can transfer certain consequences but rarely eliminate the underlying risk.

## Lesson 5 – Temporary Acceptance Needs an Exit

Temporary risk acceptance should have an expiration date and defined exit conditions.

## Lesson 6 – Treatment Must Be Monitored

Risk treatment is a lifecycle, not a one-time project.

## Case Study Conclusion

The GlobalNet case demonstrates that **risk treatment is the point where risk management becomes a management decision**.

A mature GRC function does not simply identify risks and place them in a register. It determines:

* what should be avoided;
* what should be reduced;
* what can be transferred;
* what can be accepted;
* who has authority to make the decision;
* how treatment effectiveness will be demonstrated;
* when residual risk must be reassessed.

The central principle is:

> **Treat risk according to its exposure, appetite, business importance, regulatory obligations, and cost of mitigation—and formally accept only what the organization is prepared to own.**

This creates a disciplined transition from **risk identification → treatment → validation → acceptance or escalation → continuous monitoring**.

# 44. Risk Monitoring Maturity

EuroConnect defines four stages.

### Level 1 – Periodic

Risk reviewed annually or quarterly.

### Level 2 – Structured

KRIs and thresholds established.

### Level 3 – Integrated

Risk information connected to operational systems.

### Level 4 – Continuous

Automated monitoring and near-real-time alerts for selected risks.

The organization does not need continuous monitoring for every risk.

Monitoring intensity should correspond to risk characteristics.

# 47. Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1 – Risk Scores Are Snapshots

A risk score describes exposure at a point in time.

Trend information explains where the risk is going.

## Lesson 2 – KRIs Should Trigger Action

A KRI without a defined response is simply a metric.

## Lesson 3 – Monitoring Should Be Risk-Based

Critical and fast-moving risks deserve more frequent monitoring.

## Lesson 4 – Data Quality Matters

Poor data can produce misleading risk decisions.

## Lesson 5 – Reporting Should Be Decision-Oriented

Executives need to know what changed and what action is required.

## Lesson 6 – Automation Improves Timeliness

Automated monitoring can detect changes faster, but human judgment remains necessary.

## Case Study Conclusion

The EuroConnect case demonstrates how a mature GRC function evolves from **periodic risk assessment toward continuous risk intelligence**.

Effective risk monitoring combines:

* KRIs;
* risk trends;
* risk velocity;
* control performance;
* incidents;
* emerging risks;
* risk appetite;
* treatment effectiveness;
* executive reporting.

The central principle is:

> **Risk monitoring should detect meaningful changes early enough for management to make an informed decision before the risk becomes a business-impacting event.**

A mature organization therefore does not wait for the next quarterly risk review to discover that a critical risk has deteriorated. It establishes **defined indicators, thresholds, escalation mechanisms, and decision processes** that allow risk to be actively managed throughout the risk lifecycle.
