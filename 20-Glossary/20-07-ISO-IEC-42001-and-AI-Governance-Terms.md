# 20.7 ISO/IEC 42001 and AI Governance Terms

This section defines the core terminology associated with **ISO/IEC 42001:2023**, the **Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS)**, AI governance, AI risk management, AI lifecycle management, responsible AI, AI controls, and trustworthy AI.

> **Terminology note:** ISO/IEC 42001 establishes requirements for an **Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS)**. It is a management-system standard, similar in structure to ISO/IEC 27001, but specifically focused on the governance and management of AI systems and AI-related organizational activities.

# Key ISO/IEC 42001 Concepts

## 1. AIMS vs. AI Governance

These concepts are related but not identical.

### AIMS

The **Artificial Intelligence Management System** provides the structured management-system framework for governing AI.

It covers areas such as:

* organizational context;
* leadership;
* AI policy;
* AI objectives;
* AI risk management;
* AI impact assessment;
* operational controls;
* performance evaluation;
* internal audit;
* management review; and
* continual improvement.

### AI Governance

AI governance is broader as an organizational concept and includes:

* decision rights;
* accountability;
* ethics;
* risk appetite;
* policies;
* regulatory compliance;
* oversight;
* responsible AI;
* human oversight;
* technical governance; and
* organizational controls.

Therefore:

> **AIMS = Management-system structure for AI governance and management**

> **AI Governance = Broader organizational system for directing and controlling AI**

---

# 2. AI Risk vs. AI Impact

These concepts should be distinguished.

### AI Risk

Focuses on:

> **What could happen because of uncertainty surrounding the AI system, and how could it affect objectives?**

### AI Impact

Focuses on:

> **What effects could the AI system have on people, organizations, society, rights, safety, or the environment?**

For example:

**AI use case:** AI-based employee recruitment screening

**AI risk:** The model may systematically produce inaccurate candidate rankings.

**AI impact:** Qualified candidates from a particular demographic may be disproportionately excluded.

The risk-management process and impact-assessment process therefore complement each other.

---

# 3. AI Security vs. AI Safety vs. AI Ethics

### AI Security

Protects:

* models;
* datasets;
* infrastructure;
* APIs;
* credentials;
* AI applications;
* prompts;
* outputs;
* systems against attacks and unauthorized use.

### AI Safety

Focuses on preventing unacceptable harmful behavior or outcomes.

### AI Ethics

Addresses questions such as:

* fairness;
* human dignity;
* discrimination;
* accountability;
* societal effects;
* appropriate use;
* human autonomy.

A mature AI governance program should address **all three**.

---

# 4. Transparency vs. Explainability vs. Interpretability

These terms are often used interchangeably but have different governance implications.

### Transparency

> **Can relevant stakeholders obtain appropriate information about the AI system?**

### Explainability

> **Can the organization explain why an AI system produced a particular output or decision?**

### Interpretability

> **Can the behavior or internal logic of the model be meaningfully understood?**

For GRC purposes, transparency may include documentation, governance decisions, model information, limitations, and user disclosures—not merely technical explanations.

---

# 5. AI Governance Lifecycle

A practical AI governance lifecycle is:

**AI Idea / Use Case**
↓
**AI Intake & Classification**
↓
**Risk Assessment**
↓
**Impact Assessment**
↓
**Security & Privacy Assessment**
↓
**Governance Approval**
↓
**Development / Acquisition**
↓
**Testing & Validation**
↓
**Deployment**
↓
**Monitoring**
↓
**Incident Management**
↓
**Periodic Review**
↓
**Change Management**
↓
**Retirement / Decommissioning**

This lifecycle is particularly important for organizations operating multiple AI systems because governance cannot stop at the point of initial approval.

---

# 6. AI Inventory vs. AI Use Case Register

An **AI inventory** answers:

> **What AI systems do we have?**

An **AI use case register** answers:

> **How are we using AI and for what purposes?**

For example, one organization may have:

**AI System:** Microsoft Copilot

But multiple use cases:

* document drafting;
* coding assistance;
* customer support;
* data analysis;
* meeting summarization.

Each use case can therefore have different:

* risks;
* data;
* users;
* impacts;
* regulatory requirements;
* security controls; and
* approval requirements.

---

# 7. AI Provider vs. AI Deployer

This distinction is particularly important in AI governance.

### AI Provider

The organization that develops, supplies, or makes an AI system available.

### AI Deployer

The organization that uses the AI system under its authority or control.

An organization can potentially be a **provider, deployer, or both**, depending on what it does with the AI system.

---

# 8. ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27001 Relationship

The two standards can be integrated into a common GRC architecture.

| ISO/IEC 27001                          | ISO/IEC 42001                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Information Security Management System | Artificial Intelligence Management System |
| Information security risk              | AI-related risk                           |
| Information security objectives        | AI objectives                             |
| ISMS policy                            | AI policy                                 |
| Information security controls          | AI governance/management controls         |
| Statement of Applicability             | AIMS applicability documentation          |
| Security incidents                     | AI incidents                              |
| Security monitoring                    | AI system monitoring                      |
| Internal audit                         | AIMS internal audit                       |
| Management review                      | AIMS management review                    |
| Continual improvement                  | Continual improvement                     |

The relationship is particularly important because **AI systems introduce information-security risks**, while information-security controls alone do not address every AI governance issue.

For example, ISO/IEC 27001 may address:

* confidentiality;
* integrity;
* availability;
* access control;
* cryptography;
* vulnerability management;
* logging.

ISO/IEC 42001 additionally addresses AI governance concerns such as:

* AI objectives;
* AI risk management;
* AI impact assessment;
* AI lifecycle governance;
* responsible use;
* AI-specific controls;
* AI system monitoring;
* AI-related decision-making.

# 9. ISO/IEC 42001 and the AI Regulatory Landscape

For a modern GRC professional, ISO/IEC 42001 should not be studied in isolation.

A useful governance stack is:

**ISO/IEC 42001**
→ AI Management System

**ISO/IEC 23894**
→ AI Risk Management Guidance

**ISO/IEC 22989**
→ AI Concepts and Terminology

**NIST AI RMF**
→ AI Risk Management Framework
