# 19.9 Privacy and Data Protection Case Studies

## Part 1 – Building a Privacy Governance Program

Privacy and data protection have become core components of modern GRC. Organizations collect, process, transfer, analyze, and retain increasing amounts of personal data across applications, cloud platforms, third parties, artificial intelligence systems, and international operations.

A mature privacy program therefore cannot rely solely on a privacy policy or a legal department. It requires governance structures, accountability, risk assessment, technical controls, operational processes, monitoring, and evidence.

This case study demonstrates how an organization can establish a privacy governance program from the ground up.

---

## Case Study: GlobalConnect Telecom

**Organization:** GlobalConnect Telecom
**Industry:** Telecommunications and Digital Services
**Size:** 18,000 employees
**Geographic footprint:** European Union, United Kingdom, Middle East, and Asia
**GRC maturity:** Level 3 – Defined

GlobalConnect provides:

* Mobile telecommunications
* Broadband services
* Enterprise connectivity
* Cloud services
* Digital applications
* Customer self-service platforms
* Digital payment services
* Customer analytics
* AI-enabled customer support

The organization processes significant volumes of personal information, including:

* Customer identity information
* Contact information
* Account information
* Billing information
* Location-related information
* Device information
* Network usage information
* Customer-service records
* Employee information
* Supplier information
* Authentication information

Although GlobalConnect has privacy policies, its privacy activities have developed independently across different business units.

An internal assessment identifies several weaknesses.

---

## 1. The Privacy Governance Problem

The organization discovers that:

* Business units maintain separate data inventories.
* Privacy responsibilities are unclear.
* New applications are not consistently subjected to privacy assessments.
* Data-retention practices differ between departments.
* Third-party data processing is not centrally tracked.
* International data transfers are managed inconsistently.
* Customer requests are processed through multiple channels.
* Privacy incidents are not always escalated consistently.
* AI projects sometimes begin before privacy considerations are evaluated.
* Evidence of privacy compliance is distributed across multiple systems.

The Chief Risk Officer concludes that the organization has **privacy controls but not a sufficiently mature privacy governance system**.

The organization therefore launches a formal Privacy Governance Program.

---

# 2. Establishing Privacy Governance Objectives

The first step is defining what the privacy program is intended to achieve.

GlobalConnect establishes six objectives:

1. Ensure personal data is processed lawfully and transparently.
2. Establish clear accountability for privacy.
3. Identify and manage privacy risks.
4. Embed privacy into business and technology processes.
5. Protect individual data-subject rights.
6. Demonstrate compliance through evidence and assurance.

The program is therefore designed around both **compliance and risk management**.

The objective is not simply:

> "Have a privacy policy."

The objective is:

> **Ensure that privacy is governed throughout the entire information lifecycle.**

---

# 3. Establishing the Privacy Governance Structure

GlobalConnect establishes a three-level governance model.

### Board and Executive Management

Responsible for:

* Privacy strategy
* Significant privacy risks
* Risk appetite
* Major investments
* Regulatory exposure
* Executive accountability

### Privacy Governance Function

Responsible for:

* Privacy framework
* Privacy policies
* Regulatory interpretation
* Privacy risk management
* Privacy impact assessments
* Data-subject rights governance
* Privacy incident oversight
* Privacy reporting

### Business and Operational Functions

Responsible for:

* Implementing privacy requirements
* Maintaining data records
* Applying privacy controls
* Managing data according to approved requirements
* Providing evidence

This prevents privacy from becoming solely the responsibility of the privacy officer.

---

# 4. Appointment of Privacy Leadership

GlobalConnect appoints a **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** where required by applicable law and establishes a broader privacy governance team.

The privacy organization includes:

* Data Protection Officer
* Privacy Manager
* Privacy Analysts
* Legal Counsel
* Data Governance specialists
* Information Security representatives
* Records Management representatives

The DPO maintains appropriate independence in performing the organization's data-protection responsibilities.

However, privacy governance is treated as an enterprise capability rather than as a one-person function.

---

# 5. Defining Privacy Roles and Responsibilities

GlobalConnect creates a privacy RACI model.

| Activity              | Privacy | Legal | Security | Business | IT |
| --------------------- | ------- | ----- | -------- | -------- | -- |
| Privacy policy        | A/R     | C     | C        | I        | I  |
| Data inventory        | A       | C     | C        | R        | R  |
| Privacy assessment    | A/R     | C     | C        | R        | C  |
| Security controls     | C       | I     | A/R      | C        | R  |
| Data retention        | A       | C     | C        | R        | R  |
| Data-subject requests | A/R     | C     | C        | R        | C  |
| Privacy incidents     | A       | C     | R        | R        | R  |
| Third-party privacy   | A       | C     | C        | R        | C  |
| Privacy training      | A       | C     | C        | R        | I  |

**A = Accountable**
**R = Responsible**
**C = Consulted**
**I = Informed**

The RACI model removes ambiguity regarding who must perform and approve privacy activities.

---

# 6. Creating the Privacy Policy Framework

The organization develops a hierarchy of privacy documentation.

### Level 1 – Privacy Policy

Establishes executive-level principles and requirements.

### Level 2 – Privacy Standards

Defines mandatory organizational requirements.

Examples:

* Data classification standard
* Data retention standard
* Privacy assessment standard
* Third-party privacy standard

### Level 3 – Procedures

Defines how operational teams execute privacy requirements.

Examples:

* Data-subject request procedure
* Privacy incident procedure
* Data deletion procedure
* Privacy assessment procedure

### Level 4 – Operational Records

Provides evidence that requirements were executed.

Examples:

* Assessment records
* Consent records
* Processing records
* Training records
* Incident records

This structure makes the privacy framework easier to govern and audit.

---

# 7. Establishing a Data Inventory

One of the most important activities is determining:

> **What personal data does the organization actually process?**

GlobalConnect creates an enterprise data inventory covering:

* Data category
* Data subject
* Purpose
* Processing activity
* System
* Business owner
* Data owner
* Processor
* Geographic location
* Retention period
* Legal basis
* Transfer mechanism
* Security classification

For example:

| Data              | Purpose            | System           | Owner               | Retention      |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------- | -------------- |
| Customer identity | Account management | CRM              | Customer Operations | Defined period |
| Billing data      | Billing            | Billing Platform | Finance             | Defined period |
| Location data     | Network services   | Network Platform | Network Operations  | Defined period |
| Support records   | Customer service   | CRM              | Customer Care       | Defined period |

The inventory becomes a foundational component of the privacy program.

---

# 8. Establishing Records of Processing Activities

For applicable regulatory environments, GlobalConnect establishes a formal **Record of Processing Activities (ROPA)**.

Each processing activity documents information such as:

* Purpose of processing
* Categories of personal data
* Categories of data subjects
* Recipients
* International transfers
* Retention
* Security measures
* Responsible organizational unit

The ROPA provides management with visibility into how personal information moves through the organization.

It also becomes an important source for privacy assessments and regulatory inquiries.

---

# 9. Defining Data Ownership

Privacy governance requires clear ownership.

GlobalConnect distinguishes between:

### Business Owner

Responsible for the business purpose and use of the data.

### Data Owner

Responsible for governance and classification of the information.

### System Owner

Responsible for the application or technology environment.

### Privacy Function

Provides privacy governance and oversight.

### Security Function

Protects information against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, and destruction.

This separation is important because **privacy and security are related but not identical disciplines**.

A system can be highly secure while still processing personal information for an inappropriate purpose.

---

# 10. Establishing Privacy by Design

GlobalConnect incorporates privacy into the project lifecycle.

Every significant new system, product, or service must answer privacy questions during project initiation.

The organization asks:

* What personal data will be collected?
* Why is it needed?
* Is all of it necessary?
* Who will access it?
* How long will it be retained?
* Will it be shared?
* Will third parties process it?
* Will data leave the country or region?
* Are individuals adequately informed?
* Could the processing create significant privacy risks?

Privacy is therefore considered **before implementation**, rather than after deployment.

---

# 11. Privacy Impact Assessments

For processing activities that may create significant privacy risks, GlobalConnect establishes a formal **Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA/DPIA) process**, as applicable.

The assessment evaluates:

**Processing → Necessity → Proportionality → Risk → Controls → Residual Risk**

For example, GlobalConnect proposes a new AI customer-service platform.

The platform will process:

* Customer conversations
* Customer account information
* Support history
* Potentially sensitive information contained in conversations

The privacy team identifies risks related to:

* Excessive data collection
* Secondary use
* Retention
* Unauthorized access
* Model training
* Third-party processing
* International transfers
* Automated decision-making

The project cannot proceed to production until the required privacy risks are addressed or formally accepted by the appropriate authority.

---

# 12. Privacy Risk Assessment

GlobalConnect integrates privacy risk with its enterprise GRC methodology.

A privacy risk may be represented as:

**Threat + Personal Data + Vulnerability + Impact**

Examples include:

* Unauthorized disclosure of customer information
* Excessive employee access
* Inappropriate data retention
* Unapproved secondary use
* Third-party data leakage
* Inaccurate customer information
* Failure to honor deletion requests

Risk impact considers:

* Individual harm
* Regulatory exposure
* Financial impact
* Legal consequences
* Operational disruption
* Reputation
* Customer trust

Privacy risks are then recorded in the enterprise risk register where appropriate.

---

# 13. Data Retention and Deletion Governance

GlobalConnect identifies excessive retention as a significant privacy risk.

The organization establishes a retention framework.

Each data category must have:

* Business purpose
* Legal or regulatory requirement
* Retention period
* Data owner
* Disposal method
* Exception process

The organization implements automated deletion where practical.

For example:

**Customer service record**

→ Retention period reached

→ Eligibility verification

→ Deletion/anonymization

→ System confirmation

→ Evidence recorded

This reduces the risk of retaining personal information indefinitely.

---

# 14. Data-Subject Rights Management

GlobalConnect establishes a centralized process for handling individual privacy requests.

Requests may include applicable rights such as:

* Access
* Correction
* Deletion
* Restriction
* Objection
* Portability

The process includes:

**Request → Identity Verification → Classification → Search → Review → Response → Evidence**

The organization establishes service-level targets and escalation mechanisms.

A centralized workflow also reduces the risk of different business units responding inconsistently.

---

# 15. Third-Party Privacy Governance

GlobalConnect works with hundreds of suppliers and technology providers.

The privacy program therefore integrates with third-party risk management.

Before onboarding a significant processor, the organization evaluates:

* Data processed
* Processing purpose
* Security controls
* Privacy controls
* Geographic location
* Subprocessors
* International transfers
* Retention
* Incident notification
* Audit rights
* Contractual requirements

Privacy requirements are incorporated into supplier agreements where appropriate.

This ensures that outsourcing a processing activity does not eliminate the organization's governance responsibilities.

---

# 16. Privacy Incident Management

GlobalConnect integrates privacy incidents with its cybersecurity incident-management process.

Examples include:

* Misaddressed email containing personal data
* Lost device containing personal information
* Unauthorized database access
* Excessive employee access
* Accidental publication of customer information
* Third-party data exposure

The incident process includes:

**Detection → Classification → Containment → Assessment → Notification Decision → Remediation → Lessons Learned**

The privacy team works with Security, Legal, Compliance, and the affected business unit.

The organization maintains evidence supporting its incident assessment and notification decisions.

---

# 17. Privacy Awareness and Training

GlobalConnect recognizes that many privacy failures originate from employee behavior.

Training is therefore risk-based.

### General Employees

Receive training on:

* Personal data
* Privacy responsibilities
* Phishing and social engineering
* Data handling
* Reporting incidents

### Customer-Service Employees

Receive additional training on:

* Identity verification
* Data-subject requests
* Customer information
* Unauthorized disclosure

### Developers

Receive training on:

* Privacy by design
* Data minimization
* Secure data handling
* Logging
* Retention

### Managers and Data Owners

Receive training on:

* Privacy risk
* Accountability
* Data governance
* Regulatory obligations

This creates a privacy culture rather than simply satisfying an annual training requirement.

# 19. Privacy Governance Dashboard

The executive dashboard combines:

**Data → Risk → Compliance → Controls → Incidents → Remediation**

A Chief Risk Officer should be able to determine:

* Where personal data is processed
* Which processing activities are high risk
* Which PIAs remain incomplete
* Which suppliers create privacy exposure
* Whether privacy requests are being handled on time
* Whether privacy incidents are increasing
* Which regulatory requirements require attention
* Which remediation actions are overdue

The dashboard transforms privacy from a legal topic into a measurable GRC capability.

---

# 20. Internal Audit and Independent Assurance

Internal Audit periodically assesses whether the privacy governance framework is operating effectively.

Audit areas may include:

1. Privacy governance
2. Data inventory
3. ROPA completeness
4. Privacy assessments
5. Data-subject rights
6. Retention
7. Third-party privacy
8. Incident management
9. Training
10. Evidence management

Internal Audit should maintain independence from operational privacy activities.

Its role is to provide assurance to management and the board.

---

# 21. Lessons Learned

The GlobalConnect case demonstrates that mature privacy governance requires significantly more than a privacy policy.

A strong program establishes:

**Governance**

→ Who is accountable?

**Data visibility**

→ What personal data is processed?

**Purpose**

→ Why is it processed?

**Risk**

→ What could harm individuals or the organization?

**Controls**

→ How are those risks managed?

**Rights**

→ How are individual requests handled?

**Third parties**

→ Who else processes the data?

**Incidents**

→ What happens when privacy fails?

**Assurance**

→ Can the organization demonstrate that controls operate effectively?

The resulting governance chain is:

**Personal Data → Processing Activity → Purpose → Risk → Control → Owner → Evidence → Assurance**

# 22. Practical GRC Exercise

Assume you have been appointed **Privacy GRC Manager** for a telecommunications organization that operates across five countries.

The organization has:

* 10,000 employees
* 20 million customers
* 300 major applications
* 500 third parties
* Multiple cloud platforms
* Centralized customer analytics
* AI-powered customer-service capabilities

Management asks you to establish the privacy governance program within 12 months.

Develop a program covering:

1. Privacy governance structure
2. Roles and responsibilities
3. Privacy policy framework
4. Data inventory
5. ROPA
6. Data ownership
7. Privacy risk assessment
8. Privacy impact assessment
9. Data retention
10. Data-subject rights
11. Third-party privacy
12. International data transfers
13. Privacy incident management
14. Privacy training
15. Privacy metrics
16. GRC tooling
17. Internal audit
18. Executive reporting

Your final objective is to establish a sustainable privacy governance capability rather than simply producing documentation.

The target operating model should allow the organization to answer five fundamental questions:

> **What personal data do we process?**

> **Why do we process it?**

> **What risks does the processing create?**

> **What controls manage those risks?**

> **Can we demonstrate that those controls are working?**
